# Australia — Biofuel Regulation Report
**As of 2026-07-16**

## 1. Instrument Type & Current-Year Headline Obligation

**Mandate type:** NO FEDERAL BIOFUEL BLENDING MANDATE. Biofuel regulation in Australia is **sub-national only**, with two states (New South Wales and Queensland) operating independent volumetric mandates. Federal government is in consultation phase for a demand-side measure to support low-carbon liquid fuels (LCFS) production.

**Current operative state mandates (2026):**

### New South Wales (NSW)
- **Ethanol mandate:** 6% of total petrol volume sold by fuel retailers — **CONFIRMED** (NSW Biofuels Act 2007; Biofuels Regulation 2022).
- **Biodiesel mandate:** 2% of total diesel volume sold by fuel retailers — **CONFIRMED** (ibid.).
- **Legal basis:** *Biofuels Act 2007* and *Biofuels Regulation 2022* (SL 2022-0462).
- **Effective since:** Early 2000s (Act 2007); Regulation 2022 is current operative version.
- **Implementation:** Volumetric fuel retailer mandate; exemptions available on grounds of infrastructure upgrade, supply security, market uptake — **CONFIRMED** (NSW Fair Trading Biofuels Exemptions Framework).

### Queensland (QLD)
- **Ethanol mandate (current operative):** 4% of total volume of petrol sales by fuel retailers (marketed as ~E4 blends or E10 penetration goal) — **CONFIRMED** (Queensland Business website; Liquid Fuel Supply Act 1984).
- **Biodiesel mandate (current operative):** 0.5% of total diesel volume sold by fuel wholesalers — **CONFIRMED** (ibid.).
- **Legal basis:** *Liquid Fuel Supply Act 1984* (amended by *Liquid Fuel Supply (Ethanol and Other Biofuels Mandate) Amendment Act 2015*).
- **Effective since:** Commenced **1 January 2017** (ethanol target ramped to 4% by 1 July 2018); biodiesel 0.5% effective from same date — **CONFIRMED** (Department of Energy/OECD source 2017; Business Queensland website).
- **Amendment Bill 2026 (not yet law):** Introduced by Shane Knuth MP **13 May 2026**; proposes increase to E9 (minimum 9% ethanol in E10 blends) and E2 (minimum 2% biodiesel in diesel). Committee review due **16 November 2026**. Commencement one year after assent if passed — **PROPOSED** (Queensland Parliament Bill 2026-017; Explanatory Notes 2026-05-13).

### Federal (Australia-Wide)
- **No legislated mandate.** Government supporting production via Cleaner Fuels Program (grants) and Low Carbon Liquid Fuels (LCFS) demand measure in consultation.

## 2. Legislated Trajectory / 2030 Target

### State Mandates
- **NSW:** 6% ethanol and 2% biodiesel — no increase legislated beyond current levels — **CONFIRMED**.
- **Queensland (current):** 4% ethanol and 0.5% biodiesel. **Amendment Bill 2026 (if passed)** would increase to E9 and E2, effective one year post-assent (earliest commencement: late 2027 if passed in 2026 parliamentary session) — **PROPOSED** (QLD Bill 2026-017).

### Federal LCFS Consultation (2025–2026)
- **2025-26 Budget announcement:** $1.1 billion in production incentives (grant-like) for domestic low-carbon liquid fuel (LCFL) producers; additional $250 million in prior grants announced 2024-25 budget.
- **Timeline:** Government to finalize program design in **early 2026**; applications open **mid-2026** (June/July anticipated) — **CONFIRMED** (Department of Infrastructure media release; Budget 2026-27 Fuel Supply and Security paper).
- **Written submissions deadline:** **19 December 2025** (past; consultation closed) — **CONFIRMED** (DIT consultation paper).
- **No 2030 mandated blend level set yet** — **GAP** (consultation paper canvassed options; final design not published as of 2026-07-16).

### Future Direction
- Federal government signaling intent to introduce a **demand-side measure** (mandatory blend or quota) **parallel to or following LCFS production support scheme**, but timeline and level not legislated — **PROPOSED** (Clayton Utz, DIT consultation paper).
- No explicit 2030 target; alignment with net-zero emission goals (2050 net-zero commitment) likely to drive future trajectory — **PRIMARY-ONLY**.

## 3. Advanced-Biofuel / Sub-Quotas

### No Advanced-Biofuel Sub-Quota under State Mandates
- NSW 6% ethanol and 2% biodiesel are volumetric targets without distinction between conventional and advanced biofuels — **CONFIRMED**.
- Queensland 4% ethanol and 0.5% biodiesel likewise undifferentiated — **CONFIRMED**.
- Neither state's legislation caps crop-based feedstocks or mandates RFNBO/advanced biofuel percentages — **CONFIRMED**.

### Federal LCFS Program
- Cleaner Fuels Program (production incentive) explicitly supports renewable diesel, SAF, and **e-fuels**, positioning these as low-carbon options eligible for grants — **CONFIRMED** (DIT program description; BDO article 2026).
- A demand measure (if legislated) would likely include advanced fuels but details remain in consultation — **PROPOSED**.

## 4. Fuel-Tax Treatment of Biofuels

### Excise Duty Rates (as of February 2026, indexed twice yearly)

| Fuel Type | Duty Rate | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| **Unleaded petrol (incl. E10)** | A$0.526/L | General rate; indexed Feb/Aug; temporary relief April–June 2026 reduced to A$0.206/L |
| **Diesel** | A$0.526/L | General rate; indexed Feb/Aug; temporary relief April–June 2026 reduced to A$0.206/L |
| **Ethanol (fuel-grade, internal combustion)** | A$0.172/L | Reduced rate (not full petrol duty); indexed; temporary relief April–June 2026 reduced to A$0.068/L |
| **Biodiesel** | A$0.175/L | Reduced rate; indexed; temporary relief April–June 2026 reduced to A$0.069/L |

**Source:** Australian Taxation Office (ATO) Excise Duty Rates and Biofuels pages — **CONFIRMED** (ATO official rates accessed 2026-07-16).

### Tax Relief / Exemptions
- **Biodiesel excise rate phasing:** Full rates apply at specified legislated dates; rates can be "reduced or removed with grants" — **PRIMARY-ONLY** (ATO guidance; specific grant program details not located).
- **No blanket excise exemption for biofuel blends** (E10, E2) — blends taxed under component rates (petrol/ethanol component split for E10, for example) — **CONFIRMED** (ATO).
- **July 2026 temporary relief:** 30.4% reduction applied for one month (e.g., petrol/diesel reduced 16 cent/L) — **CONFIRMED** (ATO announcement).

### State-Level Excise
- States do not levy separate fuel excise (federal monopoly post-1998) — **CONFIRMED**.

### Impact on Blending Economics
- Ethanol duty (A$0.172/L) is 67% below petrol duty (A$0.526/L), incentivizing E10 blending — **CONFIRMED** (ATO rates).
- Biodiesel duty (A$0.175/L) similar discount to ethanol, supporting B2 adoption in NSW and QLD — **CONFIRMED**.

## 5. Sustainability / Certification Regime

### Emerging Federal Framework: Guarantee of Origin (GO) Scheme

**Guarantee of Origin Scheme (commenced Nov 2025):**
- **Scope:** Voluntary framework for emissions accounting and certification of renewable electricity (current); **expansion to low-carbon liquid fuels (LCLFs) planned for 2026** — **CONFIRMED** (DCCEEW; Clayton Utz; Infrastructure Australia).
- **Legal basis:** *Future Made in Australia (Guarantee of Origin) Act 2024* — **CONFIRMED** (DCCEEW Guarantee of Origin Scheme page).
- **LCFL Coverage (2026 onward):** Renewable diesel, SAF, synthetic fuels (e-fuels), and bioethanol — **PROPOSED/IN DEVELOPMENT** (public consultation planned early 2026 for formal amendments) — **CONFIRMED** (Infrastructure Australia media release; DIT consultation paper).

**How GO works for LCLFs:**
- Digital Product GO (PGO) certificates issued; track origin and lifecycle carbon intensity — **CONFIRMED** (NoviqTech explanation).
- Producers obtain certification; certificates tradeable; enables verifiable environmental claims and access to low-emissions markets — **CONFIRMED** (DCCEEW).

### GreenPower Certification Initiative
- **GreenPower** (Australian not-for-profit) developing two new schemes:
  1. **Low Carbon Liquid Fuels Certification** — covering SAF, renewable diesel, biodiesel, renewable LPG, bioLPG — **PRIMARY-ONLY** (GreenPower announcement).
  2. **Biogenic CO2 Certification** — under development in parallel — **UNCORROBORATED** (title mentioned, no further details found).

### Sustainability Criteria (Under Consultation)
Federal consultation paper on GO expansion canvassed broader sustainability factors beyond emissions:
- **Land-use change** impact.
- **Biodiversity** effects.
- **Water** usage.
- **Food security** trade-offs.
- **Labour standards** and human rights.

No final criteria set or legislated as of 2026-07-16 — **PROPOSED** (DIT/Infrastructure consultation paper Nov 2025; outcomes TBD).

### State-Level Sustainability Requirements
- **NSW:** Biofuels Act 2007 and Regulation 2022 do not specify sustainability or feedstock-origin certification requirements; focus is on volumetric blending compliance — **CONFIRMED** (NSW legislation text).
- **Queensland:** Liquid Fuel Supply Act 1984 and amendments likewise do not mandate sustainability criteria or scheme affiliation — **CONFIRMED** (QLD legislation).
- **No restriction on crop-based (e.g., corn ethanol, soy biodiesel) or food-competitive feedstocks at state level** — **CONFIRMED**.

### Status of Certification Regime
- **Current (2026-07-16):** NSW and Queensland mandate volumes but do NOT require sustainability certification or independent audit.
- **Emerging (2026–2027):** GO scheme LCFL expansion and GreenPower schemes in development; adoption likely voluntary initially — **PROPOSED**.

## 6. Status & Data Flags

### Operative Status
- **NSW mandate:** **IN FORCE** (6% ethanol, 2% biodiesel); no sunset or review date found — **CONFIRMED** (Regulation 2022 appears indefinite).
- **Queensland mandate:** **IN FORCE** (4% ethanol, 0.5% biodiesel) until amendment bill passed; Amendment Bill 2026 in committee review (due Nov 2026) — **PROPOSED**.
- **Federal LCFS:** **CONSULTATION PHASE** (early 2026); program design finalization, applications mid-2026 — **IN PROGRESS** (DIT).

### Unresolved / Flagged Items

1. **Queensland Amendment Bill 2026 (E9 / E2) — Legislative Status Uncertain:**
   - Introduced 13 May 2026; committee review due 16 November 2026.
   - No guarantee of passage; KAP (Katter's Australian Party) sponsor; support from agriculture and manufacturing groups, but fuel retail/refining industry feedback unknown.
   - If passed, commencement one year post-assent (potentially late 2027).
   - **FLAG** (PROPOSED; outcome and timeline uncertain).

2. **Federal Demand Measure Design & Timeline Unresolved:**
   - Government signaled intent to introduce demand-side measure supporting/requiring low-carbon fuel uptake, but no bill drafted or tabled.
   - Consultation closed 19 December 2025; outcomes (blend levels, effective date, phase-in) not published.
   - Could complement or supersede/override state mandates — **FLAG** (PROPOSED; details GAP).

3. **NSW Mandate Enforcement & Current Compliance:**
   - Regulation 2022 permits exemptions; no public data on exemption rates or actual blending volumes achieved vs. mandated 6% / 2%.
   - Assumed compliance but not verified — **UNCORROBORATED**.

4. **Sustainability Criteria for Federal LCFS:**
   - Consultation paper canvassed land-use change, biodiversity, water, food security, labour; final criteria not legislated.
   - Possible inconsistency with voluntary GO scheme (if emissions-only) vs. state mandates (no requirement) — **FLAG** (CONFLICT potential; not yet realized).

5. **SAF Inclusion in Federal LCFS:**
   - LCFS consultation and Cleaner Fuels grants explicitly include SAF, but no airline mandate or blending requirement stated (unlike jet fuel-producing countries).
   - **GAP** (no SAF percentage requirement identified for domestic consumption).

6. **Biodiesel Feedstock Cap:**
   - NSW and QLD allow any biodiesel source (no palm, soy, or waste-oil restriction).
   - Concern raised by environmental groups; no government response or regulation change published — **UNCORROBORATED** (issues noted in NGO submissions, no regulatory response).

## 8. Five-Line Summary

**Australia operates NO FEDERAL biofuel mandate.** **New South Wales mandates 6% ethanol and 2% biodiesel** (Biofuels Act 2007 / Regulation 2022, in force indefinitely), and **Queensland mandates 4% ethanol and 0.5% biodiesel** (Liquid Fuel Supply Act 1984 amendments, in place since 2017–2018), with an **Amendment Bill tabled May 2026 proposing increases to 9% ethanol and 2% biodiesel** (committee review due November 2026, commencement one year post-assent if passed). **Federal government is in active consultation (closed 19 Dec 2025) on a Low Carbon Liquid Fuels production incentive scheme (applications mid-2026) and a future demand-side measure**, but no legislated blend mandate or 2030 target yet set. **Excise duty rates favor biofuels: ethanol A$0.172/L and biodiesel A$0.175/L vs. petrol/diesel A$0.526/L** (both indexed twice yearly). **Sustainability certification is currently absent from state mandates; a federal Guarantee of Origin scheme (commenced November 2025) will expand to cover LCLFs in 2026** via voluntary PGO certificates tracking lifecycle emissions and sustainability criteria (land-use change, biodiversity, water, labour) still under consultation.
