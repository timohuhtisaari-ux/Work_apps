## 1. Federal baseline (applies to all 50 states + DC)

Two federal instruments form the floor beneath any state program.

### 1a. Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS2) — EPA

- **Instrument type:** Nationwide **renewable-volume mandate**, not a
  carbon-intensity standard. EPA sets annual **Renewable Volume Obligations
  (RVOs)** in four nested categories (total renewable, advanced biofuel,
  biomass-based diesel, cellulosic biofuel). Obligated parties (refiners,
  importers of gasoline/diesel) demonstrate compliance by retiring **RINs**
  (Renewable Identification Numbers) — the tradable compliance credits generated
  when qualifying renewable fuel is produced/imported. Statutory basis: Clean Air
  Act §211(o) as amended by EISA 2007; program known as RFS2.
- **Set-rule status:** The **"Set 2" final rule for 2026–2027** was signed
  27 Mar 2026, published in the Federal Register 1 Apr 2026, with a stated
  effective date of 15 Jun 2026. It is the first rule to set volumes two years
  out and to reallocate ~70% of 2023–2025 small-refinery exemptions (SREs).
- **2026 headline RVOs (billion RINs, from EPA final table):**

  | RFS category | 2026 | 2027 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Total renewable fuel | 26.81 | 27.02 |
  | Advanced biofuel | 11.10 | 11.32 |
  | Biomass-based diesel | 9.07 | 9.20 |
  | Cellulosic biofuel | 1.36 | 1.43 |

  Totals include SRE reallocation of 0.99 bn RINs (2026) / 1.04 bn (2027); the
  2026 total net of reallocation is ~25.82 bn RINs. Implied conventional
  (corn-ethanol-type) volume held at 15 bn gal. The rule also **removes renewable
  electricity (eRINs)** as a qualifying pathway and (from 2028) halves the RIN
  value of foreign fuels/feedstocks. `CONFIRMED` (EPA final rule table + Federal
  Register + multiple law-firm/industry summaries).

### 1b. Clean Fuel Production Credit — IRC §45Z (IRA §13704)

- **Instrument type:** Federal **technology-neutral production tax credit** for
  domestically produced clean transportation fuel, scaled by the fuel's lifecycle
  carbon intensity (a tax mechanism, not a mandate — cross-reference to the
  fuel-tax workstream; included here only as part of the federal floor).
- **Status/period:** Applies to clean transportation fuel produced after
  31 Dec 2024. Original sunset 31 Dec 2027; **extended through 31 Dec 2029** by
  the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R.1 / OBBBA, enacted 4 Jul 2025), which also
  restricted eligible feedstocks to US/Canada/Mexico origin (from 2026) and
  modified lifecycle-emissions accounting. IRS/Treasury issued initial guidance
  (Notice 2025-11, Jan 2025) and **proposed regulations 3 Feb 2026** (comment
  period to 5 Apr 2026). Replaces the prior blenders'/biodiesel credits.
  `CONFIRMED` (IRS 45Z page + Federal Register + Congress.gov CRS + OBBBA
  coverage).

## 2. State low-carbon / clean-fuel standards (carbon-intensity programs)

**Four states** operate or have formally adopted an LCFS-type program (CA, OR, WA
in force; NM adopted and entering force in 2026). All use a **carbon-intensity
(CI) reduction** benchmark with tradable **credits/deficits**, distinct from the
federal RFS volume model.

| State | Program (agency) | Headline CI-reduction target & timeline | Baseline yr | Status | Confidence | Key sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **California** | Low Carbon Fuel Standard — LCFS (CARB) | **30% by 2030; 90% by 2045**; annual step-down schedule (2025 benchmark 22.75%; 2026 ≈24.20% for gasoline) | 2010 | **In force**; 2024 amendments effective 1 Jul 2025 (OAL approved 27 Jun 2025) | `CONFIRMED` (headline); 2026 24.20% figure `PRIMARY-ONLY`/aggregator | CARB news + LCFS Basics; Sidley; dieselNet; globalELR; TRICORD |
| **Oregon** | Clean Fuels Program — CFP (DEQ) | **10% by 2025; 20% by 2030; 37% by 2035** | 2015 | **In force** (since 2016; electricity/expansion rule 2022) | `CONFIRMED` | Oregon DEQ CFP overview; USDA/AFDC; OEC |
| **Washington** | Clean Fuel Standard — CFS (Dept. of Ecology) | Originally **20% by 2038** (HB 1091, 2021); **raised to 45% by 2038 (option to 55%)** by HB 1409, signed 17 May 2025 | 2017 | **In force** (compliance from 1 Jan 2023); stronger target adopted 2025 | `CONFIRMED` | WA Ecology CFS page; HB 1409; Stillwater; IETA |
| **New Mexico** | Clean Transportation Fuel Program / Standard — CTFP/CTFS (NMED + Environmental Improvement Board) | **20% by 2030; 30% by 2040** | 2018 | **Adopted** — HB 41 enacted 5 Mar 2024; rule 20.2.92 NMAC adopted by EIB 22 Jan 2026; effective 1 Apr 2026 (credit generation begins) | `CONFIRMED` | NMED CTFP page; AFDC/DOE (law 13414); Gov. of NM release; Clean Fuels Alliance |

**Notes on targets:**
- California baseline is 2010; Oregon 2015; Washington 2017; New Mexico 2018 —
  the percentage targets are **not directly comparable across states** because
  baselines and target years differ.
- Washington: the pre-2025 statute set 20% by 2038; HB 1409 (2025) is the
  operative strengthened target (45% by 2038, up to 55% if conditions met). Both
  are reported to avoid overstating the original figure.
- New Mexico is the **fourth** state to adopt an LCFS-type program; it is adopted
  and enters operation 1 Apr 2026 (i.e., transitioning from "adopted" to "in
  force" within the current year).

## 3. States with NO state clean-fuel standard (federal RFS only) — for the map

Only **California, Oregon, Washington, and New Mexico** have an adopted state
LCFS-type program. **All remaining 46 states plus the District of Columbia** have
**no state carbon-intensity fuel standard** and sit on the **federal RFS2 (RIN)**
baseline only:

Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida,
Georgia, Hawaii*, Idaho, Illinois*, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana,
Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts*, Michigan*, Minnesota*, Mississippi, Missouri,
Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York*, North Carolina,
North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South
Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin,
Wyoming, and Washington, D.C.

\* = has a **proposed/under-development** LCFS-type effort (Section 2) but **no
adopted program** — map as "RFS-only, LCFS proposed" if the map distinguishes.

Note: several RFS-only states instead use **producer tax credits** (e.g. Iowa,
Arkansas) rather than a CI standard — a tax instrument, out of this workstream's
scope; flagged only so they are not mis-mapped as having an LCFS.

## 5-line summary

1. **Federal floor (all states):** RFS2 volume mandate via RINs — final 2026 RVO
   total ~26.81 bn RINs (net ~25.82 bn after 0.99 bn SRE reallocation), advanced
   11.10, BBD 9.07, cellulosic 1.36; "Set 2" rule signed 27 Mar 2026 (`CONFIRMED`).
   Plus IRC §45Z Clean Fuel Production Credit, extended through 2029 by OBBBA.
2. **California LCFS (CARB, in force):** 30% CI reduction by 2030, 90% by 2045
   (2010 baseline); 2024 amendments effective 1 Jul 2025; 2026 benchmark ≈24.20%.
3. **Oregon CFP (DEQ, in force):** 20% by 2030, 37% by 2035 (2015 baseline);
   **Washington CFS (Ecology, in force):** raised to 45% by 2038 via HB 1409 (2025).
4. **New Mexico CTFP (NMED/EIB, adopted):** 20% by 2030, 30% by 2040 (2018
   baseline); rule adopted 22 Jan 2026, operational 1 Apr 2026 — 4th state.
5. **Everyone else = RFS-only:** the other 46 states + DC have no CI standard;
   MA/NY/MN/IL/MI (and possibly HI) are `PROPOSED` only. Flags: HI status
   `UNCORROBORATED`; CARB pages 403-blocked (worked around via search + secondaries);
   CA 2026 24.20% benchmark `PRIMARY-ONLY`.
