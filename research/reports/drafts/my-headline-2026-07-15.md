# Malaysia — Non-EU Biofuel Regulation Headline (2026-07-15)

## 1. Instrument type & 2026 headline obligation

**Type:** Blending mandate (volume %)  
**2026 Level:** B15 biodiesel (15% palm oil FAME) for transport; B7 biodiesel (7% palm oil FAME) for industrial sector; phased rollout to higher blends under way.

Malaysia operates a **mandatory blending regime** administered by the Ministry of Plantation and Commodities (KPK, formerly known as the Ministry of Plantation Industries and Commodities). As of June 1, 2026, Malaysia began nationwide implementation of **B15 biodiesel for transport**, upgrading from B10 (in effect since February 2019). **B7 remains the mandate for the industrial sector** (since July 2019).

**Obligated parties:** Fuel suppliers, petroleum distributors, and refineries operating in Malaysia.

## 2. Trajectory and 2030 target

| Year | Transport Mandate | Industrial Mandate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | B10 (Feb 2019 onwards) | B7 (July 2019 onwards) | Initial implementation |
| 2026 | B15 (June 1, 2026) | B7 (unchanged) | Phased: B12 interim, full B15 by end-2026 |
| 2027–2028 | B20 (proposed, phased) | B7 (under review) | Under 13th Malaysia Plan (13MP) |
| 2030 | B30 (announced target) | — | Commercial and public transport; advanced-fuel consideration |

**Strategic goal:** Malaysia aims to increase palm oil utilization for domestic energy security and reduce fossil fuel imports. KPK targets ~300,000 tonnes incremental annual palm oil absorption from B15 rollout. B30 for heavy commercial transport and public buses targeted for 2030; SAF refinery in Johor expected operational by 2028.

## 3. Advanced/sub-quotas and feedstock caps

**No formal advanced biofuel sub-quota structure** (analogous to EU Annex IX-A) is documented in Malaysia's mandate.

**Feedstock structure:**
- **B15/B20 mandates:** 100% palm oil-based (FAME); no cap on palm content, no crop-diversification requirement.
- **B7:** Industrial-sector requirement; also palm oil-based.
- **B30 (2030 target):** Likely palm oil FAME, with potential exploration of HVO (hydrogenated vegetable oil) or other renewable hydrocarbons for future iterations.

**Sustainability & certification:**
- No formal national sustainability certification scheme (ISCC EU, 2BSvs, REDcert) is mandated for the B15/B7 blends.
- KPK oversees supply-chain coordination; no public registry or traceability system equivalent to the EU's Union Database (UDB) is documented.
- International certification is not a regulatory requirement but may be voluntarily adopted for export-market access.

---

## 4. Fuel-tax treatment of biofuels

**Fuel excise structure in Malaysia:**
- Malaysia has not published a separate, formal excise rate schedule specifically differentiating biofuel blends (B7, B10, B15) as of 2026-07-15.
- Fuel taxation in Malaysia is historically administered by the Ministry of Domestic Trade and Consumer Affairs and the Customs and Excise Division; prices are often set administratively rather than purely by excise rates.
- **Biofuel tax treatment:** No explicit tax reduction or exemption for biofuel-blended fuels has been identified in published sources.
- Pump prices for B15 diesel are set by market and regulatory coordination; the premium/discount relative to conventional diesel is determined by crude oil and crude palm oil prices, not a formal tax mechanism.

**Status:** Gap — detailed excise rate schedule and any biofuel-specific tax treatment not located in 2026 English-language sources; Malaysian tax authority may have more detailed information in BM (Bahasa Malaysia) publications.

---

## 5. Sustainability and certification regime

**No national sustainability certification scheme** is formally mandated for Malaysia's B15/B7 blends as of 2026-07-15.

- **Sustainability criteria:** Not explicitly regulated in the B15 mandate language. No reference to RED II/III or similar EU frameworks in Malaysian legislation.
- **Feedstock traceability:** KPK coordinates supply; no public registry or verification requirement documented at the national level.
- **Environmental standards:** Industry stakeholders (biodiesel producers, palm oil millers) operate under MSPO (Malaysian Sustainable Palm Oil) certification for export markets, but this is a voluntary industry scheme, not a mandate tied to the blending obligation.
- **International schemes:** ISCC EU and 2BSvs are available but not nationally mandated for B15 compliance.

## 5-line summary

Malaysia operates a **volume-percent blending mandate**, with **B15 biodiesel (15% palm oil FAME) now in effect for transport as of June 1, 2026**, replacing B10 (since Feb 2019). **B7 remains for industrial use.** A **phased trajectory toward B20 (2027–2028, proposed) and B30 (2030 target) is under the 13th Malaysia Plan**, but these are not formally legislated. Malaysia is **not RED III compliant** (non-EU); the mandate is autonomous national policy. **Key gaps:** No formal national sustainability certification scheme; B20/B30 dates are aspirational/proposed, not legislated; excise-rate schedule for biodiesel blends not published.
