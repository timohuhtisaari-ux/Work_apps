# Switzerland (CH) — Section 2: Fuel taxation

- **Report date:** 2026-07-05 (draft filename 2026-07-04)
- **Researched by:** fuel-tax-researcher
- **Corroboration pass:** pending
- **Scope note:** Switzerland is **not EU/EEA**; the Energy Taxation Directive
  2003/96/EC does **not** apply and there is **no EU ETS2** obligation. Road
  fuel is taxed under the **Mineralölsteuergesetz (MinöStG, SR 641.61)** and its
  ordinance (MinöStV, SR 641.611), administered by **BAZG/OFDF**. Carbon policy
  for **motor fuels** runs through the **CO2 Act (SR 641.71)** compensation
  obligation, **not** through the CO2 levy. The Swiss ETS links to the EU ETS
  for stationary/aviation only.

Two federal excises stack on road fuel: the **Mineralölsteuer** (mineral oil
tax) and the **Mineralölsteuerzuschlag** (mineral oil surtax).

## 2.2 CO2 levy vs. compensation obligation (the clarification asked for)

**Motor fuels (petrol, diesel) are NOT subject to the CO2 levy.** The Swiss
CO2-Abgabe is charged only on **combustibles/thermal fuels** (heating oil,
natural gas for heating). Instead, importers placing motor fuels on the market
carry a **compensation obligation** under the CO2 Act: they must offset a rising
share of transport CO2 emissions through domestic and foreign climate projects
and by bringing renewable fuels to market. Under the CO2 Ordinance partial
revision **in force 1 January 2026**, the total to compensate is **30% in 2026**
(25% in 2025), with a **minimum 12% compensated domestically** across 2025–2030
and a maximum 18% abroad in 2026. Importers may pass on a maximum of **5 Rp per
litre** to consumers. This is the Swiss functional analogue to a carbon charge
on road fuel — but it is a compliance/offset duty, not an excise or a levy.

## 2.3 Biofuel treatment — CORRECTION to the source registry

The registry (`research/sources/non-eu-states.md`) states the mineral-oil-tax
exemption for biofuels **"expired end-2023."** That is **outdated**. The
pre-2024 relief was indeed *scheduled* to sunset on 31 Dec 2023, but the
**revised CO2 Act (in force 1 Jan 2025) extended the mineral-oil-tax relief for
renewable fuels to 31 December 2030.** BAZG's own current page states verbatim:
*"Die heutige Regelung der Steuererleichterung ist von Gesetzes wegen bis zum
31. Dezember 2030 befristet"* ("the current tax-relief rule is limited by law
until 31 December 2030"), with full relief of up to ~75 Rp/L depending on
product, conditional on ecological and social minimum requirements. **So a
biofuel mineral-oil-tax relief is in force in 2026.** Registry should be
corrected. (See flag on possible 2024 transitional treatment.)

Because Switzerland has historically had **no blending mandate**, this relief is
not conditioned on "volumes above an obligation" (contrast Sweden/Finland). The
newer pressure to use renewable fuels comes via the importers' **compensation
obligation** (§2.2), not a classic quota.

## 2.4 Rate stability / announced changes

The 76.82 / 79.57 Rp/L headline rates are stable into 2026 (no 2026 budget
change identified). The last structural change traces to a **3.7 Rp/L** increase
on the surtax linked to the biofuel-relief financing mechanism (2021 onward).
Forward: renewable-fuel relief sunsets end-2030; compensation-obligation share
rises year on year (25%→30%→… to 2030).

### 4-line summary — Switzerland
- **Petrol:** mineral oil tax + surtax total **76.82 Rp/L (CHF 0.7682)**, in force 2026, stable (BAZG).
- **Diesel:** total **79.57 Rp/L (CHF 0.7957)**, in force 2026, stable (BAZG).
- **Biofuel relief:** contrary to the registry, mineral-oil-tax relief for qualifying renewable fuels (up to full exemption / ~75 Rp/L) is **still in force and legally runs to 31 Dec 2030**; no blending-mandate "above-obligation" condition.
- **Flags:** motor fuels are NOT under the CO2 levy — importers instead face a compensation obligation (30% in 2026, min 12% domestic, max 5 Rp/L surcharge, CO2 Ordinance in force 1 Jan 2026); registry line "exemption expired end-2023" needs correction.
