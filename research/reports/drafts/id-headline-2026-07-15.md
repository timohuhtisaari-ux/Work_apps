# Indonesia — Non-EU Biofuel Regulation Headline (2026-07-15)

## 1. Instrument type & 2026 headline obligation

**Type:** Blending mandate (volume %)  
**2026 Level:** B50 biodiesel (50% palm oil methyl ester) as of July 1, 2026; E5 ethanol (5% bioethanol blend) as of July 1, 2026 in selected regions.

Indonesia operates under a **mandatory blending regime** administered by the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM). The B50 biodiesel mandate became effective on July 1, 2026, replacing the B40 requirement that was in force from January 1, 2025. Simultaneously, the E5 ethanol mandate was introduced on July 1, 2026, in Jakarta, East Java, West Java, Central Java, Yogyakarta, Bali, and Lampung.

**Obligated parties:** Fuel suppliers and refineries (Pertamina and other licensed distributors).

**Strategic goal:** Indonesia aims to end diesel imports by 2026 and achieve energy independence. Government has allocated 3.5 million tonnes of crude palm oil for B50 production. No formal legislated target beyond B50 (July 2026) has been published; further escalation is under review but remains `PROPOSED`.

## 3. Advanced/sub-quotas and feedstock caps

**No explicit advanced biofuel sub-quota or feedstock cap structure** documented for Indonesia's blending mandates. The B50 and E5 regimes are currently **feedstock-agnostic at the regulatory level**, though in practice:
- **Biodiesel (B40/B50):** Primarily palm oil-based (FAME — fatty acid methyl ester).
- **Ethanol (E5):** The ESDM has mandated that all raw materials for E5 must be domestically sourced; three companies identified with combined capacity ~26,000 kL/year (far below the ~1.0–1.2 million kL/year needed for full E5 rollout). Sources: sugarcane (molasses) and cassava (proposed).

No formal sustainability certification scheme (ISCC, REDcert) has been documented as mandatory at the national level for these mandates; verification relies on supplier declarations.

---

## 4. Fuel-tax treatment of biofuels

**Fuel excise structure in Indonesia:**
- Indonesia has **not published a consolidated public excise table for fuels** (as of 2026-07-15).
- Fuel pricing is administered by Pertamina under government subsidy/regulation; prices are set administratively rather than via a pure market mechanism.
- **Biofuel tax treatment:** No specific biofuel tax reduction or exemption has been identified in published sources. B40/B50 and E5 are blended and sold at regulated pump prices set by the government, not differentiated by fuel type.
- Diesel and petrol are subject to indirect taxation via the subsidy mechanism; biofuel content is embedded in the pump price.

**Status:** Gap — full excise rate schedule for biofuel-blended fuels not available in English-language sources accessed; Indonesian-language official gazette may contain this detail.

---

## 5. Sustainability and certification regime

**No national sustainability certification scheme** is formally documented as compulsory for Indonesia's B50 and E5 mandates as of 2026-07-15.

- **Sustainability criteria:** Not explicitly regulated at the national mandate level. No reference to RED II/III (EU frameworks) in Indonesian legislation.
- **Feedstock traceability:** Suppliers are expected to provide source documentation for biodiesel and ethanol; the central role of Pertamina as the primary distributor implies internal supply-chain controls, but no public registry or verification system analogous to the EU's Union Database (UDB) has been identified.
- **International certification:** No mention of ISCC EU, 2BSvs, or Pronovo schemes in the mandate language; these may be adopted by private suppliers seeking international market access but are not nationally mandated.

## 5-line summary

Indonesia operates a **volume-percent blending mandate** (not energy % or GHG-intensity). As of July 1, 2026, **B50 biodiesel (50% palm oil FAME) and E5 ethanol (5% bioethanol, selected regions) mandates are in effect**, replacing B40 (Jan 2025–Jun 2026). No formal legislated trajectory beyond 2026 is published; a B50+ escalation may be considered but remains `PROPOSED`. Indonesia is **not RED III compliant** (RED III does not apply to non-EU states); the mandate is purely national policy without EU legal reference. **Key gap:** No formal national sustainability certification scheme; ethanol production capacity (26,000 kL/yr) is a material constraint on E5 nationwide rollout.
