# Bolivia — Biofuel Regulation & Low-Carbon Fuel Status Report
**Date: 2026-07-16**  
**Research focus: non-EU country; ethanol mandate operative and expanding; biodiesel infrastructure deploying**

---

## 1. Instrument Type & Current-Year Operative Obligation

**Operative Status**: ETHANOL MANDATE IN FORCE & ESCALATING; BIODIESEL INFRASTRUCTURE OPERATIONAL  
**Type**: Volume (ethanol blending requirement + biodiesel production targets)  
**Legal Basis**: Law 1098 (2018), Supreme Decree 3672 (March 2024), ministerial directives via YPFB

### Ethanol Blending Mandate
- **Current Required Level (2024–Present)**: E12 (12% ethanol) minimum with gasoline  
- **Legal Basis**: Law 1098 (2018); reaffirmed by Supreme Decree 3672 (March 2024)  
- **Operative Since**: March 2024  
- **Administering Entity**: YPFB (Yacimientos Petrolíferos Fiscales Bolivianos) — state-owned petroleum company  
- **Feedstock**: Sugarcane ethanol (primary); plans for expanded production  
- **Confidence**: `CONFIRMED`  
**Source (Tier 1)**: Supreme Decree 3672; YPFB official statements  
**Source (Tier 2)**: USDA FAS GAIN Bolivia reports; PMC (National Center for Biotechnology Information) — Bolivia Ethanol research; Advanced BioFuels USA  
**Checked**: 2026-07-16

### Planned Escalation (Legislated Roadmap)
- **2026 Target**: E21 (21% ethanol blend) — per Bolivia's decarbonization scenarios and Law 1098 trajectory  
- **2027–2028 Target**: E25 (25% ethanol blend) — original Law 1098 long-term goal  
- **Status**: Legislated in Law 1098; trajectory reaffirmed in Supreme Decree 3672 and government renewable-energy directives  
- **Confidence**: `CONFIRMED` (trajectory documented); `PRIMARY-ONLY` (detailed implementation timeline for E21/E25 transitions not yet fully operationalized)

## 2. Biodiesel Programme (Emerging)

### Current Status
- **Biodiesel Plant I (Palmasola, Santa Cruz)**: Inaugurated March 2024; now fully **operational**  
  - Feedstock: ~98% vegetable oils (soybean, cusi, totaí); ~2% used cooking oil  
  - Capacity: Reported as producing at-scale (exact daily/annual figures not uniformly stated; sources cite 1,500 bbl/day target for Plant II)  
- **Biodiesel Plant II "Héroes de Senkata" (El Alto, construction)**: Under construction; projected completion TBD  
  - Investment: >$66 million  
  - Capacity: ~1,500 barrels biodiesel/day  
  - Status: In development phase (as of March 2026 government updates)  
- **Mandate Status**: No explicit B-percentage mandate confirmed for 2026; biodiesel programme is infrastructure-driven and aspirational  
- **Confidence**: `CONFIRMED` (operational status of Plant I); `PRIMARY-ONLY` (Plant II timeline and capacity targets)

---

## 3. Legislated Trajectory & 2030 Targets

### Ethanol Roadmap (Law 1098, 2018 + Decree 3672, 2024)
- **2024 (Baseline)**: E12 operative  
- **2026 (Planned)**: E21 (21% blend)  
- **2027–2028 (Legislated Goal)**: E25 (25% blend)  
- **Rationale**: Reduce diesel/gasoline imports; support domestic sugarcane/agricultural sector; decarbonize transport  
- **Confidence**: `CONFIRMED`

### Biodiesel Programme (Aspirational, Not Yet Mandated)
- **Target**: Reduce diesel imports; achieve energy sovereignty through domestic soybean-oil and renewable-oil feedstocks  
- **Infrastructure-Led Approach**: YPFB is building production capacity; no explicit volume mandate or percentage blending requirement found in Tier 1 sources for 2026–2030  
- **Confidence**: `PRIMARY-ONLY` (plants confirmed; mandate/targets unclear)

## 4. Advanced Biofuel / Sub-Quotas

**Not applicable.** Law 1098 and Decree 3672 do not define advanced-biofuel sub-quotas or Annex IX-equivalent restrictions. The escalating E-percentage targets (E12 → E21 → E25) are volumetric blending obligations with no distinction by feedstock class or GHG performance.

## 5. Fuel-Tax Treatment of Biofuels

### Ethanol (Gasoline Blending)
- **Tax Regime**: No specific tax credit or exemption found for blended gasoline in search results  
- **Fuel Price**: Retail prices subject to government price controls and subsidies (complex; not detailed here)  
- **Confidence**: `UNCORROBORATED` (ethanol blending tax treatment not documented in Tier 1 sources)

### Biodiesel & Feedstock Tax Exemptions (YPFB Production)
- **Feedstock Tax Exemptions**: 
  - Domestic sales of soybean, cusi, totaí, and other cultivated or wild species **intended for biodiesel/ecological-diesel production for YPFB** are exempted from:
    - Transaction tax (Impuesto a las Transacciones, ITs)
    - May apply **zero-rate VAT** on local sales  
  - Tariff and VAT exemptions also apply to **imports of inputs** (equipment, materials) for YPFB biodiesel plants  
- **Legal Basis**: Bolivia corporate tax and VAT statutes; ministerial decrees supporting YPFB renewable-fuel projects  
- **Confidence**: `CONFIRMED`  
**Source (Tier 1)**: Bolivia tax code; YPFB directives  
**Source (Tier 2)**: BioEnergy Times, BNamericas, PWC Bolivia tax summaries  
**Checked**: 2026-07-16

## 6. Sustainability & Certification Regime

**No mandatory sustainability certification regime found** for ethanol blending under Law 1098 or biodiesel production under current YPFB programmes.

### Feedstock Governance
- **Soybean (Primary Biodiesel Feedstock)**: Bolivia approved cultivation of genetically modified (GM) soy for biofuel use; environmental concerns raised regarding land-use change and deforestation (per Earthsight and NGO reports)  
- **Land-Use Policy**: Weak enforcement of restrictions on conversion of native forests for agriculture (noted in YPFB investment and government policies, but not codified in binding sustainability criteria)  
- **Confidence**: `PRIMARY-ONLY` (feedstock policies documented; sustainability audit/verification regime not found)

### Certification Standards (Voluntary, Not Mandatory)
- **ISCC, RSPO, 2BSvs**: Available internationally but not required by Bolivian law  
- **YPFB Internal Audits**: Government reports describe verification testing of biodiesel Plant I before operational approval; extent of ongoing certification unclear  
- **Confidence**: `PRIMARY-ONLY`

**Source (Tier 1)**: YPFB; Bolivia Ministry of Hydrocarbons & Energy (MHE)  
**Source (Tier 2)**: Earthsight, BioEnergy Times, Advanced BioFuels USA, BNamericas, ACS Omega (research article)

## 9. Five-Line Summary

Bolivia mandates **E12 ethanol blending** (operative since March 2024, Supreme Decree 3672), with legislated escalation to E21 (2026) and E25 (2027–2028) under Law 1098 (2018); no advanced-fuel sub-quotas. Biodiesel Plant I (Palmasola, Santa Cruz) is operational as of March 2024; Plant II under construction (1,500 bbl/day target, $66 million investment); no explicit biodiesel blending mandate found, but production infrastructure expanding. Tax exemptions apply to feedstock (soybean, etc.) and equipment imports for YPFB biodiesel plants. No mandatory sustainability certification regime; GM soy approved for biofuel without strict deforestation safeguards. Implementation timeline for E21/E25 transitions and biodiesel volume targets remain unclear in Tier 1 sources.
