## 1. Instrument Type & Current-Year Operative Obligation

**Operative Status**: NO NATIONAL ETHANOL OR BIODIESEL MANDATE  
**Type**: Energy/volume (aspirational only)  
**Legal Basis**: NOM-016-CRE-2016 (Official Mexican Standard for Ethanol Blending Limits)

### Ethanol Blending
- **Current Maximum Allowable**: 5.8% (E5.8) as fuel oxygenate in Magna and Premium gasoline  
- **Geographic Exemption**: Mexico City, Guadalajara, Monterrey: 0% ethanol (E0 only)  
- **Effective Since**: 2016  
- **Confidence**: `CONFIRMED`

## 2. Recent Regulatory Conflict: E10 Court Ruling (January 22, 2020)

In January 22, 2020, **Mexico's Supreme Court ruled unconstitutional** an attempted 2017 regulatory amendment by the Energy Regulatory Commission (CRE/ERC) to raise the ethanol blending cap to 10% (E10) nationwide (with exceptions for the three major urban centers).

- **CRE's 2017 Proposal**: Increase to E10 to reduce high-octane gasoline imports  
- **Supreme Court Decision**: Invalidated the rule on **environmental grounds** — potential for higher pollution levels and increased GHG emissions; weighed these risks against economic benefits and determined the environmental harm outweighed the benefits  
- **Current Status**: NOM-016-CRE-2016 remains unchanged at E5.8 maximum  
- **Confidence**: `CONFIRMED`  
**Source (Tier 1)**: Mexico's Supreme Court; Ethanol Producer Magazine; Biofuels International Magazine, Advanced BioFuels USA  
**Checked**: 2026-07-16

## 3. Legislated Trajectory & 2030 Targets

**No specific biofuel blending target for 2030** has been enacted into law.

### Future Plans (Aspirational, Not Legislated)
- **SENER's stated desire**: 10% ethanol blend minimum; preference for 15–20% (Dir. Ana María Gómez Solares, per Hydrocarbons Law 2025)  
- **2025 Hydrocarbons Sector Law**: Transferred authority to set fuel-quality standards from CRE to SENER; biofuel blending specifications now fall under SENER purview  
- **2026 Planning**: SENER expected to publish  
  - Plan for Energy Transition and Sustainable Energy Use (PLATEASE) — 15-year horizon  
  - Plan for Hydrocarbons Development (PLADESHi)  
  These may include scenarios for higher biofuel production and blending, but are not yet adopted law.

### Renewable Energy (Non-Biofuel) Targets
- **2030 Goal**: 45% of electricity generation from renewables  
- **2030 Goal**: 38.5% of clean energy in electricity generation  
- **Confidence**: `CONFIRMED` (energy-wide, not biofuel-specific)

## 5. Fuel-Tax Treatment of Biofuels

### Excise Tax (IEPS — Special Tax on Production and Services)
- **Framework**: IEPS applies to sale/import of diesel, biodiesel, and gasoline  
- **Biofuel/Biodiesel Credit**: A tax incentive credit (effective 2026) allows eligible taxpayers to credit IEPS paid on fuel used for transport (passengers, cargo, tourism) against Income Tax in the same fiscal year  
- **Condition**: Fuel must be destined for final consumption; traceability of use must be demonstrated  
- **Recent Updates (June 2026)**: Agreement 98/2026 decreased available tax credits for imported gasoline and diesel (announced June 26, 2026)  
- **Confidence**: `CONFIRMED`  
**Source (Tier 1)**: 2025/2026 Excise Tax Law amendments; Mexican Ministry of Finance (Hacienda)  
**Source (Tier 2)**: Garrigues law firm client brief (2025); Global Trade Alert  
**Checked**: 2026-07-16

## 6. Sustainability & Certification Regime

**No mandatory sustainability certification** is required under Mexican law for biofuel blending (since no blending mandate exists).

### Optional/Available Standards
- **ISCC (International Sustainability & Carbon Certification)**: Available through SGS Mexico and other auditors; used voluntarily by some producers for export markets (esp. EU)  
- **Feedstock Requirements (Biofuels Law 2025)**: Feedstock production must not pose environmental risks or compromise national food self-sufficiency (e.g., no conversion of cropland for fuel)  
- **SENER Permitting**: Biofuels Law (March 2025) requires SENER permits for production, sales, and transport; as of Aug 2025, 42 valid permits issued (7 production, 16 sales, 6 transport)  
- **Confidence**: `PRIMARY-ONLY` (law provisions confirmed; no detailed sustainability auditing regime found)

**Source (Tier 1)**: Biofuels Law 2025; SENER; CRE  
**Source (Tier 2)**: Garrigues; Advanced BioFuels USA; ISCC systems  
**Checked**: 2026-07-16

## 9. Five-Line Summary

Mexico **lacks a national ethanol or biodiesel mandate**; the technical ceiling remains 5.8% ethanol under NOM-016-CRE-2016 (2016), prohibited in three urban centers. The Supreme Court ruled E10 unconstitutional in January 22, 2020, blocking a 2017 CRE attempt to raise the limit on environmental grounds. SENER (shifted authority in 2025 Hydrocarbons Law) has expressed intent to increase blending to 10–20%, but no legislation is in force; PLATEASE (2026) may signal future direction. Tax credits exist for biofuel/biodiesel use in transport, but production is negligible. No mandatory sustainability regime applies (ISCC voluntary).
