## 5. Fuel eligibility & sustainability criteria

### 5.1 What counts as "SAF" (the eligible-fuel universe)

**Definition — Article 3(7):** "sustainable aviation fuels" or "SAF" means aviation fuels that are **either (a) synthetic aviation fuels, (b) aviation biofuels, or (c) recycled carbon aviation fuels.** `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex 32023R2405; corroborated DG MOVE, EASA).

| Eligible pathway | Legal basis | Definition / condition | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Synthetic aviation fuels** (e-fuels) | Art. 3(12) | Renewable fuels of non-biological origin (**RFNBOs**) as defined in RED (Dir (EU) 2018/2001) that meet the lifecycle emissions-savings threshold of **RED Art. 29a(1)** and are certified under **RED Art. 30**. | `CONFIRMED` |
| **Aviation biofuels — advanced** | Art. 3(8)(a) | Advanced biofuels as defined in RED (feedstock in **Annex IX Part A**). | `CONFIRMED` |
| **Aviation biofuels — Annex IX Part B** | Art. 3(8)(b) | Biofuels produced from feedstock listed in **Annex IX Part B** of RED (e.g. used cooking oil, certain animal fats). | `CONFIRMED` |
| **Aviation biofuels — other** | Art. 3(8)(c) | Other biofuels **complying with the sustainability and lifecycle GHG-emissions-savings criteria of RED Art. 29** and certified under **RED Art. 30**, **excluding food/feed-crop-based biofuels**. | `CONFIRMED` |
| **Recycled carbon aviation fuels (RCFs)** | Art. 3(9) | Recycled carbon fuels meeting the lifecycle emissions-savings threshold of **RED Art. 29a(2)** and certified under **RED Art. 30**. | `CONFIRMED` |

Also relevant: Art. 3(6) "aviation fuel" = drop-in fuel manufactured for direct use by aircraft; Art. 3(11) "lifecycle emissions" computed via the methodologies of RED Art. 28(5) or Art. 31(5). `PRIMARY-ONLY` (EUR-Lex).

### 5.2 THE CROP EXCLUSION (key fact)

**Article 4 (final paragraph, cited in source as Art. 4(5)):** SAF produced from the following feedstocks is **EXCLUDED from counting toward the minimum SAF shares** in Annex I:

- **food and feed crops** (as defined in RED Art. 2(2)(40));
- **intermediate crops**;
- **palm fatty acid distillate (PFAD)**;
- **palm- and soy-derived materials**;
- **soap stock and its derivatives.**

**Sole carve-out:** the exclusion does **not** apply to feedstocks that are **listed in Annex IX to RED**, under the conditions set out in that Annex. In practice this means food/feed-crop and palm/soy-pathway fuels earn **zero credit** toward the mandate even if physically blended; only waste/residue (Annex IX) and synthetic/RCF pathways count. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex 32023R2405 Art. 4; corroborated DG MOVE). Rationale: Recital 23 (indirect land-use change / biodiversity loss).

Dynamic link: **Recital 22** — changes to the RED Annex IX feedstock list flow through automatically to the ReFuelEU list of eligible fuels (no separate ReFuelEU amendment needed). `PRIMARY-ONLY`.

> Cross-check with RED-transposition/mandates agents: this exclusion is stricter/parallel to the RED 7% crop cap — ReFuelEU does not merely cap crop fuels, it gives them **no eligibility** for the aviation SAF share.

### 5.3 GHG emissions-saving thresholds & certification

- **Biofuels:** must meet the sustainability and GHG-emissions-saving criteria of **RED Art. 29** (well-to-wake / lifecycle). `CONFIRMED`.
- **Synthetic fuels (RFNBOs) and recycled carbon fuels:** must meet the **RED Art. 29a** thresholds. The RFNBO/RCF minimum GHG saving is **≥ 70%** versus the fossil comparator of **94 gCO₂eq/MJ** (i.e. ≤ 28.2 gCO₂eq/MJ), per **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1185** (10 Feb 2023), which sets the RCF threshold and the RFNBO/RCF savings methodology. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex 32023R1185 + corroborating analyses).
- **Certification / chain of custody:** eligibility is anchored to **RED Art. 30** — i.e. compliance is demonstrated through the **EU-recognised voluntary schemes** (ISCC EU, REDcert-EU, 2BSvs, etc.) or a national scheme, using the RED **mass-balance** chain-of-custody. ReFuelEU does **not** create a separate SAF sustainability certification; it re-uses the RED apparatus. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 3(8)(c)/3(9)/3(12) all reference "certified in compliance with Article 30" of RED; corroborated DG ENER voluntary-schemes framework).

## 6. Monitoring, reporting, verification & compliance

### 6.1 Aircraft operators' annual report — Article 8

- **Who:** aircraft operators within scope (cross-ref scope section / Art. 3(4)).
- **Deadline:** **by 31 March of each reporting year, first time in 2025.** `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 8; corroborated EASA MRV page).
- **To whom:** the **competent authority** and **EASA (the Agency)**.
- **Format:** template in **Annex II**.
- **Content (per Union airport):** total aviation fuel uplifted (t); yearly aviation fuel required (t); yearly non-tanked quantity; quantity tanked for safety-compliance reasons; total SAF purchased incl. conversion process, feedstock origin and lifecycle emissions; number and hours of flights operated. `PRIMARY-ONLY` (EUR-Lex Annex II detail).
- **Verification:** the report must be **verified by an independent verifier** in accordance with **Articles 14 and 15 of the EU ETS Directive 2003/87/EC** (and its MRV/accreditation implementing rules). `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 8; corroborated EASA "Manual for Aircraft Operators and Verification Bodies").

### 6.2 Aviation fuel suppliers' annual report — Article 10

- **Who:** aviation fuel suppliers.
- **Deadline:** **by 14 February of each reporting year, first time in 2025.** `PRIMARY-ONLY` (EUR-Lex Art. 10; EASA MRV page did not restate the supplier date — flag for corroboration).
- **Where:** reported into the **Union Database** referred to in **RED Art. 31a** (the UDB), not to EASA directly. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 10; corroborated DG MOVE).
- **Content (per Union airport):** total aviation fuel supplied (t); SAF supplied by type incl. conversion process, feedstock origin and lifecycle emissions; **aromatics and naphthalenes** (% volume) and **sulphur** (% mass); energy content of fuels supplied. `PRIMARY-ONLY`.

### 6.3 The Union Database (UDB) — traceability backbone

- Suppliers enter fuel/SAF data into the **UDB (RED Art. 31a)**; this ties SAF traceability to the same chain-of-custody database used for all RED biofuels/RFNBOs. `CONFIRMED`.
- **Member States must ensure** the information entered by suppliers into the UDB is **accurate, verified and audited** before it is relied upon. `PRIMARY-ONLY` (EUR-Lex).
- **EASA and competent authorities** are granted access; EASA uses UDB data **only once it has been verified at Member-State level.** `PRIMARY-ONLY`.
- **UDB onboarding note:** because ReFuelEU supplier reporting is executed *through* the UDB, aviation fuel suppliers placing fuel on the EU market must be onboarded to the UDB (operated for the Commission by DG ENER) — same platform as road-transport biofuel operators. `PRIMARY-ONLY` — cross-ref sustainability agent's UDB-onboarding findings.

### 6.4 Competent authorities & enforcement — Articles 11–12

- **Article 11 "Competent authority":** each **Member State designates** the competent authority/authorities responsible for enforcing the Regulation. Attribution rules: aircraft operators → the administering Member State per Commission Reg (EC) No 748/2009 / EU ETS Art. 18a; airports → territorial; fuel suppliers → Member State of the supplier's principal place of business. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex, two fetches).
- **Article 12 "Enforcement":** Member States lay down **penalties/administrative fines**. Minimum fine formulas: `PRIMARY-ONLY` (EUR-Lex Art. 12):
  - **Art. 5 (uplift/anti-tankering) breach:** fine **≥ 2×** (yearly average aviation-fuel price per tonne × total yearly non-tanked quantity).
  - **Art. 4 (SAF minimum share) breach:** fine **≥ 2×** (difference between yearly average price of conventional aviation fuel and SAF per tonne × the tonnage of the SAF shortfall).
  - **Art. 4 (synthetic-SAF sub-share) breach:** fine **≥ 2×** (difference between yearly average price of synthetic aviation fuel and conventional aviation fuel per tonne × the synthetic shortfall).
  - A fine **does not release** the supplier from the obligation to make up the **shortfall quantity** — it must be supplied in the following reporting period **in addition** to that period's obligation.

### 6.5 EASA technical report & Union labelling scheme — Articles 13–14

- **Article 13 "Data collection and publication":** EASA **publishes a technical report every year** and forwards it to the **European Parliament and the Council**. Content includes aggregated Union- and airport-level SAF purchased/supplied, market/price/production trends, per-operator and per-supplier compliance status, aromatics/naphthalenes/sulphur analysis, and hydrogen/electricity infrastructure progress. This is the **aggregated Union-level SAF reporting** function. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 13; corroborated EASA "Annual Technical Report" page).
- **Article 14 "Environmental labelling scheme":** establishes a **voluntary** Union environmental labelling scheme measuring the environmental performance of flights (the "**Flight Emissions Label**"); operators **apply to EASA**, which issues an expected-CO₂ figure using a standardised methodology. Operationalised by **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/3170** (adopted 18 Dec 2024). `CONFIRMED` that the Art. 14 scheme is **voluntary** (EUR-Lex Art. 14 title/text + EASA). ⚠ **`CONFLICT` flag:** the DG MOVE summary page was paraphrased as "mandatory flight emissions labelling"; the Regulation text and EASA both state the Art. 14 scheme is **voluntary** — treat "voluntary" as correct and re-verify the exact status/timing of Implementing Reg (EU) 2024/3170 before final.

### 6.6 Entry into force / application

- Regulation signed/published **31 Oct 2023 (OJ)**; enters into force on the 20th day after publication and **applies from 1 January 2024**, with the **SAF-share and uplift obligations (Arts. 4 & 5) applying from 1 January 2025** and first reporting in 2025. `PRIMARY-ONLY` — the precise dates in the final article (entry-into-force/application) were only partially returned by the fetch; **verify Article on entry into force before final.**

## 7. Compliance checklist — items in this slice (draft)

*(Numbering to be merged by lead with the mandates/tax checklist items.)*

**For an aviation fuel supplier:**
1. Ensure all SAF claimed is from an **eligible pathway** (synthetic/RFNBO, Annex IX-A advanced, Annex IX-B, other non-crop biofuel, or RCF) and is **RED Art. 30-certified** under a recognised voluntary/national scheme. Verify **none** of the claimed SAF derives from food/feed crops, intermediate crops, PFAD, palm/soy materials or soap stock (Art. 4) — such volumes earn **zero** mandate credit. — *held: scheme PoS/UDB record.*
2. Confirm synthetic/RCF volumes meet the **RED Art. 29a ≥70%** GHG-saving threshold (Del. Reg (EU) 2023/1185 methodology); biofuels meet **RED Art. 29**. — *before consignment.*
3. **Enter fuel + SAF data into the Union Database (RED Art. 31a) by 14 February** each year, per-airport, incl. conversion process, feedstock origin, lifecycle emissions, aromatics/naphthalenes/sulphur, energy content (Art. 10). — *to: UDB; oversight: competent authority (principal-place-of-business MS).* `PRIMARY-ONLY` date.
4. Make good any **SAF/synthetic shortfall** in the following period **in addition** to that period's obligation; a fine does not discharge the shortfall (Art. 12).

**For an aircraft operator:**
5. Maintain a monitoring approach for fuel uplifted, required fuel, non-tanked and safety-tanked quantities, and SAF purchased per Union airport (Annex II data); an EASA monitoring-plan template/Fuel Monitoring Tool is available (optional).
6. Have the annual report **verified by an independent verifier** accredited per **Dir 2003/87/EC Arts. 14–15** (EU ETS MRV rules) before submission (Art. 8).
7. **Submit the verified Annex II report by 31 March** each year to the **competent authority and EASA** (via the EASA Sustainability Portal) (Art. 8). — *first cycle: 2025.* `CONFIRMED`.
8. (Optional) Apply to EASA for the **Flight Emissions Label** under the Art. 14 voluntary scheme (Impl. Reg (EU) 2024/3170).
