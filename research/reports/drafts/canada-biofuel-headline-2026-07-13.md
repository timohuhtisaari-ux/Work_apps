# Canada — Federal + Provincial Biofuel / Clean-Fuel Policy (Headline Level)

- **Jurisdiction:** Canada (federal + provinces/territories) — **non-EU**; described on its own terms.
- **Report date:** 2026-07-13
- **Researched by:** biofuel-mandates researcher (North America map task)
- **Purpose:** Headline renewable-content / low-carbon-fuel requirement per jurisdiction for a North America map.
- **Corroboration pass:** pending (drafted with Tier-1 primary + Tier-2 corroboration inline)

> **Framing note.** Canada is not an EU member state; RED II/III, EU ETS2, and the 21 May 2025 RED III
> transposition deadline do **not** apply. Canada runs a **federal carbon-intensity (CI) standard**
> (Clean Fuel Regulations) that applies to primary fuel suppliers nationwide, layered over a patchwork of
> **provincial volumetric blend mandates** (older, since ~2010–2011) and, in two provinces (BC, Québec),
> provincial **low-carbon / CI-based** requirements. There is no single national blend percentage.

## 1. Federal baseline (all provinces) — Clean Fuel Regulations (CFR)

**Instrument:** Clean Fuel Regulations, **SOR/2022-140**, made under the *Canadian Environmental
Protection Act, 1999* (CEPA). Administered by **Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC)**.

**What it is.** A **carbon-intensity-reduction** requirement (not a volumetric blend mandate) on
**primary suppliers** (producers and importers) of **gasoline and diesel** in Canada. Suppliers must
reduce the lifecycle CI of the gasoline and diesel they produce/import, relative to a **2016 baseline**,
by a growing amount each year, demonstrated through a **credit market** (three credit categories:
CI-reduction projects along the fossil supply chain; supplying low-carbon fuels; and supplying
energy to advanced vehicles/EVs). The CFR also carries a **minimum volumetric requirement**
(≥5% low-CI fuel content in the gasoline pool and ≥3% in the diesel pool at the supplier level),
with geographic exemptions (e.g. Newfoundland and Labrador and the territories are exempt from
the volumetric requirement).

**Headline CI-reduction trajectory (reduction below 2016 baseline):**

| Compliance year | Required CI reduction | Absolute CI limit (gasoline/diesel) |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 (from 1 Jul) | **3.5 gCO2e/MJ** | 80 → (2022/2023) 80 gCO2e/MJ |
| 2024 | ~ (schedule step) | 79 gCO2e/MJ |
| 2025 | ~ | 78 gCO2e/MJ |
| **2026 (current)** | ~ | **77 gCO2e/MJ** |
| 2027 | ~ | 76 gCO2e/MJ |
| 2028 | ~ | 74 gCO2e/MJ |
| 2029 | ~ | 73 gCO2e/MJ |
| **2030 and after** | **14 gCO2e/MJ** | **72 gCO2e/MJ** |

**Status / dates.**
- **Registered:** 21 June 2022. **In force (CI-reduction + volumetric requirements first apply):** **1 July 2023.**
- The CFR **repealed and replaced** the legacy federal **Renewable Fuels Regulations (SOR/2010-189)**
  (which set 5% renewable content in gasoline / 2% in diesel nationally). Repeal is effected by
  **s. 175** of SOR/2022-140. **Status: in force; RFR repealed.**
- ECCC published (2025) a **discussion paper on targeted amendments** to the CFR — those amendments are
  **PROPOSED**, not adopted; headline 2030 target of 14 gCO2e/MJ remains the operative endpoint.

**Confidence: CONFIRMED** (Tier-1: Justice Laws consolidated text + Canada Gazette Part II RIAS;
ECCC "About the CFR"; corroborated by Advanced Biofuels Canada / trade summaries).

**Notes on the two "low-carbon" provinces vs. the blend-mandate provinces.**
- **BC** runs the country's principal **LCFS** (CI-reduction standard, conceptually like the CFR/California
  LCFS) and *additionally* sets volumetric floors — it is the most stringent provincial regime.
- **Québec's** regime is **volume-based but CI-adjusted** (a hybrid), distinct from BC's pure CI standard.
- **Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario** are **volumetric blend mandates** (ethanol %/renewable-diesel %),
  the oldest layer of Canadian policy (~2010–2011), now overlaid by the federal CFR.

## 3. Provinces / territories with **no provincial instrument** (federal CFR only) — for the map

These jurisdictions have **no province-specific renewable-content or LCFS instrument**; the **federal
Clean Fuel Regulations** are the only biofuel/clean-fuel obligation applying (subject to CFR geographic
exemptions on the *volumetric* component):

- **New Brunswick** — federal CFR only.
- **Nova Scotia** — federal CFR only.
- **Prince Edward Island** — federal CFR only.
- **Newfoundland and Labrador** — federal CFR only; **exempt from the CFR volumetric requirement**.
- **Yukon** — federal CFR only; territories are **exempt from the CFR volumetric requirement**.
- **Northwest Territories** — federal CFR only; volumetric exemption applies.
- **Nunavut** — federal CFR only; volumetric exemption applies.

(Confidence: **CONFIRMED** that these four Atlantic provinces + three territories lack their own mandates —
Advanced Biofuels Canada "Biofuels in Canada" corroborates that Atlantic Canada has no provincial fuel
mandate; CFR text provides the volumetric exemptions. **PRIMARY-ONLY/CONFIRMED**.)

## 5-line summary

1. **Federal (all provinces):** Clean Fuel Regulations (SOR/2022-140) — a CI-reduction standard on gasoline/diesel primary suppliers, **3.5 gCO2e/MJ (2023) → 14 gCO2e/MJ (2030)** below 2016 baseline; in force **1 Jul 2023**; repealed the legacy Renewable Fuels Regulations. **CONFIRMED.**
2. **Provinces with own instruments (6):** BC (LCFS: **20.60% CI cut 2026 → 30% 2030**, plus 5% gasoline / 8% diesel renewable content, jet 2%), Alberta (**5% ethanol / 2% renewable diesel**), Saskatchewan (**7.5% ethanol / 2% renewable diesel**), Manitoba (**10% ethanol / 5% biodiesel**), Ontario (**gasoline 11%→13%→15%**, diesel 4%), Québec (**gasoline 10%→15%, diesel 3%→10%**, CI-adjusted). All **in force**.
3. **Federal-CFR-only (no provincial instrument):** New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, PEI, Newfoundland & Labrador, and the three territories (Yukon, NWT, Nunavut) — NL and territories are exempt from the CFR volumetric floor.
4. **RED III status:** N/A — Canada is non-EU; described on its own terms (no EU transposition obligation).
5. **Flags:** Québec 2026 interim step **UNCORROBORATED** (LégisQuébec 403); ECCC targeted-amendment paper is **PROPOSED**, not adopted.
