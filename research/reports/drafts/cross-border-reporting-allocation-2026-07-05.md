# Cross-Border Reporting & Enforcement Allocation — FuelEU Maritime vs ReFuelEU Aviation

- **Purpose:** deep-dive refinement of the two existing final reports on HOW the reporting/enforcement obligation is allocated across Member States for operators active in several countries. Focus only on the allocation mechanism; obligation levels, trajectories and penalty magnitudes are cross-referenced to the existing reports, not re-derived.
- **Instruments:** Regulation (EU) 2023/1805 (FuelEU Maritime); Regulation (EU) 2023/2405 (ReFuelEU Aviation). Both are directly-applicable EU Regulations — no national transposition of the substance; Member States only designate authorities and enforce.
- **Report date / facts date-stamped:** 2026-07-05
- **Method:** primary EUR-Lex text first (Reg 2023/1805 Arts 2–3, 23–25; Reg 2023/2405 Arts 3, 8, 10, 11; Directive 2003/87/EC Art. 3gf; Commission Reg (EC) 748/2009), Tier-2 corroboration per METHODOLOGY.md. Confidence labels on every row.

## PART A — FuelEU Maritime: one "administering State" per shipping company

### A.1 The obligated unit is the *company*, not the ship or the flag State

| # | Rule | Legal basis | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | The obligated party is the **"company"** = "the shipowner or any other organisation or person such as the manager or the bareboat charterer, which has assumed the responsibility for the operation of the ship from the shipowner and has agreed to take over all the duties and responsibilities imposed by the [ISM] Code" | **Art. 3(13)** Reg 2023/1805 | CONFIRMED | M1, M6 |
| A2 | The **company** (not the individual ship and not the flag State) is the compliance/reporting unit; the ISM Company under the ISM Code is the "shipping company" responsible unless another entity is designated in writing to the administering State | Art. 3(13) + Art. 4 (obligation on the company) | CONFIRMED | M1, M6 |

Note: this mirrors the maritime EU ETS, where the same ISM entity is the responsible "shipping company." The flag of the ship and the ports it happens to call at do **not** change who the obligated company is.

### A.2 The single-administering-State rule (the core allocation mechanism)

| # | Rule | Legal basis | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A3 | Each company is assigned **one "administering State"** that supervises it for **all its ships/voyages EU-wide** — the company does **not** report or settle separately in each port State | **Art. 3(40)** Reg 2023/1805 (defines "administering State" by applying **Art. 3gf(1) of Directive 2003/87/EC**) | CONFIRMED | M1, M2, M3 |
| A4 | "one shipping company shall have one administering authority for all its ships operating in Union waters" | Commission maritime-ETS administering-authority guidance (GD1) | CONFIRMED (Tier-1 guidance) | M3 |
| A5 | **Company registered in a Member State** → administering State = **that Member State** ("the Member State in which the shipping company is registered") | **Art. 3gf(1)(a)** Dir 2003/87/EC | CONFIRMED | M2, M5 |
| A6 | **Company not registered in a Member State (third-country)** → the Member State with the **greatest estimated number of port calls from voyages performed by that company in the preceding four monitoring years** | **Art. 3gf(1)(b)** Dir 2003/87/EC | CONFIRMED | M2, M5 |
| A7 | **Third-country company with no in-scope voyages in the preceding four monitoring years** (fallback) → the Member State where a ship of the company **started or ended its first in-scope voyage** | **Art. 3gf(1)(c)** Dir 2003/87/EC | CONFIRMED | M2, M5 |
| A8 | Port-call data for attributing an unregistered company is drawn from the **Union maritime information system (SafeSeaNet)** (Dir 2002/59/EC) | Art. 3gf / Commission guidance | CONFIRMED | M3, M5 |
| A9 | FuelEU explicitly imports the EU-ETS mechanism: "administering State" = the MS "determined by applying **Article 3gf(1) of Directive 2003/87/EC** … without prejudice to the choice of the competent authorities within the relevant Member State" | Art. 3(40) Reg 2023/1805 | CONFIRMED | M1 |

Cross-reference verified: the criterion in Art. 3gf(1)(b) is **greatest estimated number of port calls** over the **preceding four monitoring years** (not "voyages" as a distinct metric, and not the flag State).

### A.3 The Commission-published list and reassignment

| # | Rule | Legal basis | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A10 | The **Commission publishes/updates a list of shipping companies with the corresponding administering authority** | **Art. 3gf(2)** Dir 2003/87/EC (imported into FuelEU via Art. 3(40)) | CONFIRMED | M2, M3, M5 |
| A11 | Initial list **by 1 February 2024**; thereafter updated on a periodic cycle — updates reflecting **Member-State registration changes by 1 February 2026 and every two years**; re-attribution of **unregistered companies (Art. 3gf(1)(b) criterion) by 1 February 2028 and every four years** | Art. 3gf(2) Dir 2003/87/EC | CONFIRMED | M2, M3, M5 |
| A12 | A company is **reassigned** when its registration MS changes (two-year cycle) or, for third-country companies, when the port-call distribution shifts across the four-year re-attribution cycle | Art. 3gf(2) | PRIMARY-ONLY | M2 |

Note on cadence: the Commission guidance (M3) summarises the list as published "1 February 2024 … updated every two years"; the consolidated Directive text (M2) is more granular (two-year cycle for registration changes; four-year cycle for the port-call re-attribution of unregistered companies). No substantive conflict — the two-year figure is the headline cadence.

### A.4 What the company submits, and to whom (one channel, not per-port-State)

| # | Rule | Legal basis | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A13 | The company submits a **per-ship FuelEU monitoring plan** and the annual **FuelEU report** (amount/type/emission factor of energy used, compliance balance) to its **accredited verifier**, recorded in the central **FuelEU database (EMSA THETIS-MRV)** — a single EU-wide channel, **not** a separate filing in each port State | Arts. 8, 15, 16 Reg 2023/1805 | CONFIRMED | M1, M4, M6 |
| A14 | Fleet-level flexibility (**banking, borrowing, pooling**) operates within the company's own ships (pooling requires the same verifier), reinforcing that the company — under its single administering State — is the EU-wide compliance unit | Arts. 20–21 | CONFIRMED (cross-ref existing FuelEU report §3) | M1, M6 |
| A15 | The **administering State** (its competent authority) supervises the company, conducts additional compliance checks, **ensures the FuelEU penalty is paid** (before 30 June) and — where applicable — its competent authority issues the FuelEU **Document of Compliance** | Arts. 23, 24, 25 | CONFIRMED (mechanism; verbatim article text of 23–25 not re-fetched — see gaps) | M1, M6, M7 |
| A16 | Verbatim caveat: the long EUR-Lex HTML for Reg 2023/1805 truncates before Arts. 23–25 in automated fetches; the **allocation mechanism** (one administering State supervises/collects penalties EU-wide) is corroborated by the Commission guidance (M3), NEa operator guidance (M6) and Skuld (M7). Penalty *values* are CONFIRMED in the existing FuelEU report, not restated here. | — | PRIMARY-ONLY (verbatim 23–25) | M1, M6, M7 |

**Bottom line (maritime):** allocation is **one administering State per company**, determined by the company's Member State of registration (or, for third-country companies, greatest port calls over four years), imported wholesale from the EU-ETS Art. 3gf mechanism. That single State supervises the company's **entire in-scope fleet** EU-wide; reporting flows through one central database (THETIS-MRV), not to each port State.

## PART B — ReFuelEU Aviation: administering MS per aircraft operator + single competent authority per supplier

### B.1 Aircraft operators — one administering Member State

| # | Rule | Legal basis | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Each aircraft operator is assigned to **one responsible/administering Member State**, determined **pursuant to Commission Regulation (EC) No 748/2009** (the EU-ETS aviation administering-MS list); for operators not covered there, per **Art. 18a of Directive 2003/87/EC** | **Art. 11(5)** Reg 2023/2405 | CONFIRMED | A1, A2 |
| B2 | Determination rule = the **Member State that granted the operating licence**; otherwise the Member State with the **greatest estimated attributed aviation emissions** — identical concept to EU-ETS aviation | Reg (EC) 748/2009 / Art. 18a Dir 2003/87/EC, as applied by Art. 11(5) | CONFIRMED | A2, A4 |
| B3 | The operator files **ONE annual report (Annex II template)** to the **competent authority [of its administering MS] AND the Agency (EASA)** — **not** to each country it flies to; **by 31 March** each year (first 2025) | **Art. 8(1)–(2)** Reg 2023/2405 | CONFIRMED | A1, A2 |
| B4 | "each aircraft operator falling under the scope of RFEUA is assigned to one Member State" → consolidated single filing rather than per-destination filing | DG MOVE FAQ (Q4) | CONFIRMED (Tier-1 FAQ) | A2 |

### B.2 Aviation fuel suppliers — single competent authority (principal place of business), but per-airport reporting into the UDB

| # | Rule | Legal basis | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B5 | The SAF-share obligation is computed **per supplier**; a **transitional flexibility** lets a supplier meet the Annex I shares as a **weighted average across all Union airports it supplies until 31 Dec 2034** (per-airport from 1 Jan 2035) | Art. 4 + Art. 15 Reg 2023/2405 | CONFIRMED (cross-ref existing aviation report §3) | A1 |
| B6 | Suppliers **report per Union airport** — amount of aviation fuel supplied at each Union airport, and SAF supplied at each Union airport — **into the Union Database (UDB, RED Art. 31a)**, **by 14 February** each year (first 2025) | **Art. 10** Reg 2023/2405 | CONFIRMED | A1, A3 |
| B7 | **Enforcement allocation:** the competent authority responsible for a supplier is that of the **single Member State in which the supplier has its principal place of business** — "even if it operates in two or more Member States." It is **NOT** the competent authority of each MS where an airport sits | **Art. 11(7)** Reg 2023/2405 | CONFIRMED | A1, A2, A5 |
| B8 | "Principal place of business" = the head/registered office in the MS where the supplier's principal financial and operational control takes place | **Art. 3(21)** Reg 2023/2405 | CONFIRMED | A5 |
| B9 | **Fallback:** a supplier with **no principal place of business in any Member State** → the MS in which it **supplied the most aviation fuel in 2023** (or the first year of supplying the Union market, whichever is latest) | **Art. 11(8)** Reg 2023/2405 | CONFIRMED | A2, A5 |
| B10 | Airport managing bodies: responsibility determined **territorially** — the competent authority of the MS with jurisdiction over that Union airport | **Art. 11(6)** Reg 2023/2405 | CONFIRMED | A1 |

Key resolved point (task Q6): for a supplier delivering at airports in several Member States, enforcement is by **one** competent authority (principal-place-of-business MS, Art. 11(7)) — *not* per-airport. The **per-airport dimension survives only in the data** (each airport's volumes are reported line-by-line into the UDB, Art. 10), so the physical/territorial granularity is a *reporting* attribute, while *enforcement jurisdiction* is single-State.

### B.3 Competent authorities and EASA's central role

| # | Rule | Legal basis | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B11 | Each **Member State designates the competent authority or authorities** responsible for enforcing the Regulation (one **or more** per MS permitted) | **Art. 11(1)** Reg 2023/2405 | CONFIRMED | A1 |
| B12 | **Penalties** (administrative fines, Art. 12) are imposed by that competent authority — for the supplier, by the principal-place-of-business MS; for the operator, by its administering MS | Arts. 11–12 | CONFIRMED (cross-ref existing aviation report §4) | A1, A2 |
| B13 | **EASA ("the Agency")** holds the Union-level functions: it **receives operator reports** alongside competent authorities (Art. 8), **aggregates** Union- and airport-level data and publishes the **annual technical report** incl. price information (Art. 13), runs the **digital reporting tool** and the **Flight Emissions Label** voluntary scheme (Art. 14). EASA aggregates; Member-State competent authorities enforce | Arts. 8, 11, 13, 14 | CONFIRMED | A1, A2, A6 |
| B14 | EASA uses UDB / reported data for aggregation and benchmarking; Member States verify/audit supplier UDB data before reliance (RED Art. 31a mechanics) | Art. 10 + RED Art. 31a | PRIMARY-ONLY (cross-ref existing aviation report §6.3) | A1 |

**Bottom line (aviation):** two obligated parties, two allocation rules. **Aircraft operators** → one **administering Member State** (operating-licence MS / greatest attributed emissions, via Reg 748/2009 + EU-ETS Art. 18a); ONE report to that MS's competent authority + EASA. **Fuel suppliers** → one **competent authority = principal-place-of-business MS** (Art. 11(7)), even when supplying airports across several MS; but they still report **per Union airport** into the **Union Database** (Art. 10). EASA is the central aggregator/benchmark body, not an enforcer.

## PART C — Two-model comparison

| Dimension | FuelEU Maritime (Reg 2023/1805) | ReFuelEU Aviation (Reg 2023/2405) |
|---|---|---|
| Obligated unit(s) | The **company** (ISM entity) | **Aircraft operators** (uplift/anti-tankering) + **aviation fuel suppliers** (SAF share) |
| Allocation anchor | **One administering State per company** | **Administering MS per aircraft operator**; **principal-place-of-business MS per supplier** |
| Determination — EU entity | MS of **registration** (Art. 3gf(1)(a)) | Operator: MS that **granted the operating licence**; Supplier: MS of **principal place of business** (Art. 11(7)) |
| Determination — non-EU/third-country entity | MS with **greatest port calls** over preceding **4 monitoring years** (Art. 3gf(1)(b)); fallback = MS of first in-scope voyage (Art. 3gf(1)(c)) | Operator: MS with **greatest attributed aviation emissions** (Art. 18a Dir 2003/87/EC); Supplier: MS where it **supplied the most fuel in 2023 / first Union year** (Art. 11(8)) |
| Data basis for attribution | **SafeSeaNet** port-call data | Reg (EC) 748/2009 list (operators); self-declared principal place of business (suppliers) |
| Central published list | **Commission** list of companies + administering authority (from 1 Feb 2024, updated ~every 2 yrs; 4-yr re-attribution for unregistered) | **Reg (EC) 748/2009** list of aircraft operators + administering MS (EU-ETS aviation list) |
| Where the report goes | Per-ship monitoring plan + FuelEU report to **accredited verifier → central FuelEU database (THETIS-MRV)** | Operator: **Annex II report to competent authority + EASA**. Supplier: **per-airport data into the Union Database (UDB)** |
| Reporting granularity vs enforcement | Fleet-wide, single administering State (per-port-State filing **not** required) | Operator: single MS + EASA. Supplier: **per-airport data**, but **single-MS enforcement** |
| Central EU agency | **EMSA** (operates THETIS-MRV / FuelEU database) | **EASA** (aggregates, annual technical report, digital tool, labelling) |
| Enforcement/penalty collector | The **administering State** (Arts. 23–25) | The **competent authority** of the operator's administering MS / the supplier's principal-place-of-business MS (Arts. 11–12) |
| Key design principle | "One company → one administering State → one EU-wide compliance channel" | "One operator → one administering MS; one supplier → one enforcing MS; but supplier *data* stays per-airport" |

**The essential contrast:** maritime uses a **single, clean per-company administering State** for everything (supervision, reporting channel, penalties). Aviation **splits** the model: operators get an administering-MS single point of contact just like maritime, but **suppliers** face a hybrid — a **single enforcing competent authority** (principal place of business) sitting on top of **per-airport reporting into the UDB**. In neither regime does an operator/company/supplier report separately to every port/airport State; the per-location granularity in aviation is a data attribute, not a jurisdictional multiplication.

## PART D — Worked examples

**D.1 Shipping company calling at ports in 5 EU States (FuelEU Maritime).**
A company registered in Greece operates 12 ships calling at ports in Greece, Italy, Spain, France and the Netherlands. Its administering State is **Greece** (registration, Art. 3gf(1)(a)) — for **all 12 ships and all voyages**, regardless of how many calls occur in the other four States. It submits per-ship monitoring plans and annual FuelEU reports through **one** accredited verifier into the central **FuelEU database (THETIS-MRV)**; it may pool the 12 ships' balances; any FuelEU penalty is paid to **Greece** by 30 June; the Greek competent authority (or the verifier) issues the Documents of Compliance. Italy, Spain, France and the Netherlands receive **no** separate filing and do not enforce against this company for FuelEU. If instead the company were registered in Panama (third country), its administering State would be whichever of the 5 States recorded the **greatest number of its port calls over the preceding four monitoring years** (SafeSeaNet data), and everything would route through that one State.

**D.2 Fuel supplier delivering at airports in 3 EU States (ReFuelEU Aviation).**
A supplier with its principal place of business in Belgium supplies aviation fuel at Union airports in Belgium, Germany and Poland. Its **single enforcing competent authority is Belgium** (Art. 11(7)), even though two-thirds of its airports are elsewhere. It nonetheless reports **per Union airport** — the volumes and SAF supplied at each of the Belgian, German and Polish airports — as separate lines **into the Union Database** by **14 February** (Art. 10). Until 31 Dec 2034 it may meet the Annex I SAF share as a **weighted average across all three airports** (Art. 15). Germany and Poland do **not** each open an enforcement case; Belgium assesses compliance and imposes any Art. 12 fine. If the supplier had **no** EU principal place of business, the enforcing MS would be whichever of the three it **supplied the most fuel to in 2023** (Art. 11(8)).

**D.3 Aircraft operator flying to 10 EU States (ReFuelEU Aviation).**
An operator holding a French operating licence flies to Union airports in 10 Member States. Its **administering Member State is France** (licence granting MS, via Reg 748/2009 / Art. 11(5)). It files **one** Annex II annual report by **31 March** to the **French competent authority and EASA** (Art. 8) — covering the ≥90% uplift/anti-tankering position at each of the 10 airports — and does **not** file with the other nine States. France enforces the anti-tankering obligation and imposes any Art. 12 fine. EASA aggregates the operator's data into its Union-level technical report. A non-EU operator with no EU licence would instead be attributed to the MS with the **greatest attributed aviation emissions** (Art. 18a Dir 2003/87/EC).

## Gaps & caveats

- **FuelEU Arts. 23–25 verbatim** — the consolidated EUR-Lex HTML truncates before these articles in automated fetches (same issue flagged in the existing FuelEU report §8). The **allocation mechanism** (single administering State supervises, collects penalties, issues/holds the DoC EU-wide) is CONFIRMED via Commission guidance (M3), NEa (M6) and Skuld (M7); the paragraph-level verbatim of 23–25 is a citation gap, not a substance gap.
- **List cadence nuance (M2 vs M3)** — headline "every two years" (M3) vs granular 2-yr (registration) / 4-yr (unregistered re-attribution) cycles (M2). Reconciled, no conflict.
- **DG MOVE Art. 10 guidance PDF (A3)** is largely image-based and did not yield extractable text on this fetch; the supplier per-airport/UDB/14-Feb points are pinned to the primary text (A1) and FAQ (A2) instead, consistent with the existing aviation report (which corroborated A3 as V7).
- **Reg (EC) 748/2009 (A4)** not independently re-fetched this pass; its role as the operator administering-MS list is established via Art. 11(5) text (A1) and the DG MOVE FAQ (A2).
- **Art. 3gf(2) reassignment detail (A12)** marked PRIMARY-ONLY — the trigger/mechanics for mid-cycle reassignment were not separately corroborated beyond the Directive text.

### 6-line summary
1. **Maritime allocation:** one **administering State per company** (Art. 3(40) Reg 2023/1805 → Art. 3gf(1) Dir 2003/87/EC) — registration MS for EU companies, greatest-port-calls-over-4-years MS for third-country companies; that single State supervises the whole fleet EU-wide. CONFIRMED.
2. **Maritime channel:** the company files per-ship monitoring plans + FuelEU reports through one verifier into the central **FuelEU database (EMSA THETIS-MRV)** and settles any penalty with its administering State — never per port State. CONFIRMED (Arts. 23–25 verbatim is a citation gap).
3. **Aviation operators:** one **administering Member State** (Reg 748/2009 / Art. 18a — licence MS or greatest attributed emissions, Art. 11(5)); ONE Annex II report by 31 March to that MS's competent authority + EASA (Art. 8). CONFIRMED.
4. **Aviation suppliers:** single enforcing competent authority = **principal-place-of-business MS** (Art. 11(7)), even across many MS; fallback Art. 11(8); but **per-airport data reported into the Union Database** by 14 Feb (Art. 10). CONFIRMED.
5. **Model contrast:** maritime = clean single-State-per-company; aviation = single-MS per operator PLUS single-MS-per-supplier enforcement layered over per-airport UDB reporting; EMSA vs EASA as central aggregators (EASA does not enforce). CONFIRMED.
6. **Flags:** FuelEU Arts. 23–25 verbatim not re-fetchable (mechanism corroborated); DG MOVE Art. 10 guidance PDF image-based (points pinned to primary text + FAQ); Reg 748/2009 and Art. 3gf(2) reassignment mechanics not independently re-fetched this pass.
