# Philippines — Non-EU Biofuel Regulation Headline (2026-07-15)

## 1. Instrument type & 2026 headline obligation

**Type:** Blending mandate (volume %)  
**2026 Level:** E10 ethanol (10% ethanol blend) for gasoline; **B3 biodiesel (3% coco methyl ester, CME) for diesel**, with **B4 and B5 suspensions in effect**.

Philippines operates a **mandatory blending regime** under **Republic Act 9367 (Biofuels Act of 2006)** administered by the **Department of Energy (DOE)** in coordination with the **National Biofuels Board (NBB)**. As of 2026-07-15, **E10 has been mandated for all gasoline since 2012**, and **B3 for diesel is in effect since October 1, 2024**. The planned escalation to B4 (Oct 2025) and B5 (Oct 2026) was **suspended by DOE Advisory No. 2025-07-001 (July 17, 2025)** due to rising coconut oil prices and inflationary concerns.

**Obligated parties:** Fuel suppliers, petroleum distributors, oil refineries (Petron, Shell, Chevron, other downstream operators).

**Strategic goal:** The Biofuels Act aims to reduce fossil fuel imports and promote domestic renewable energy. As of July 2026, there is no formally published roadmap beyond the current B3 level, although the Department of Agriculture has called for lifting the suspension to implement B5.

**No formal 2030 target** has been legislated in accessible sources.

## 3. Advanced/sub-quotas and feedstock caps

**No formal advanced biofuel sub-quota structure** (analogous to EU Annex IX-A) is documented in the Philippine mandate.

**Feedstock structure:**
- **Ethanol (E10, E20 voluntary):** Sugarcane (molasses) + cassava (minor) as domestic feedstock; NBB allows importation of fuel ethanol due to insufficient domestic supply.
- **Biodiesel (B3, B4 suspended, B5 suspended):** 100% coco methyl ester (CME) from coconut oil; no cap on coconut content; no crop-diversification requirement.

**Sustainability & certification:**
- No formal national sustainability certification scheme (ISCC EU, 2BSvs, REDcert) is mandated for E10 or B3 blends.
- DOE and NBB oversee supply-chain coordination through coconut oil and ethanol industry bodies; no public registry (equivalent to EU UDB) is documented.
- International certification is not a regulatory requirement; some producers may voluntarily pursue ISO or industry standards for market credibility.

## 4. Fuel-tax treatment of biofuels

**Fuel excise structure in Philippines:**
- Philippines has not published a detailed public excise rate schedule specifically differentiating biofuel blends (E10, B3) as of 2026-07-15.
- Fuel taxation is administered by the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and subject to periodic adjustment; excise structure is embedded in pump pricing regulation by the DOE and Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC).
- **Biofuel tax treatment:** No explicit tax reduction or exemption for E10 or B3 blends has been identified in published sources.
- Pump prices are administratively regulated; biofuel content does not receive a preferential tax rate.

**Status:** Gap — formal excise schedule for Philippine fuel blends not publicly accessible in English-language sources; Department of Finance or BIR official rate documents (in English or Filipino) not located.

---

## 5. Sustainability and certification regime

**No national sustainability certification scheme** is formally mandated for the Philippines' E10 or B3 blends as of 2026-07-15.

- **Sustainability criteria:** Not explicitly regulated in RA 9367 or subsequent DOE circulars. No reference to RED II/III or similar frameworks.
- **Feedstock traceability:** NBB and DOE coordinate with coconut oil producers and ethanol suppliers; no formal traceability registry or audit requirement has been documented at the national mandate level.
- **Environmental standards:** Coconut oil producers may operate under voluntary sustainability schemes (e.g., RSPO, national agricultural standards); ethanol producers may pursue ISO certifications. These are not regulatory prerequisites for blending compliance.
- **International alignment:** The Philippines does not reference EU RED criteria; sustainability is not a gate for the blending mandate.

## 5-line summary

Philippines operates a **volume-percent blending mandate** established by **RA 9367 (Biofuels Act 2006)**. **E10 ethanol (10%) has been in force since 2012; B3 biodiesel (3% CME from coconut oil) since Oct 1, 2024.** **B4 (Oct 2025) and B5 (Oct 2026) were suspended by DOE Advisory 2025-07-001 (July 17, 2025)** due to coconut oil price concerns, though the Department of Agriculture called for lifting the suspension in June–July 2026. Philippines is **not RED III compliant** (non-EU); the mandate is autonomous national policy. **Key gaps:** B4/B5 suspension status as of 2026-07-15 unclear (no DOE reversal decree published); no formal national sustainability scheme; domestic ethanol capacity constraints.
