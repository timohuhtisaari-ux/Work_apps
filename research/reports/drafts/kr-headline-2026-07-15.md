# South Korea — Biofuel Instruments (Non-EU) — 2026

**Date-stamped: 2026-07-15**

## 1. Instrument Type & 2026 Headline Obligation

South Korea's biofuel framework combines a **volume-based blending mandate for biodiesel in diesel** under the Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS), plus emerging **aviation biofuel (SAF) policy** announced for 2027 onward.

### Road Fuels (Diesel)

**2026 Headline Obligation:**
- **B4 (4% biodiesel by volume)** mandatory in all automotive diesel from **January 1, 2024** onward, continuing through 2026.
- **Target escalation to B5 (5%)** mandated for **2030** (per the 2021 amendment of the New and Renewable Energy Act).
- **Obligated parties:** Petroleum refiners, fuel importers, fuel exporters (per Article 23-2 of the New and Renewable Energy Act).
- **Compliance mechanism:** Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS); annual mandatory blending ratios set by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE) with oversight by Korea Energy Agency (KEA).

**Legal basis:**
- **New and Renewable Energy Act (amended 2021):** Establishes RFS framework; Article 23-2 sets mandatory blending ratios up to 2030.
- **RFS Guidelines:** Ministerial regulations and KEA operational guidance (see MOTIE and KEA publications).
- **MOTIE Announcements:** Periodic circular notices setting annual mandatory ratios.

**Confidence:** `CONFIRMED` — B4 (2024) and B5 (2030) targets confirmed by multiple sources: Advanced BioFuels USA, Argus Media ("South Korea to raise biofuels mandate to B5 by 2030"), Chambers & Partners legal guide (Renewable Energy 2025 Korea). Statutory basis (NRE Act 2021 amendment, Article 23-2) confirmed.

## 3. Advanced/Sub-Quotas

**No explicit advanced-biofuel sub-quota or sub-target carve-out found within the RFS.**

- Biodiesel B4/B5 blends do not distinguish feedstock type (waste cooking oil, animal fats, palm oil, soy permitted).
- No Annex IX Part A / advanced-feedstock sub-category within the mandate.
- **Feedstock composition:** Korean biodiesel sources:
  - **Waste cooking oil (WCO):** Primary domestic feedstock; government incentives for WCO collection.
  - **Animal fats:** Allowed; rendered from meat processing.
  - **Palm oil, soy, camelina:** Imported feedstocks; no restriction or sub-quota cap found.

**Eco-Friendly Biofuel Expansion Plan (announced, non-legislated):**
- Next-generation biodiesel (hydrogenated animal/vegetable oils) targeting 8% blend by 2030 (per some government announcements).
- **Status:** `PROPOSED` — not yet legislated; pilot/demonstration phase.

**Confidence:** `PRIMARY-ONLY` — RFS framework (B4/B5) contains no sub-quota language. Next-generation biodiesel targets are policy aspiration, not law. Feedstock diversity noted in trade sources but no mandatory sub-category found.

## 4. Fuel-Tax Treatment of Biofuels

### Road Fuels (Diesel)

**Tax Incentives:**
- **Tax exemption (historical):** Biodiesel granted tax exemption starting 2006 (at 0.5% blend); intended to promote early RFS development.
- **Current status (2026):** No explicit current-year excise tax exemption or reduction rate found in primary sources for biodiesel.
- **Corporate tax / Production incentives:** Biodiesel producers may qualify for corporate income tax (CIT) preferences and R&D tax credits, but these are not fuel-tax instruments; they apply to producers, not at pump.

**Subsidy mechanisms:**
- **WCO feedstock incentives:** Government has promoted waste cooking oil collection via subsidies and tax incentives to stabilize biodiesel feedstock.
- **RFS portfolio compliance:** Fuel suppliers use RFS credits/certificates to demonstrate compliance; no buy-out price or penalty fee explicitly quantified in sources accessed.

**Confidence:** `PRIMARY-ONLY` — Historical tax exemption (2006) confirmed; current 2026 excise tax status unclear (likely expired). Production/feedstock incentives noted in trade analysis but not quantified. No pump-level preferential rate equivalent to Vietnam or Thailand found.

## 5. Sustainability / Certification Regime

### Biodiesel (B4/B5)

**Sustainability certification:**
- **No mandatory national certification scheme identified** for Korean biodiesel producers in primary sources.
- **RSPO (Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil):** Available certification for imported palm-oil feedstock; Control Union and SGS operate in South Korea; uptake is voluntary/supply-chain driven.
- **ISCC (International Sustainability and Carbon Certification):** Available but not mandated by Korean regulations.
- **National certification:** South Korea does not appear to maintain a standalone national biofuel sustainability scheme equivalent to EU's RED II/III or ISCC EU.

**Feedstock restrictions:**
- No explicit ILUC (Indirect Land Use Change) risk assessment or high-ILUC feedstock ban found in primary sources.
- Palm oil and soy biodiesel are permitted in blends (no phase-out or cap identified).
- Waste cooking oil and animal fats (domestic primary sources) are not subject to sustainability restrictions in primary Korean legislation.

**Confidence:** `UNCORROBORATED` — Korean government does not appear to mandate sustainability certification for RFS biodiesel. RSPO and ISCC schemes available but uptake status unknown. Korean biofuel sustainability framework not formally legislated as of July 15, 2026.
