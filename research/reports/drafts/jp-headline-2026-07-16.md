# Japan — Biofuel Regulation Report
**As of 2026-07-16**

## 1. Instrument Type & Current-Year Headline Obligation

**Mandate type:** Volumetric biofuel blending mandate under **Act on Sophisticated Methods of Energy Supply Structures** (sophistic. act), implemented via mandatory targets for oil refineries.

**Current operative level (2026):**
- **Bioethanol (ETBE route):** 824 million liters per annum (equivalent to 500 million liters crude oil equivalent) — in force since March 2023 amendments, effective through fiscal year 2028 (ends March 2029) — **CONFIRMED** (USDA FAS GAIN Biofuels Annual JA2025-0063; Growth Energy statement 2023-03-31).
- **Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF):** 10% of jet fuel consumed by Japanese airlines by 2030 (non-mandatory target stated by MLIT). Oil wholesalers producing ≥100 kt/year jet fuel must supply an amount equivalent to ≥5% GHG emissions reduction from FY2019 baseline — **PROPOSED/IN DEVELOPMENT** (announced December 2022 by MLIT Basic Policy for Promoting Decarbonization in Aviation; supplier obligation adopted September 2024 under amended Act, per IEA/InfluenceMap).
- **Biodiesel:** No mandatory blend mandate. Maximum 5% FAME (Fatty Acid Methyl Ester) permitted under Japan Quality Assurance Law; limited niche market — **CONFIRMED** (USDA FAS Biofuels Annual reports 2025-2026).

**Legal basis:** 
- Act on Sophisticated Methods of Energy Supply Structures (Enecho). 
- Amendment effective **2023-03-30**, in force through **2028-03-31** (FY 2028).
- Covered under Notification 3.0 (METI) and revised LCA standards.

---

## 2. Legislated Trajectory / 2030 Target

### Bioethanol (ETBE) & Direct Blending
- **2026–2028 (current period):** 824 ML bioethanol / 500 ML crude oil equivalent annually; ETBE remains primary delivery mechanism (imported bio-ETBE from Brazil, US, etc.).
- **FY 2028 onward:** Planned introduction of **E10 (10% ethanol direct blending)** in limited regional rollouts (Okinawa starting FY 2028); nationwide deployment targeted for FY 2030 — **PRIMARY-ONLY** (USDA FAS report JA2025-0035 "Action Plan to Introduce E10 and E20"; BioEnergy Times 2025).
- **E20 (20% ethanol):** Mentioned in roadmap; no specific mandate date yet — **PROPOSED** (referenced in METI advisory documents, not legislated).
- **GHG reduction target:** Current: 55% below gasoline baseline (U.S. corn-based ethanol ≈ 36.86 gCO2/MJ; Brazilian sugarcane ≈ 28.59 gCO2/MJ). Target to increase to 60% by **spring 2026** once new gasoline LCA finalized — **CONFIRMED** (USDA FAS JA2025-0039 "Japan Raises Carbon Intensity Value"; Notification 3.0).

### Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF)
- **2030 target:** 10% of jet fuel (advisory/aspirational policy target by MLIT).
- **Oil wholesaler supply target (legislated):** Equivalent to ≥5% GHG reduction from FY2019 baseline of jet fuel supplied — adopted **September 2024** under amendments to Sophistication Act — **CONFIRMED** (InfluenceMap Japan; METI Decarbonized Fuel Policy Subcommittee 2024-09).
- **No trajectory beyond 2030 legislated** — **GAP**.

---

## 3. Advanced-Biofuel / Sub-Quotas

### SAF
- Supplier obligation: oil wholesalers ≥100 kt/year jet fuel production/supply must achieve 5% GHG reduction equivalent (not a volumetric quota, but an emission-intensity requirement).
- Tax credit: 30 JPY/liter for domestically produced and sold SAF (introduced **2023 fiscal year** under Strategic Domestic Production Promotion Tax Incentive, formalized in **2024 Tax Reform Act**) — **CONFIRMED** (EY Japan tax alert 2024; Alternative Fuels Data Center).
- Subsidy: 4.1 JPY/L fixed-price reduction available (METI) — **PRIMARY-ONLY** (Resourcewise article 2024).
- **No mandated sub-quota within the main 824 ML ETBE target for advanced bioethanol** — **CONFIRMED**.

### Biodiesel & Advanced Options
- Refiner Idemitsu planning to supply imported B5-equivalent (hydro-processed ester and fatty acids, HEFA) starting early 2025; planned SAF and by-product biodiesel production at Tokuyama plant from FY 2028–2029 — **CONFIRMED** (Argus Media 2024).
- No legislated biodiesel mandate; sustainability certification for advanced feedstocks available (see Section 5).

## 4. Fuel-Tax Treatment of Biofuels

### Excise / Tariff Status
- **Bio-ETBE import tariff:** Abolished **2008** — **CONFIRMED** (Growth Energy, U.S. Grains Council).
- **Ethanol import tariff (for ETBE use):** Abolished **2016** — **CONFIRMED** (ibid.).
- **Ongoing tariff reductions:** Gradual decreases under CPTPP, Japan–EU EPA, U.S.–Japan Trade Agreement — **PRIMARY-ONLY** (USDA FAS Biofuels Annual 2025).

### Domestic Taxes & Credits
- **SAF:** 30 JPY/L tax credit (2024 tax reform onwards) for domestic production — **CONFIRMED** (EY Japan; AFDC).
- **Provisional gasoline tax:** 25.1 yen/liter currently in place; **planned abolition by end of 2025** (announced under PM Sanae Takaichi administration) — **PROPOSED** (USDA FAS JA2025-0035). If abolished, may slow E10 adoption momentum.
- **No specific excise exemption or reduction for bio-ETBE or ethanol blends** — **CONFIRMED** (searches found no dedicated ETBE/ethanol excise relief).

### Impact
- Fuel duty abolished for imported feedstock; SAF receives 30 JPY/L subsidy; direct-blend ethanol (E10 post-2028) not yet subject to dedicated tax treatment — **CONFIRMED**.

## 5. Sustainability / Certification Regime

### Accepted Schemes
Japan recognizes the following voluntary certification schemes for sustainable biofuel feedstocks:

1. **ISCC Japan FIT** — METI-recognized certification for sustainable materials eligible under Japan's Feed-in Tariff system for electricity/heat. Criteria: land-use change excluded, no food-use conflict, ecosystem impacts assessed, mass-balance approach. Recognized **for electricity FIT; biofuel transport mandate applicability not explicitly stated** — **PRIMARY-ONLY** (ISCC System; RSB 2024).

2. **RSB Japan FIT** — Revised by RSB, formally recognized by METI **August 2024**, with expanded feedstock list (cashew nut shell, palm kernel shell, coconut shell, corn straw pellet, etc.). Alignment with biodiversity and sustainable procurement criteria — **CONFIRMED** (RSB announcement 2024-08-12).

3. **Sustainable Biomass Procurement Standard (SBP)** — Instruction document for Japan available; applicability to transport biofuels under Sophistication Act not explicitly cross-referenced — **PRIMARY-ONLY** (SBP Instruction Document Japan v1.0, 2024).

### Criteria
Under the Sophistication Act amendments (Notification 3.0), the LCA framework requires:
- GHG reduction: 55% (target to 60% spring 2026) vs. gasoline baseline.
- Eligible feedstocks: Primarily agricultural residues, waste oils, and import eligibility confirmed for Brazil sugarcane, U.S. corn, Thai sugar, Thai cassava (expanded in Notification 3.0) — **CONFIRMED** (METI Ordinance 3.0; USDA FAS).
- **No feedstock bans (e.g., palm phase-out, soy caps) legislated; accepted at LCA level** — **CONFIRMED**.

### Verification & Registry
- **No dedicated national biofuel registry** (unlike EU Nabisy, HBE). Compliance verified by METI through oil refinery reporting and third-party audit of feedstock documentation — **PRIMARY-ONLY** (METI guidance; no public registry found).

## 6. Status & Data Flags

### Operative Status
- ETBE/bioethanol mandate: **IN FORCE** (through 2028-03-31).
- SAF supplier obligation: **RECENTLY LEGISLATED** (September 2024), operational framework for supply tracking under development.
- E10 direct blending: **IN PILOTING** (Okinawa FY 2028 test); nationwide FY 2030 target not yet legislated, pending vehicle fleet compatibility studies.
- Certification regime: **PARTIAL** — schemes recognized, but no unified national registry or mandatory scheme designation for transport biofuels (electricity FIT-focused).

### Unresolved / Flagged Items

1. **GHG reduction target spring 2026 update:** Target to increase from 55% to 60% pending new gasoline LCA. Effective date, timing of regulatory amendment not finalized — **FLAG** (USDA FAS JA2025-0039).

2. **Provisional gasoline tax abolition impact:** If abolished by end 2025, may reduce blending economics for E10 pilots and affect 2028–2030 rollout trajectory — **FLAG** (PROPOSED; not yet enacted).

3. **E10 nationwide deployment 2030:** No legislative requirement yet; pilot phase (FY 2028) will inform final mandate decision. Depends on vehicle compatibility approval (road-testing phase) — **FLAG** (PRIMARY-ONLY; PROPOSED for 2030).

4. **SAF supply obligation target specificity:** "5% GHG reduction equivalent" is not a volumetric mandate; actual SAF volumes to supply by 2030 remain to be clarified by METI — **FLAG** (UNCORROBORATED).

5. **Biodiesel mandated introduction:** No timeline found for mandatory B2 or B5; current limit 5% FAME under QA law, with Idemitsu commercial supply starting early 2025 (voluntary) — **GAP** (no government mandate proposal located).

6. **Certification scheme mandatory designation:** ISCC Japan FIT and RSB recognized, but no formal requirement to use a single scheme for transport biofuels audit — **UNCORROBORATED** (searches did not locate mandatory certification requirement for Sophistication Act compliance).

## 8. Five-Line Summary

Japan maintains a **mandatory volumetric biofuel mandate of 824 million liters bioethanol (500 ML crude oil equivalent) annually through March 2028**, delivered primarily via imported bio-ETBE under the *Act on Sophisticated Methods of Energy Supply Structures* (amended March 2023). A **10% SAF target by 2030** is declared policy; supplier obligations (≥5% GHG reduction equivalent) were legislatively adopted September 2024. **Direct ethanol blending (E10) is planned for fiscal 2028 pilot and nationwide FY 2030 deployment**, pending vehicle compatibility approval. **Biodiesel remains a niche market capped at 5% FAME** with no mandated introduction timeline. **Tax treatment includes a 30 JPY/L credit for domestically produced SAF** and planned abolition of provisional gasoline tax (25.1 yen/L by end 2025), though broader fuel-tax exemptions for bio-ETBE or ethanol blends are absent. Certification schemes (ISCC Japan FIT, RSB Japan FIT) are recognized; no unified national registry exists for transport-biofuel compliance.
