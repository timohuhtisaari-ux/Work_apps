# Norway — Biofuel Regulation Compliance Report

- **Country:** Norway (NO) — EEA member, non-EU
- **Report date:** 2026-07-04
- **Researched by:** mandates/tax/sustainability researchers + corroborator
- **Corroboration pass:** done
- **Regulatory regime & EU relationship:** Norway sets its biofuel obligation
  **autonomously** under the **Produktforskriften** (Product Regulations,
  FOR-2004-06-01-922) chapter 3, administered by **Miljødirektoratet**. As an
  **EEA** member Norway is **not** bound by the EU RED III transposition deadline
  of 21 May 2025, and there is **no EU ETS2** obligation on Norwegian road fuels
  (domestic carbon pricing is the CO2-avgift). RED II (2018/2001) is EEA-relevant,
  but **RED II / RED III incorporation into the EEA Agreement / Norwegian law was
  not confirmed** in this pass — a central gap (see §5). Do not read EU state-aid
  or EU ETS2 obligations onto Norway; state aid here is supervised by ESA/EFTA.

## Executive summary

Norway's obligation is a **volume-percent biofuel sales mandate (omsetningskrav)**
levied per transport segment on any party placing liquid fuel on the market, not
a GHG- or energy-share obligation. For 2026 the road obligation is **20 vol%**
biofuel (of which ≥13.5% advanced/Annex-V-B and ≥1% advanced-only), with parallel
segment mandates of 11% (other purposes), 7% (maritime) and 2% (aviation);
compliance is enforced by Miljødirektoratet, backed by an overtredelsesgebyr of up
to ~NOK 1.86 m. The road mandate is legislated to rise to **21 vol% from 1 January
2027** (14.5% / 2% sub-quotas), while aviation stays fixed at 2% (ReFuelEU
trajectory, no 2027 step). On tax, Norway levies a veibruksavgift plus a
CO2-avgift on road fuel; since 2020 **all liquid biofuel pays the full
veibruksavgift** whether inside or above the mandate, so the only fiscal advantage
left to liquid biofuel is CO2-avgift relief on its biogenic share — biogas is fully
exempt. The top supplier obligations are: meet the per-segment volume mandate, hold
sustainability proof under an EU-recognised voluntary scheme, run a quarterly
mass-balance, and file an auditor-verified annual report to Miljødirektoratet by
31 March. Note that Norway categorically excludes high-deforestation-risk (palm)
biofuel from counting toward the mandate since 1 Jan 2020, and is not in the EU
Union Database or ETS2.

## 1. Biofuel mandates

Norway's instrument is the **omsetningskrav** — a volume-percent biofuel *sales*
(turnover) obligation on any party that places liquid fuel on the market, run per
transport segment. Administered and enforced by **Miljødirektoratet** (Norwegian
Environment Agency).

### 1.2 Scheme mechanics

- **Obligated party:** any "omsetter av flytende drivstoff" (party placing liquid
  fuel on the market) per segment; compliance is calculated on annual total volume
  sold. No litre de-minimis identified in the text (gap).
- **Segments run in parallel:** road (§3-3), aviation (§3-3a), maritime (§3-3b),
  other purposes (§3-3c). Produktforskriften provides limited flexibility for
  **combined fulfilment (samlet oppfyllelse)** and flexibility between the road
  and other-purposes obligations [12].
- **Registry / reporting:** annual reporting to **Miljødirektoratet**; sustainability
  documentation via EU-recognised voluntary schemes (see §3).
- **Double counting:** as above (§3-3(2)(c)) — only advanced/Annex-V-B *above* the
  sub-quota, not below it.
- **Enforcement:** overtredelsesgebyr (§3-17). Interacts with fuel taxation: a
  **veibruksavgift** (road-use tax) is levied on biofuel sold *above* the mandate,
  which historically drove palm oil out of the market (see §2).

## 2. Fuel taxation

**Scope note:** Norway is an **EEA** state, not an EU member. The Energy Taxation
Directive 2003/96/EC is **not directly applicable**; Norway runs its own excise
system (særavgifter) set annually by the Storting's tax resolutions
(avgiftsvedtak) and detailed in the særavgiftsforskrift, all administered by
**Skatteetaten**. There is **no EU ETS2** obligation for Norwegian road fuels;
carbon pricing on road fuel is the domestic **CO2-avgift**. Do not read EU
state-aid decisions here (state aid is supervised by ESA/EFTA, not the Commission).

Norway taxes road fuel in **two stacked excises**: the **veibruksavgift**
(road-usage duty) and the **CO2-avgift på mineralske produkter** (CO2 tax on
mineral products). Both are levied per litre and both apply on top of each other
(and a sulphur duty).

**Interaction with the mandate (omsetningskrav).** Unlike Sweden/Finland (tax
relief only *above* the obligation), Norway does the opposite for **liquid**
biofuel: since 2020 **veibruksavgift applies to all biofuel** — inside *and* above
the mandate — so there is no road-usage-duty advantage to biofuel over fossil fuel
per litre. The remaining fiscal advantage of biofuel is that its **biogenic share
escapes the CO2-avgift**. Gaseous renewables (biogas/biomethane, hydrogen) remain
fully untaxed. The pending høring (S7) would erode the biofuel advantage further by
taxing the fossil share of high blends under the CO2-avgift.

**The 2026 fuel-price relief episode (adopted).** The Storting adopted — against
the sitting Government's budget proposal — a temporary **NOK 0** veibruksavgift on
all road fuels for **1 April 2026 to 1 September 2026** (Skatteetaten verbatim:
*"Satsene for veibruksavgiften er i perioden 1. april 2026 til 1. september 2026
satt til null (0) kroner per liter"* [S2]). Reported effect: pump price down ~NOK
4.41/L (incl. VAT) on petrol and ~NOK 2.85/L (incl. VAT) on diesel; estimated
revenue loss ~NOK 3.3 bn. The same relief package also introduces **temporarily
reduced CO2-avgift rates from 1 May 2026**, including **auto-diesel at NOK
3.09/litre** (vs the standard NOK 4.42/litre), installation diesel at NOK 1.92/L,
and reduced maritime/fishing rates [S5]. So during the relief window the CO2
component on auto-diesel is **also cut** (from 1 May), not merely the
veibruksavgift. Outside the relief periods the standard §1 veibruksavgift and
standard CO2-avgift rates in the table apply. This is **adopted**, not a proposal.

## 3. Sustainability & compliance requirements

> Framing: Norway is an **EEA member**, not an EU member. It does **not** transpose
> RED III on the EU timetable. Its biofuel sustainability regime lives in national
> law (Produktforskriften ch. 3) and mirrors the EU Renewable Energy Directive as
> incorporated into the EEA Agreement. RED II (2018/2001) and RED III (2023/2413)
> were **not yet incorporated into the EEA Agreement / Norwegian law** at the time
> of the sources checked — a central flag below.

### 3.1 Transposition instruments
- **Forskrift 2004-06-01 nr. 922 (Produktforskriften), Kapittel 3** — "Omsetningskrav for biodrivstoff og bærekraftskriterier for biodrivstoff og flytende biobrensel." This is the operative national instrument for both the sales mandate and the sustainability/GHG criteria. `PRIMARY-ONLY` (Lovdata).
- ILUC Directive implementation: **Forskrift 2018-11-21 nr. 1731** ("...gjennomføring av ILUC-direktivet") amended Produktforskriften ch. 3 to bring in ILUC-directive elements. `PRIMARY-ONLY` (Lovdata).
- **Flag — RED version lag:** RED II (2018/2001) and RED III (2023/2413) are not yet incorporated into the EEA Agreement or Norwegian law per sources checked; Norway's criteria therefore track the earlier RED framework. `PRIMARY-ONLY / to corroborate` (Miljødirektoratet/regjeringen material, USDA-type secondary).

### 3.2 GHG-saving thresholds applied nationally
Per **Produktforskriften § 3-6** (calculation per Annexes I, II, IV):
| Threshold | Applies | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **35%** GHG saving vs fossil comparator | up to 17 Jan 2017 (legacy) | `PRIMARY-ONLY` (Lovdata §3-6) |
| **50%** GHG saving | from 1 Jan 2018 onward | `PRIMARY-ONLY` |
| **60%** GHG saving | installations that started operating on/after 5 Oct 2015 (from 1 Jan 2017) | `PRIMARY-ONLY` |

- **Flag:** No **65%** tier (which RED II sets for installations starting after 1 Jan 2021) appears in §3-6 — consistent with RED II not being incorporated. Corroborator should confirm the current consolidated §3-6 text.
- Fuels from **wastes and residues** (except agricultural residues) need only meet the **GHG-saving** requirement, not the land criteria (§ 3-5). `PRIMARY-ONLY`.

### 3.3 Land / feedstock sustainability criteria
- **§ 3-5** — sustainability criteria apply to biofuels/bioliquids counted toward the mandate or receiving support, regardless of feedstock origin. `PRIMARY-ONLY`.
- **§§ 3-7 / 3-8** — exclusion of feedstock from land with high biodiversity value and high carbon stock (status referenced to January 2008), with limited documented exceptions. `PRIMARY-ONLY`.

### 3.4 Accepted certification / proof of sustainability
- **§ 3-10** — documentation of sustainability may be provided via **recognised voluntary certification schemes**; scheme documentation substitutes for individual proof within the scheme's scope. Norway relies on the **EU-recognised voluntary schemes** (ISCC EU, REDcert-EU, 2BSvs, RSB EU RED, etc. — the DG ENER list). `PRIMARY-ONLY` (Lovdata) + `CONFIRMED` for the EU scheme list itself (DG ENER page).
- **Proof per consignment:** a **mass-balance system** under **§ 3-10a** — different sustainability-characterised batches may mix at a physical location with characteristics remaining attached; balance reconciled at least quarterly; data cannot support claims beyond two years after intake. `PRIMARY-ONLY`.
- Raw-material classification in **Vedlegg V (Annex V)**: Part A (advanced feedstocks) and Part B (feedstocks enabling double counting). `PRIMARY-ONLY`.

### 3.5 National registry & Union Database (UDB) status
- **National reporting** is to **Miljødirektoratet** via an annual electronic report (skjema "Biodrivstoff: Årlig rapportering"). There is **no Nabisy-style transactional registry**; compliance rests on the annual report + mass balance + voluntary-scheme proofs. `PRIMARY-ONLY` (Miljødirektoratet reporting page/veileder).
- **UDB:** The EU Union Database is a tool under RED II/Implementing Reg. 2022/996. Because **RED II is not yet incorporated into the EEA Agreement**, Norwegian operators are **not subject to a national UDB onboarding obligation** and Norway does not run a UDB connection; operators rely on voluntary-scheme documentation (which the schemes themselves may register in UDB for EU-market consignments). `PRIMARY-ONLY / partly inferred — FLAG for corroboration.`

### 3.6 Feedstock restrictions — palm / high-ILUC
- **No standalone national statutory palm ban** was located; palm oil is governed through the sustainability/GHG criteria and the ILUC-directive implementation (Forskrift 2018-11-21 nr. 1731). `PRIMARY-ONLY`.
- **High-ILUC:** under the EU framework only palm oil currently meets the high-ILUC-risk classification (>10% expansion into high-carbon land); low-ILUC-certified palm can be exempt. Norway's transposition follows this logic. `PRIMARY-ONLY` (regjeringen høringsnotat) + Tier-2 (Miljødirektoratet biodrivstoff pages).
- **Practical outcome (context):** In 2023, **no palm-oil biodiesel** was reported sold under the mandate (2022: ~2 million litres). In 2024, biodiesel from **palm-production waste (e.g. POME/PFAD-type residues)** rose to ~4% of biodiesel volume. `UNCORROBORATED / Tier-2` (Miljødirektoratet news, Preem sustainability reports) — verify against the official annual statistics.
- **Advanced-biofuel definition tightened 1 Jan 2025:** "advanced" now counts only **Part A** feedstocks (previously A and B). This narrows what qualifies for the advanced sub-quota and affects UCO/animal-fat (Part B) treatment. `PRIMARY-ONLY / Tier-2` — corroborate against amending forskrift 2024-12-16 nr. 3392.
- **UCO / waste verification:** waste/residue consignments must still satisfy the GHG-saving criterion and be documented under a recognised scheme's chain of custody (Part A/B classification per Annex V). No Norway-specific extra UCO-audit rule beyond scheme requirements was located. `PRIMARY-ONLY`.
- **Public-procurement palm-free guidance:** Anskaffelser.no publishes guidance for buying deforestation/ILUC-low biofuel (palm/PFAD/soy-free) — a soft, non-statutory measure. `Tier-2 context only`.

## 4. Compliance checklist for a fuel supplier

1. **Meet each per-segment volume mandate for 2026** as an omsetter of liquid fuel: road **20 vol%** (of which ≥13.5% advanced/Annex-V-B and ≥1% advanced-only), other purposes 11%, maritime 7%, aviation 2% — calculated on annual total volume sold. Authority: **Miljødirektoratet** (Produktforskriften §§3-3, 3-3a/b/c).
2. **Classify every feedstock against Annex V (Vedlegg V)** — Part A (advanced) vs Part B — and note the **1 Jan 2025** narrowing of "advanced" to Part A only; apply double counting only to advanced/Annex-V-B supplied *above* the §3-3(2)(a) sub-quota.
3. **Do not count high-deforestation-risk (palm / palm by-product) biofuel** toward the mandate — excluded within and outside the mandate since 1 Jan 2020.
4. **Hold proof of sustainability** for every consignment counted toward the mandate under an EU-recognised voluntary scheme (ISCC EU / REDcert-EU / 2BSvs, etc.), covering the applicable **GHG-saving threshold (35/50/60% per §3-6)** and, for crop feedstocks, the land criteria (§§3-5, 3-7, 3-8). Authority: Miljødirektoratet / Produktforskriften §3-10.
5. **Operate a compliant mass-balance system**, reconciled at least quarterly, retaining records ≤2 years from intake (§3-10a).
6. **File the annual report to Miljødirektoratet by 31 March** each year for the prior calendar year, **verified by an auditor (revisor)** under §3-11.
7. **On the tax side, register with Skatteetaten** and account for veibruksavgift + CO2-avgift; note that liquid biofuel pays full veibruksavgift whether inside or above the mandate (biogenic share escapes CO2-avgift; biogas fully exempt), and mind the temporary 2026 relief windows (veibruksavgift NOK 0 for 1 Apr–1 Sep 2026; auto-diesel CO2-avgift reduced to NOK 3.09/L from 1 May 2026).
8. **Budget for penalty exposure:** overtredelsesgebyr up to **15 G (~NOK 1.86 m)** for failing the mandate or sustainability criteria (§3-17; Miljødirektoratet).
9. **Track EEA developments:** no UDB onboarding obligation currently applies (RED II not in EEA) — monitor EEA Joint Committee decisions on RED II/III incorporation.
