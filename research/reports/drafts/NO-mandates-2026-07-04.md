## 1. Biofuel mandates

Norway's instrument is the **omsetningskrav** — a volume-percent biofuel *sales*
(turnover) obligation on any party that places liquid fuel on the market, run per
transport segment. Administered and enforced by **Miljødirektoratet** (Norwegian
Environment Agency).

### 1.2 Scheme mechanics

- **Obligated party:** any "omsetter av flytende drivstoff" (party placing liquid
  fuel on the market) per segment; compliance is calculated on annual total volume
  sold. No litre de-minimis identified in the text (gap).
- **Segments run in parallel:** road (§3-3), aviation (§3-3a), maritime (§3-3b),
  other purposes (§3-3c). Produktforskriften provides limited flexibility for
  **combined fulfilment (samlet oppfyllelse)** and flexibility between the road
  and other-purposes obligations [12].
- **Registry / reporting:** annual reporting to **Miljødirektoratet**; sustainability
  documentation via EU-recognised voluntary schemes (sustainability agent's scope).
- **Double counting:** as above (§3-3(2)(c)) — only advanced/Annex-V-B *above* the
  sub-quota, not below it.
- **Enforcement:** overtredelsesgebyr (§3-17). Interacts with fuel taxation: a
  **veibruksavgift** (road-use tax) is levied on biofuel sold *above* the mandate,
  which historically drove palm oil out of the market (fuel-tax agent's scope — cross-reference).

- 2027 step-ups are **adopted law** (present in Lovdata §3-3(3), §3-3b, §3-3c;
  announced by Klima- og miljødepartementet on 2 July 2025) [1][6][7][8], i.e.
  legislated, not merely proposed.
- **RED III / EEA status:** Norway is not bound by the EU 21 May 2025 deadline.
  Its 2026–2027 levels already exceed RED III's transport ambition and are set
  autonomously. Whether RED III itself has been incorporated into the EEA Agreement
  could not be confirmed here (Gap).
