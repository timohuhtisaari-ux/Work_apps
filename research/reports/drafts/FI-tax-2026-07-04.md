## 2. Fuel taxation

Finland taxes motor fuels under the **Act on excise duty on liquid fuels
(1472/1994)** and the **Act on excise duty on electricity and certain fuels
(1260/1996)** — the national implementation of Directive 2003/96/EC. The excise
has three components: an **energy content tax** (uniform €/MJ across fuels, with
a fixed reduction for diesel-class fuels), a **CO2 tax** (€/tonne CO2-eq on
*lifecycle* emissions), and a **strategic stockpile fee (huoltovarmuusmaksu)**
levied on fossil **and** bio fuels. There is **no exemption for biofuels**;
instead the CO2 component structurally rewards them: **R-category** fuels
(RED-sustainability-compliant) pay **half** CO2 tax and **T-category** fuels
(advanced/waste-based, Annex IX A type) pay **zero** CO2 tax, and lower energy
content per litre means a lower per-litre energy tax. In 2026: the CO2 tax base
fell from €62.0 to **€56.8/t on 1 Jan 2026** (first stage of a two-step cut,
HE 132/2025), and the **stockpile fee was reformed to an energy-content basis
on 1 Apr 2026** — hence the current tax table "1 A" runs 1.4.2026–31.12.2026.

### 2.2 Interaction with the mandate (cross-reference)

Finland's **jakeluvelvoite** (distribution obligation, Act 446/2007) is the
volume driver; taxation is complementary, not conditional. Unlike Sweden and
(historically) several other member states, the reduced CO2 rates for R/T fuels
are **not** limited to volumes beyond the obligation — the energy-tax model is
considered a general measure. Obligation levels and trajectories: see the
mandates researcher's section.
