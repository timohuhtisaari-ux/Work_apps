# United States — State Biofuel Blend Mandates & Headline Tax Incentives (Non-EU)

- **Jurisdiction:** United States (50 states) — federal + state instruments, described on their own terms
- **Scope:** HEADLINE level only. State *statutory blend content mandates* (biodiesel/ethanol) for the on-road fuel supply, plus the most material state tax incentives. Blend-mandate levels only; sustainability certification and the federal RFS volume mechanics belong to other agents (cross-referenced, not duplicated).
- **Report date:** 2026-07-13
- **Fetches performed:** 2026-07-15 (live; access dates in source log)
- **Researched by:** fuel-taxation researcher (US map task)
- **Corroboration pass:** primary (AFDC/state) + one independent source per key fact; see confidence column

## Framing note (read first)

The United States has **no federal blend mandate**. The federal instrument is the **Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS)**, a national *volume obligation* on refiners/importers administered by EPA — it is a blending *quota*, not a per-gallon content mandate, and it is out of scope here (other agent). "No state mandate" below therefore means "federal RFS only."

State blend mandates fall into four practical buckets, and the critical finding is that **most are trigger-based (contingent on in-state production capacity), suspended, repealed, fleet-only, or apply to heating oil rather than motor fuel.** Only a handful are live, unconditional motor-fuel content mandates. Several instruments commonly cited as "state mandates" (New Mexico B5, Louisiana 2%, Montana E10, Hawaii E10, Massachusetts Advanced Biofuels) are **not in force in 2026.**

Also note: California, Oregon, Washington and (from 2026) New Mexico run **Clean Fuel Standard / LCFS carbon-intensity programs**. Those are *not* blend mandates and are cross-referenced only.

## 3. Headline state biofuel tax incentives (most material only — not exhaustive)

- **Iowa** — the flagship retailer-credit state. **E15 Plus Gasoline Promotion Tax Credit** (~$0.09/gal) **set to be repealed Jan 2026**; **Biodiesel Blended Fuel Retailer Tax Credit** and **E85 credit** run to **Jan 2028**; biodiesel **producer** credit $0.04/gal (cap 25 M gal) to 2028. Separately, from **Jan 1 2026 retail stations must offer E15** from at least one dispenser. Admin: Iowa Dept of Revenue. Confidence: CONFIRMED. [S21, S22]
- **Illinois** — incentive delivered through **sales/use-tax treatment tied to blend level** (not a mandate). Biodiesel: exempt blend threshold **rose to B20 effective April 1 2026** (from B17), dropping to **B10 in winter (Dec 1–Mar 31)**; program runs to 2032. Ethanol: through 2028, sales tax applies to only 90% of E15 proceeds and 80% of E20–E50. Admin: Illinois Dept of Revenue. Confidence: CONFIRMED. [S23, S24]
- **Missouri** — refundable **biodiesel retailer tax credit**: $0.02/gal (B5–B10), $0.05/gal (B11–B20). Mo. Rev. Stat. § 135.775. Confidence: PRIMARY-ONLY (AFDC). [S13]
- **Minnesota** — E15 retailer **infrastructure grants** (up to ~$199k) rather than a per-gallon credit; supports the E15 push behind the E10 mandate. Confidence: PRIMARY-ONLY (AFDC). [S14]
- **Rhode Island** — RI-produced **biodiesel exempt from the $0.34/gal state motor-fuel tax**. Confidence: PRIMARY-ONLY (AFDC). [S10]

(Cross-reference: LCFS/Clean Fuel credit markets in CA, OR, WA and NM-from-2026 are separate carbon-intensity instruments, not tax incentives — out of scope.)

## 4. States with NO state blend mandate — federal RFS only (for the map)

**Affirmative state motor-fuel blend mandates actually IN FORCE in 2026 (color these on the map):**
- **Biodiesel:** Minnesota (B20/B5), Oregon (B5), Washington (B2), Pennsylvania (B2).
- **Ethanol:** Minnesota (E10), Missouri (E10), Oregon (E10), Washington (2%).
- **Heating oil only (distinct category):** Rhode Island (Bioheat, → B50/2030).

**Commonly-cited but NOT a live road-fuel mandate in 2026 (do NOT shade as active):**

- **Louisiana** — 2% biodiesel + 2% ethanol triggers dormant/unenforced.
- **Montana** — E10 **repealed** (2017).
- **Hawaii** — E10 **repealed** (2015).

- **Pennsylvania higher tiers & cellulosic-E10** — not triggered.

## 5. Conflicts, gaps & flags

- **Rhode Island intermediate steps (FLAG):** RI OER page shows B5 now → B50 by 2030 (with a B10 step by 2023); Clean Fuels Alliance material cites a **B20 step reached July 1 2025**. The B50/2030 endpoint and in-force status are consistent; the intermediate-year schedule (B10 vs B20 timing) should be pinned to the current DEM regulation text before publishing exact interim blends. Heating-oil scope is certain.
- **Minnesota 2026 session (FLAG):** one AFDC search snippet referenced the biodiesel statute being "affected by law enacted during the 2026 Regular Session," but the live AFDC/MDA text still shows the B20/B5/B10 schedule unchanged. Treated as **no confirmed change**; re-check Minn. Stat. 239.77 for any 2026 amendment before final.
- **Missouri biodiesel (RESOLVED):** initial secondary text described a staged B5→B10→B20 "Missouri Made Fuels Act" as if enacted; AFDC primary confirms **no statewide biodiesel mandate exists** — those were unenacted bills. Logged as PROPOSED/REPEALED honestly.
- **Washington two instruments (note):** distinguish RCW 19.112 (statewide B2/2%-ethanol *supply* mandate, in force) from RCW 43.19.642 (state-*agency* B20/ferry B5 procurement rule) — they are different laws; the map should use the 19.112 supply mandate.
- **New Mexico / Louisiana:** classified PRIMARY-ONLY (AFDC only) for the "suspended/dormant" status; a state-agency confirmation would upgrade confidence.

## 5-line summary

1. Only five states run live on-road blend content mandates: **Minnesota (B20/B5 + E10), Oregon (B5 + E10), Washington (B2 + 2% ethanol), Pennsylvania (B2 only), and Missouri (E10 only)** — Minnesota's B20 is the strongest.
2. Several "mandates" are NOT in force in 2026: **New Mexico B5 (suspended), Louisiana 2% (dormant), Montana E10 (repealed 2017), Hawaii E10 (repealed 2015), Massachusetts Advanced Biofuels (suspended), and Pennsylvania's B5/B10/B20 + cellulosic-E10 tiers (production triggers unmet).**
3. **Rhode Island** is a mandate but for **heating oil (Bioheat, → B50 by 2030)**, not motor fuel — map it as a separate category; **Missouri's statewide biodiesel mandate was only proposed, never enacted** (fleet B20 only).
4. Headline incentives: **Iowa** retailer credits (E15 credit repealed Jan 2026; biodiesel/E85 to 2028; E15-offer rule from 2026) and **Illinois** blend-linked sales-tax exemption (biodiesel threshold rises to B20 on 1 Apr 2026); Missouri, Minnesota (E15 grants) and Rhode Island add smaller measures.
5. **All other ~42 states have no state blend mandate — federal RFS (EPA volume obligation) only**; flags to resolve before publishing: RI interim-year blend steps, any Minnesota 2026-session amendment, and upgrading NM/LA "suspended/dormant" status from PRIMARY-ONLY.
