# Lithuania — Biofuel Regulation Compliance Report

- **Country:** Lithuania (LT)
- **Report date:** 2026-07-04
- **Researched by:** mandates/tax/sustainability researchers + corroborator
- **Corroboration pass:** done

## Executive summary

Lithuania imposes an **energy-share (RES-in-transport) supplier obligation** in percentage points under the **Law on Alternative Fuels (Alternatyviųjų degalų įstatymas, XIV-196)**: the 2026 level is **≥ 9.8 pp** overall, of which **≥ 1.4 pp** must be advanced biofuels and/or RFNBO, on a legislated trajectory rising to **16.8 % / 3.5 %** by 2030. Obligated parties are petrol/diesel suppliers plus transport natural-gas suppliers; compliance is tracked through **DAEI/VIET renewable-fuel accounting units** operated by **BALTPOOL**, with the Lithuanian Energy Agency (ENA) maintaining the feedstock list and multiple counting (2×) applied from 1 January 2026. On tax, petrol excise is EUR 562/1000 L and diesel EUR 553.6/1000 L, each with an **embedded CO2 (variable) component** (petrol 96, diesel 53.6) rising to 2030; a **biogas/biomethane excise exemption** applies from 1 July 2025 but is treated as state aid pending EC clearance, and liquid biofuels get no separate cut after the 2024 fossil-relief repeal. RED III was **transposed early** (June 2025 package), though Lithuania remains on the Commission's 24 July 2025 formal-notice list. A supplier's top obligations are therefore: register and submit data to the DAEI system (BALTPOOL), hold EU-recognised voluntary-scheme certification for each consignment, and meet the annual RES-T obligation via accounting units.

## 1. Biofuel mandates

**Headline:** Lithuania's **Law on Alternative Fuels (Alternatyviųjų degalų įstatymas, XIV-196, adopted 23 March 2021)** sets a rising RES-in-transport supplier obligation. The **current consolidated version is valid from 1 July 2026**; multiple counting for the obligation applies from 1 January 2026.

> **Corroborator note (2026-07-04):** the 2026 figures (**9.8 pp overall / 1.4 pp advanced+RFNBO**) are confirmed from a direct quote of the e-seimas primary text ("From 2026, fuel and transport-sector natural-gas suppliers … at least 9.8 percentage points … of which at least 1.4 percentage points advanced biofuels and/or RFNBO"). The 2030 endpoint (**16.8 % / 3.5 %**) and the **€1,000–€1,000,000** fine are corroborated on the **PwC Legal** briefing [4] (Tier-2). Note: the penalty rests on a *secondary* source only — no primary penalty-article read was achieved — so it is downgraded to UNCORROBORATED.

### 1.2 Scheme mechanics

- **Obligated party:** **fuel suppliers** placing petrol/diesel on the market, and **transport natural-gas suppliers** (Art. 16(4),(9)). Compliance is tracked through **renewable-energy fuel accounting units (DAEI/VIET units)**. [1]
- **Accounting-units administrator:** **BALTPOOL UAB** operates the accounting-units system. [5]
- **Lithuanian Energy Agency (Lietuvos energetikos agentūra / ENA):** evaluates feedstocks for inclusion on the advanced-biofuel/RFNBO **feedstock list**, publishing the assessment **annually by 1 June** per the Art. 21 methodology; supports the accounting/registry framework. [1][5]
- **Regulator/enforcement:** fines are administered under the alternative-fuels enforcement regime (State Energy Regulatory Council / VERT context). [4]

The compliance obligation now starts **1 Jan 2028** — the original 2027 start was **postponed by one year** in the early-2026 climate package (adopted alongside Regulation (EU) 2026/667, the 2040 climate-target amendment). **First allowance surrender 2029** (for 2028 emissions); auctioning from 2027. **Interaction with the mandate:** biofuel uptake is driven by the RES-T obligation under the Law on Alternative Fuels (Section 1). On the tax side, the notable relief is the **biogas/biomethane excise exemption** (fixed + CO2 parts), a state-aid measure; liquid biofuels do not appear to carry a separate reduced excise following the 2024 reform.

**National act implementing the Energy Taxation Directive (2003/96/EC):** the **Law on Excise Duties (*Akcizų įstatymas*), Art. 21** — sets motor-fuel excise (now split into a fixed part and a CO2 variable part) and implements Directive 2003/96/EC. Consolidated text on e-seimas / TAR (e-seimas.lrs.lt). The Dec 2024 amending act introduced the CO2 component trajectory and the biogas exemption. **State-aid:** the biogas/biomethane excise exemption is treated by the Lithuanian Competition Council as state aid requiring **European Commission approval** — the notified decision reference remains a gap.

## 4. Compliance checklist for a fuel supplier

1. **Register in the DAEI accounting-units system (BALTPOOL)** and submit fuel data (renewable share, type, origin, energy value) — mandatory for excise-liable fuel suppliers and transport natural-gas suppliers (data mandatory since 1 Jan 2022). Authority: **BALTPOOL** (operator) / **VERT** (oversight).
2. **Meet the 2026 RES-T obligation** of **≥ 9.8 pp** overall, of which **≥ 1.4 pp** advanced biofuels + RFNBO, applying multiple counting (2×) from 1 Jan 2026; respect the UCO/animal-fat cap (≤ 1.7 %) and crop cap (2020 share + 1 pp). Authority: **VERT / Ministry of Energy**.
3. **Hold EU-recognised voluntary-scheme certification** (ISCC EU / REDcert / 2BSvs, etc.) for each liquid biofuel consignment (AIEĮ Art. 38). Authority: **Ministry of Energy**.
4. **Provide a per-consignment proof-of-sustainability document** stating the DAEI quantity in MJ, and maintain a mass balance per IR 2022/996. Authority: **BALTPOOL / VERT**.
5. **Meet the applicable GHG-saving thresholds** (50/60/65 %; RFNBO 70 %). Authority: **Ministry of Energy / VERT**.
6. **Register and record transactions in the EU Union Database (UDB)** for transport-fuel operators, alongside the DAEI system. Authority: **EU / national administrator**.
7. **(Solid-biomass suppliers only)** Certify under **RED BP** (BALTPOOL) or an accepted scheme (FSC/PEFC/SURE/SBP/KZR INiG) to satisfy biomass-fuel sustainability. Authority: **BALTPOOL**.
8. **Budget for enforcement penalties** — indicatively **€1,000–€1,000,000** per obligation breach (UNCORROBORATED, secondary source only). Authority: **VERT**.
9. **Note ETS2 exposure:** the fuel-supplier surrender obligation begins **1 Jan 2028**, first surrender **2029**. Authority: EU (Dir. (EU) 2023/959).

## 5. Conflicts and gaps

**Carried-forward flags (must remain prominent):**
- **RED III transposed early** (June 2025 package; LT among the first MS to transpose RFNBO mandates and multiple counting from 1 Jan 2026).
- **Supplier obligation ≥ 9.8 pp (2026)** rising to **16.8 % / 3.5 % (advanced+RFNBO) by 2030** — CONFIRMED (e-seimas primary + PwC).
- **DAEI accounting-units system / BALTPOOL** administers compliance; ENA runs the feedstock list (annual assessment by 1 June).
- **Biogas/biomethane excise exemption (from 1 July 2025) is pending EC state-aid clearance** — no formal EC decision reference located (AF Observatory lists biogas at 0 %).
- **The €1,000–€1,000,000 penalty is UNCORROBORATED** — rests on the PwC Legal secondary briefing only; no primary penalty article was read.
- **ETS2** established by Directive (EU) 2023/959; obligation start **postponed to 1 Jan 2028** (first surrender 2029) in the early-2026 climate package.
