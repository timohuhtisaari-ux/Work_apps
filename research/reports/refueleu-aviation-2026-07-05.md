# ReFuelEU Aviation — Compliance Report

- **Instrument:** ReFuelEU Aviation — Regulation (EU) 2023/2405 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 18 October 2023 on ensuring a level playing field for sustainable air transport
- **Legal form:** directly applicable EU Regulation (no national transposition; Member States only designate a competent authority (Art. 11) and impose penalties (Art. 12); EASA holds Union-level reporting, data-publication and labelling functions (Arts. 13–14))
- **Report date:** 2026-07-05
- **Researched by:** obligation + compliance researchers + corroborator
- **Corroboration pass:** done (COMPLETED 2026-07-05 by source-corroborator)

## Executive summary

ReFuelEU Aviation sets a rising **sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) supply mandate** on aviation fuel suppliers and an **anti-tankering uplift obligation** on aircraft operators at **Union airports** (> 800 000 passengers or > 100 000 tonnes freight), applying generally from **1 January 2024** with the core obligations from **1 January 2025**. Suppliers must ensure the fuel made available contains a minimum SAF share rising from **2% (2025) → 6% (2030) → 20% (2035) → 34% (2040) → 42% (2045) → 70% (2050)**, with a synthetic (e-SAF/RFNBO) sub-quota from 1.2% (averaged 2030–2031) to **35% (2050)**; aircraft operators must uplift **≥ 90%** of their yearly required fuel at each Union airport. Eligible SAF is limited to synthetic fuels, advanced and Annex IX biofuels and recycled carbon fuels certified under the RED apparatus — **food/feed-crop, palm/soy and PFAD pathways earn zero mandate credit** (Art. 4). Non-compliance draws administrative fines of **at least 2× the relevant per-tonne price difference × the shortfall** (Art. 12), and a fine does not release a supplier from making up the shortfall the following period. Compliance runs on annual reporting — aircraft operators file a verified Annex II report by **31 March** to the competent authority and EASA, and suppliers report into the **Union Database by 14 February** — with ReFuelEU operating alongside (not within) the EU ETS for aviation and CORSIA.

## 2. Core obligation & trajectory

**Supplier obligation (Art. 4(1)):** aviation fuel suppliers shall ensure that **all aviation fuel made available to aircraft operators at each Union airport contains the minimum shares of SAF** set out in **Annex I**. Annex I also fixes a sub-quota of **synthetic aviation fuel** (e-SAF / RFNBO-derived aviation fuel).

| SAF minimum share | **6 %** | Annex I | 2030-01-01 | CONFIRMED | S1, S2, S3, S4 |
| SAF minimum share | **20 %** | Annex I | 2035-01-01 | CONFIRMED | S1, S2, V1, V2 |
| SAF minimum share | **34 %** | Annex I | 2040-01-01 | CONFIRMED | S1, S2, V1, V2, V3 |

Note on the averaging rule: for 2030–2031 and 2032–2034 the synthetic sub-quota is expressed as a **period average** with an annual floor, so a supplier may under-supply e-SAF in the first year of the window provided the two/three-year average and the annual minimum floor are met. This is the "2030–2031 averaging" flagged — verified in Annex I and independently corroborated (V3).

**Anti-tankering / uplift obligation (Art. 5) — on aircraft operators, not suppliers:**

| Item | Value | Legal basis | Effective | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum uplift | The **yearly quantity of aviation fuel uplifted by an aircraft operator at a given Union airport shall be ≥ 90 % of the yearly aviation fuel required** at that airport | Art. 5(1) | 2025-01-01 | CONFIRMED | S1, S2 |

Purpose: prevents "fuel tankering" (carrying excess cheaper fuel loaded elsewhere to avoid uplifting SAF-blended fuel at Union airports). The 90 % floor is measured per operator per Union airport per reporting year.

| Item | Value | Legal basis | Effective | Confidence | Sources |

Environmental labelling scheme for aircraft operators is Art. 14.

## 4. Penalties & economic instruments

All fines sit in **Article 12 ("Enforcement")** — imposed by Member State competent authorities (Art. 11). Fines must be "proportionate and dissuasive" and meet the statutory floors below. (Note: an earlier task brief referenced "Articles 12–13"; in the consolidated text the **fines are all in Art. 12**, while **Art. 13** is "Data collection and publication." Corroborator confirmed — Art. 12 = penalties, Art. 13 = data publication.)

| Item | Value / formula | Legal basis | Effective | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SAF-shortfall fine (suppliers)** | ≥ **2×** [ (yearly average price of **conventional aviation fuel** − price of **SAF**) **per tonne** ] × (quantity of aviation fuel not complying with the minimum SAF share) | Art. 12(4) | 2025-01-01 | CONFIRMED | S1, S3 |
| **Synthetic-shortfall fine (suppliers)** | ≥ **2×** [ (yearly average price of **synthetic aviation fuel** − price of **conventional aviation fuel**) **per tonne** ] × (quantity not complying with the synthetic minimum/average share) | Art. 12(5) | 2030-01-01 | CONFIRMED | S1, S3 |
| **Anti-tankering fine (aircraft operators)** | ≥ **2×** [ yearly average price of aviation fuel **per tonne** ] × (total yearly **non-tanked** quantity, i.e. the shortfall below the 90 % uplift) | Art. 12(2) | 2025-01-01 | CONFIRMED | S1 |
| **Make-up obligation (does not exempt fine)** | Payment of a fine does **not release** the supplier from the Art. 12(8) obligation to supply the shortfall quantity in the next reporting period | Art. 12(8) | 2025-01-01 | CONFIRMED | S1 |
| **Price-methodology transparency** | When imposing a fine, the competent authority must **explain the methodology applied for determining the price**; the Agency publishes price information/trends annually (Art. 13) | Art. 12(7), Art. 13 | 2025-01-01 | PRIMARY-ONLY | S1 |
| Illustrative magnitude (Tier-2, not legal) | Using 2024 EASA reference prices (~€700/t jet fuel, ~€2,000/t biofuel-SAF, ~€7,700/t e-kerosene): SAF-shortfall fine ≈ **€2,700/t**; e-SAF-shortfall fine ≈ **€14,000/t** | (illustration) | — | UNCORROBORATED (single Tier-2) | S3 |

**EU ETS interaction:** ReFuelEU Aviation is a supply/uplift mandate and operates **alongside** the EU Emissions Trading System for aviation and CORSIA (separate instruments); the Regulation itself does not set an ETS price. The aviation/maritime ETS and ReFuelEU are distinct from **ETS2** (road/buildings); do not conflate.

## 5. Fuel eligibility & sustainability criteria

### 5.1 What counts as "SAF" (the eligible-fuel universe)

**Definition — Article 3(7):** "sustainable aviation fuels" or "SAF" means aviation fuels that are **either (a) synthetic aviation fuels, (b) aviation biofuels, or (c) recycled carbon aviation fuels.** `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex 32023R2405 (S1); corroborated DG MOVE (S4), EASA (S5)).

| Eligible pathway | Legal basis | Definition / condition | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Synthetic aviation fuels** (e-fuels) | Art. 3(12) | Renewable fuels of non-biological origin (**RFNBOs**) as defined in RED (Dir (EU) 2018/2001) that meet the lifecycle emissions-savings threshold of **RED Art. 29a(1)** and are certified under **RED Art. 30**. | `CONFIRMED` |
| **Aviation biofuels — advanced** | Art. 3(8)(a) | Advanced biofuels as defined in RED (feedstock in **Annex IX Part A**). | `CONFIRMED` |
| **Aviation biofuels — Annex IX Part B** | Art. 3(8)(b) | Biofuels produced from feedstock listed in **Annex IX Part B** of RED (e.g. used cooking oil, certain animal fats). | `CONFIRMED` |
| **Aviation biofuels — other** | Art. 3(8)(c) | Other biofuels **complying with the sustainability and lifecycle GHG-emissions-savings criteria of RED Art. 29** and certified under **RED Art. 30**, **excluding food/feed-crop-based biofuels**. | `CONFIRMED` |
| **Recycled carbon aviation fuels (RCFs)** | Art. 3(9) | Recycled carbon fuels meeting the lifecycle emissions-savings threshold of **RED Art. 29a(2)** and certified under **RED Art. 30**. | `CONFIRMED` |

Also relevant: Art. 3(6) "aviation fuel" = drop-in fuel manufactured for direct use by aircraft; Art. 3(11) "lifecycle emissions" computed via the methodologies of RED Art. 28(5) or Art. 31(5). `PRIMARY-ONLY` (S1).

### 5.2 THE CROP EXCLUSION (key fact)

**Article 4 (final paragraph, cited in source as Art. 4(5)):** SAF produced from the following feedstocks is **EXCLUDED from counting toward the minimum SAF shares** in Annex I:

- **food and feed crops** (as defined in RED Art. 2(2)(40));
- **intermediate crops**;
- **palm fatty acid distillate (PFAD)**;
- **palm- and soy-derived materials**;
- **soap stock and its derivatives.**

**Sole carve-out:** the exclusion does **not** apply to feedstocks that are **listed in Annex IX to RED**, under the conditions set out in that Annex. In practice this means food/feed-crop and palm/soy-pathway fuels earn **zero credit** toward the mandate even if physically blended; only waste/residue (Annex IX) and synthetic/RCF pathways count. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex 32023R2405 Art. 4 (S1); corroborated DG MOVE (S4)). Rationale: Recital 23 (indirect land-use change / biodiversity loss).

Dynamic link: **Recital 22** — changes to the RED Annex IX feedstock list flow through automatically to the ReFuelEU list of eligible fuels (no separate ReFuelEU amendment needed). `PRIMARY-ONLY`.

> Cross-check: this exclusion is stricter/parallel to the RED 7% crop cap — ReFuelEU does not merely cap crop fuels, it gives them **no eligibility** for the aviation SAF share.

### 5.3 GHG emissions-saving thresholds & certification

- **Biofuels:** must meet the sustainability and GHG-emissions-saving criteria of **RED Art. 29** (well-to-wake / lifecycle). `CONFIRMED`.
- **Synthetic fuels (RFNBOs) and recycled carbon fuels:** must meet the **RED Art. 29a** thresholds. The RFNBO/RCF minimum GHG saving is **≥ 70%** versus the fossil comparator of **94 gCO₂eq/MJ** (i.e. ≤ 28.2 gCO₂eq/MJ), per **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1185** (10 Feb 2023), which sets the RCF threshold and the RFNBO/RCF savings methodology. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex 32023R1185 (S6) + corroborating analyses).
- **Certification / chain of custody:** eligibility is anchored to **RED Art. 30** — i.e. compliance is demonstrated through the **EU-recognised voluntary schemes** (ISCC EU, REDcert-EU, 2BSvs, etc.) or a national scheme, using the RED **mass-balance** chain-of-custody. ReFuelEU does **not** create a separate SAF sustainability certification; it re-uses the RED apparatus. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 3(8)(c)/3(9)/3(12) all reference "certified in compliance with Article 30" of RED; corroborated DG ENER voluntary-schemes framework).

## 6. Monitoring, reporting, verification & compliance

### 6.1 Aircraft operators' annual report — Article 8

- **Who:** aircraft operators within scope (cross-ref §1 / Art. 3(4)).
- **Deadline:** **by 31 March of each reporting year, first time in 2025.** `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 8 (S1); corroborated EASA MRV page (S2)).
- **To whom:** the **competent authority** and **EASA (the Agency)**.
- **Format:** template in **Annex II**.
- **Content (per Union airport):** total aviation fuel uplifted (t); yearly aviation fuel required (t); yearly non-tanked quantity; quantity tanked for safety-compliance reasons; total SAF purchased incl. conversion process, feedstock origin and lifecycle emissions; number and hours of flights operated. `PRIMARY-ONLY` (EUR-Lex Annex II detail).
- **Verification:** the report must be **verified by an independent verifier** in accordance with **Articles 14 and 15 of the EU ETS Directive 2003/87/EC** (and its MRV/accreditation implementing rules). `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 8 (S1); corroborated EASA "Manual for Aircraft Operators and Verification Bodies" (S5)).

### 6.2 Aviation fuel suppliers' annual report — Article 10

- **Who:** aviation fuel suppliers.
- **Deadline:** **by 14 February of each reporting year, first time in 2025.** `CONFIRMED` — corroborated independently by the DG MOVE **"Guidance Document: Reporting of Aviation Fuel Suppliers under Article 10 RFEUA"** (V7), which states reports are submitted "by 14 February of each reporting year … using Reporting Template B" via the Union Database; also IATA ReFuelEU Handbook and Normec Verifavia guidance (V9).
- **Where:** reported into the **Union Database** referred to in **RED Art. 31a** (the UDB), not to EASA directly. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 10 (S1); corroborated DG MOVE Article 10 Guidance (V7)).
- **Content (per Union airport):** total aviation fuel supplied (t); SAF supplied by type incl. conversion process, feedstock origin and lifecycle emissions; **aromatics and naphthalenes** (% volume) and **sulphur** (% mass); energy content of fuels supplied. `PRIMARY-ONLY`.

### 6.3 The Union Database (UDB) — traceability backbone

- Suppliers enter fuel/SAF data into the **UDB (RED Art. 31a)**; this ties SAF traceability to the same chain-of-custody database used for all RED biofuels/RFNBOs. `CONFIRMED`.
- **Member States must ensure** the information entered by suppliers into the UDB is **accurate, verified and audited** before it is relied upon. `PRIMARY-ONLY` (EUR-Lex).
- **EASA and competent authorities** are granted access; EASA uses UDB data **only once it has been verified at Member-State level.** `PRIMARY-ONLY`.
- **UDB onboarding note:** because ReFuelEU supplier reporting is executed *through* the UDB, aviation fuel suppliers placing fuel on the EU market must be onboarded to the UDB (operated for the Commission by DG ENER) — same platform as road-transport biofuel operators. `PRIMARY-ONLY`.

### 6.4 Competent authorities & enforcement — Articles 11–12

- **Article 11 "Competent authority":** each **Member State designates** the competent authority/authorities responsible for enforcing the Regulation. Attribution rules: aircraft operators → the administering Member State per Commission Reg (EC) No 748/2009 / EU ETS Art. 18a; airports → territorial; fuel suppliers → Member State of the supplier's principal place of business. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex, two fetches (S1)).
- **Article 12 "Enforcement":** Member States lay down **penalties/administrative fines**. Minimum fine formulas: `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 12 (S1); corroborated §4 + DG MOVE (S4)):
  - **Art. 5 (uplift/anti-tankering) breach:** fine **≥ 2×** (yearly average aviation-fuel price per tonne × total yearly non-tanked quantity).
  - **Art. 4 (SAF minimum share) breach:** fine **≥ 2×** (difference between yearly average price of conventional aviation fuel and SAF per tonne × the tonnage of the SAF shortfall).
  - **Art. 4 (synthetic-SAF sub-share) breach:** fine **≥ 2×** (difference between yearly average price of synthetic aviation fuel and conventional aviation fuel per tonne × the synthetic shortfall).
  - A fine **does not release** the supplier from the obligation to make up the **shortfall quantity** — it must be supplied in the following reporting period **in addition** to that period's obligation.

### 6.5 EASA technical report & Union labelling scheme — Articles 13–14

- **Article 13 "Data collection and publication":** EASA **publishes a technical report every year** and forwards it to the **European Parliament and the Council**. Content includes aggregated Union- and airport-level SAF purchased/supplied, market/price/production trends, per-operator and per-supplier compliance status, aromatics/naphthalenes/sulphur analysis, and hydrogen/electricity infrastructure progress. This is the **aggregated Union-level SAF reporting** function. `CONFIRMED` (EUR-Lex Art. 13 (S1); corroborated EASA "Annual Technical Report" page (S3)).
- **Article 14 "Environmental labelling scheme":** establishes a **voluntary** Union environmental labelling scheme measuring the environmental performance of flights (the "**Flight Emissions Label**", FEL); operators **apply to EASA**, which issues an expected-CO₂ figure using a standardised methodology. Operationalised by **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/3170** (adopted 18 Dec 2024). **`CONFLICT` RESOLVED → the Art. 14 scheme is VOLUNTARY.** `CONFIRMED`: Art. 14(1) itself reads "a voluntary environmental labelling scheme"; EASA states aircraft operators (EU and non-EU) "can **voluntarily** participate" and, when they do, must request FEL for all their flights departing from Union airports; the official FEL FAQ (flightemissions.eu) confirms voluntary participation. The earlier "mandatory" wording traced to a paraphrase on the DG MOVE overview page and is not supported by the legal text — treat "voluntary" as correct.

## 6a. Cross-border reporting allocation (operators/suppliers active in several Member States)

ReFuelEU has **two obligated parties**, each with its own single-point-of-contact
allocation — neither reports separately to every State it serves.

- **Aircraft operators → one administering Member State.** Determined via
  Commission Reg. (EC) 748/2009 / Art. 18a of the EU-ETS Directive (the Member
  State that granted the operating licence; otherwise the State with the greatest
  attributed aviation emissions), applied by **Art. 11(5)**. The operator files
  **one** annual Annex II report **by 31 March** to that State's competent
  authority **and EASA** (Art. 8) — not to each destination country. `CONFIRMED`.
- **Fuel suppliers → one enforcing competent authority = the Member State of the
  supplier's principal place of business** (Art. 11(7); "principal place of
  business" defined in Art. 3(21); fallback under Art. 11(8) = the State where it
  supplied most aviation fuel in 2023 / its first Union year). This holds **even
  when the supplier serves airports in several Member States**. `CONFIRMED`.
- **But supplier reporting stays per-airport:** the supplier reports the fuel and
  SAF volumes at **each Union airport** as separate lines **into the Union
  Database by 14 February** (Art. 10). So the per-airport granularity is a
  **data** attribute; **enforcement jurisdiction is single-State**. Until 31 Dec
  2034 the SAF share may be met as a weighted average across all the supplier's
  Union airports (Art. 15).
- **EASA** aggregates and publishes (annual technical report, digital tool,
  voluntary Flight Emissions Label) but **does not enforce** — Member-State
  competent authorities (Art. 11(1)) impose the Art. 12 fines.

*Worked examples:* a supplier headquartered in Belgium that fuels airports in
BE/DE/PL is enforced **only by Belgium**, yet reports each airport's volumes into
the UDB. A French-licensed aircraft operator flying to 10 States files **one**
Annex II report to **France** + EASA.

Full detail, the two-regime comparison, and the source log are in the companion
report `cross-border-reporting-allocation-2026-07-05.md`.

## 7. Compliance checklist for an obligated operator

Two obligated parties. Each step: action → deadline → authority/system.

**A. For an aviation fuel supplier (SAF-share obligation, Art. 4):**

1. **Verify SAF eligibility.** Ensure all SAF claimed is from an **eligible pathway** (synthetic/RFNBO, Annex IX-A advanced, Annex IX-B, other non-crop biofuel, or RCF) and is **RED Art. 30-certified** under a recognised voluntary/national scheme. Confirm **none** of the claimed SAF derives from food/feed crops, intermediate crops, PFAD, palm/soy materials or soap stock (Art. 4) — such volumes earn **zero** mandate credit. → *ongoing; recognised voluntary scheme PoS / UDB record.* `CONFIRMED`
2. **Confirm GHG thresholds.** Synthetic/RCF volumes meet the **RED Art. 29a ≥ 70%** GHG-saving threshold (Del. Reg. (EU) 2023/1185 methodology); biofuels meet **RED Art. 29**. → *before each consignment; certification scheme.* `CONFIRMED`
3. **Supply the required SAF share** at each Union airport per Annex I (2% from 2025, rising), using the transitional weighted-average-across-airports flexibility (Art. 15) available until 31 Dec 2034; from 1 Jan 2035 the share applies per airport. → *each reporting year; competent authority (principal place of business MS).* `CONFIRMED`
4. **Report into the Union Database (RED Art. 31a) by 14 February** each year (first: 2025), per-airport, incl. conversion process, feedstock origin, lifecycle emissions, aromatics/naphthalenes/sulphur, energy content (Art. 10, Reporting Template B). → *UDB; oversight: competent authority.* `CONFIRMED`
5. **Make good any SAF/synthetic shortfall** in the following reporting period **in addition** to that period's obligation; a fine does not discharge the shortfall (Art. 12(8)). → *following reporting period; competent authority.* `CONFIRMED`
6. **Settle any administrative fine** (≥ 2× the relevant per-tonne price difference × shortfall) imposed by the competent authority (Art. 12(4)–(5)). → *on assessment; competent authority.* `CONFIRMED`

**B. For an aircraft operator (uplift/anti-tankering obligation, Art. 5):**

7. **Uplift ≥ 90%** of the yearly aviation fuel required at each Union airport (Art. 5), avoiding fuel tankering; retain records of fuel uplifted, required fuel, non-tanked and safety-tanked quantities, and SAF purchased per airport (Annex II data). An EASA monitoring-plan template / Fuel Monitoring Tool is available (optional). → *ongoing; own records / EASA tool.* `CONFIRMED`
8. **Have the annual report verified by an independent verifier** accredited per **Dir 2003/87/EC Arts. 14–15** (EU ETS MRV rules) before submission (Art. 8). → *before 31 March; accredited verifier.* `CONFIRMED`
9. **Submit the verified Annex II report by 31 March** each year (first: 2025) to the **competent authority and EASA** (via the EASA Sustainability Portal) (Art. 8). → *31 March; competent authority + EASA.* `CONFIRMED`
10. **Settle any anti-tankering fine** (≥ 2× fuel price/t × non-tanked quantity) imposed by the competent authority (Art. 12(2)). → *on assessment; competent authority.* `CONFIRMED`
11. *(Optional)* Apply to EASA for the **Flight Emissions Label** under the Art. 14 **voluntary** scheme (Impl. Reg. (EU) 2024/3170). → *optional; EASA.* `CONFIRMED`

### 4-line summary

1. **Obligation & trajectory:** directly-applicable Regulation (EU) 2023/2405 binds **aviation fuel suppliers** (SAF share 2% 2025 → 6% 2030 → 20% 2035 → 34% 2040 → 42% 2045 → 70% 2050, with an e-SAF sub-quota 1.2% avg 2030–2031 → 35% 2050) and **aircraft operators** (≥ 90% uplift/anti-tankering) at Union airports; core obligations from 1 Jan 2025.
2. **Penalty:** administrative fines ≥ 2× the relevant per-tonne price difference (conventional vs SAF / vs synthetic) × the shortfall for suppliers, and ≥ 2× fuel price × non-tanked quantity for operators (Art. 12); a fine does not discharge the make-up obligation.
3. **Key compliance deadline:** aircraft operators file a verified Annex II report by **31 March** to the competent authority + EASA; fuel suppliers report into the Union Database by **14 February** (both first in 2025).
4. **Top flag:** illustrative penalty magnitudes are UNCORROBORATED (single Tier-2), and the Annex II operator data-fields are PRIMARY-ONLY (single EUR-Lex fetch).
