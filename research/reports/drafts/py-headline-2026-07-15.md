# Paraguay: Biofuel Mandates—Headline Report (2026-07-15)

## 1. Instrument Type & 2026 Headline Obligation

**Primary Instruments:** 
1. **Ethanol blending mandate** via Decreto 3.241/2024 and implementing Laws 7.357/2024 & 7.391/2024 (Ministry of Industry and Commerce).
2. **Biodiesel blending mandate** via Ley 7.635/2026 (modifying prior Ley 6.389/2019).

**2026 Headline Obligation:**

- **Ethanol (E30):** 30% mandatory minimum blend with gasoline (Gasolina C).
  - Effective: 17 Mar 2025 (Decreto 3.241/2024 implementation).
  - **50% minimum domestic sugarcane requirement** (per decree regulation).
  
- **Biodiesel:** 8%–10% operational range (B8–B10) set by MIC discretion.
  - Legal ceiling: 5%–20% per Ley 7.635/2026.
  - Effective: June 2026 (Ley 7.635 enactment date).
  - **Brazilian supply only** (import restriction via recent MIC regulation).

**Legal Basis:**
- **Ethanol:** Decreto 3.241/2024 (implementation); Laws 7.357/2024 & 7.391/2024 (authorization).
- **Biodiesel:** Ley 7.635/2026; Ley 6.389/2019 (prior regime, modified); MIC Resolución (operational range B8–B10).

**Obligated Parties:** Fuel importers and distributors; blending at distribution/retail level.

## 2. Legislated Trajectory / 2030 Target

**Ethanol Trajectory:**
- **Pre-2025:** E25–E27 (prior blend, variable by regulation).
- **17 Mar 2025 onward:** **E30** (legislated mandatory minimum via Decreto 3.241/2024).
- **No further step-up legislated** for ethanol; E30 is current terminal mandate.
- **Potential expansion:** Laws 7.357/2024 & 7.391/2024 empower government to adjust blend; future increases possible via decree (not pre-legislated).

**Biodiesel Trajectory:**
- **Prior regime (Ley 6.389/2019):** 5% mandatory (Resolución 1.276, Oct 2023).
- **Ley 7.635/2026 (June 2026):** Expands legal range to 5%–**20%** (MIC discretion).
- **Current MIC setting (June 2026):** B8–B10 operational range.
- **Future potential:** MIC may increase within 5–20% range by resolution (no specific step-up pre-legislated, but ceiling now 20% vs. prior 5%).

## 4. Fuel-Tax Treatment of Biofuels

**Tax Status & Structure:**

**Selective Consumption Tax (ISC — Impuesto Selectivo al Consumo):**
- **2023 Decree 8896/2023:** Set ISC tax base at **3,083 Paraguayan guaraní per liter** (~USD 0.42 at 2023 rates) for diesel type 3 (gasoil, where biodiesel is blended).
- **Validity:** Extended through Mar 31 (2023 decree; current renewal status as of 2026 unclear).
- **Biofuel-specific treatment:** No explicit exemption found in biofuel laws; biodiesel appears subject to standard ISC.

**Prior Tax Incentives (2008–2010, mostly expired):**
- **Decree 12.240/2008:** Exempted biofuels from internal fuel tax; exempted flex-fuel cars from import duties; reduced VAT.
- **Decree 3.667/2010 (superseded):** Eliminated the internal fuel tax exemption.
- **Status 2026:** Prior exemptions no longer operative; only selective consumption tax applies.

**Proposed Changes (not yet enacted):**
- Elimination of VAT on biodiesel.
- Reduction of import duties on fuel flex vehicles.

## 5. Sustainability & Certification Regime

**Voluntary Certification Schemes (Not Mandated by Law):**
- **ISCC (International Sustainability & Carbon Certification):** Available via SGS Paraguay; used for export-destined biofuels.
- **No national mandate:** Ley 7.635 and Decreto 3.241 do not prescribe sustainability criteria or GHG-saving thresholds for domestic blending.

**GHG Performance & Sustainability Thresholds:**
- **Domestic market:** Volume-based mandate only (E30, B8–B10); no GHG-intensity requirement in law.
- **Export compliance:** Biofuels destined for EU or other regulated markets use ISCC certification; typical GHG savings for sugarcane ethanol 60–70%, for soy biodiesel 50–65%.

**Feedstock Safeguards:**
- **E30 decree (Decreto 3.241):** Includes quality/technical specifications (e.g., reduction of MTBE to 1% max; RON gasoline reclassification).
- **Sustainability criteria:** Not found in Paraguay's domestic laws; rely on international schemes (ISCC) for export.

**No Land-Use or Sustainability Restrictions:**
- Ley 7.635 does not impose post-2008 land-conversion bans, food-feed restrictions, or high-carbon-land exclusions (unlike EU RED II/III).

## 8. Five-Line Summary

Paraguay mandates **E30 ethanol** (since 17 Mar 2025, world-first minimum; 50% domestic sugarcane required) and **B8–B10 biodiesel** (operational range since June 2026 under Ley 7.635/2026, within legal ceiling 5–20%). No legislated step-up trajectory for ethanol; biodiesel may increase within 5–20% legal band by MIC resolution (2030 target **GAP**; current setting B8–B10 does not indicate pre-legislated 2030 level). No domestic GHG-intensity mandate or advanced-biofuel sub-quota; ISCC/2BSvs certification available for exports only. Biofuels subject to selective consumption tax (ISC, ~3,083 PYG/L per 2023 decree; renewal status 2024–2026 unclear); prior tax exemptions expired (2010). **PRIMARY-ONLY** confidence on E30 and Ley 7.635 (news summaries, no official text fetched); **GAP** on biodiesel 2030 trajectory, MIC discretion timing, and ethanol source-verification enforcement.

## Cross-References & Further Notes

- **World-first E30 milestone:** Paraguay became the first country globally to mandate 30% ethanol minimum (March 2025), exceeding Brazil's prior E27 and future E30 (Aug 2025).
- **No EU RED/ETS2 applicability:** Paraguay is not an EU member; RED II/III and ETS2 do not apply. Ley 7.635 and Decreto 3.241 are Paraguay's standalone instruments.
- **MERCOSUR coordination:** Paraguay, Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay in common market framework; biofuel policy divergence evident (Brazil mandate-plus-credit hybrid, Argentina volume-only, Paraguay volume-only with MIC discretion).
- **SAF feedstock promotion:** Recent decree (0% export tax for alternative oils) signals interest in sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) development; not yet translated into domestic SAF mandate.
- **Omega Green biodiesel plant (Villeta):** Long-touted project; remains under construction as of 2026; completion/operational date uncertain per USDA GAIN (PA2026-0002).
