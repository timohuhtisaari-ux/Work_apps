# EU Biofuel Regulation — Cross-Country Comparison (all 27 member states)

- **As-of date:** 2026-07-04
- **Scope:** transport-fuel biofuel/renewable-fuel obligations, headline fuel excise, biofuel tax
  treatment, sustainability-certification regime and RED III transposition status for every EU
  member state.
- **Provenance:** every figure in this file is synthesised **only** from the 27 per-country
  reports `research/reports/<ISO2>-country-report-2026-07-04.md`; no value was re-researched
  online. Confidence labels (`CONFIRMED`, `PRIMARY-ONLY`, `UNCORROBORATED`, `CONFLICT`,
  `PROPOSED`, `GAP`) follow `research/METHODOLOGY.md`. Where a per-country report flags a value
  as a gap or a conflict, that caveat is preserved here (see the data-quality section at the end);
  cells that a report could not establish read "gap". Note that the four obligation designs
  (volume-blending, energy-share, GHG-reduction, tax-incentive) are **not directly comparable by
  headline percentage** — a 10 % GHG-intensity cut and a 10 % energy share measure different things.

## 3. Narrative sections

### 3.1 Obligation-design split

Counting each state by the design its own report emphasises:

- **Energy-share (12):** Belgium, Estonia, Finland, Croatia, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Lithuania,
  Poland, Portugal, Slovenia, Spain. (Ireland, Italy, Poland, Portugal and Spain run this through
  tradable/accounting certificates — RTFC, CIC, NCW settlement, TdB/TdC, SICBIOS respectively.)
- **Volume-blending (7):** Bulgaria, Cyprus, Greece, Luxembourg, Malta, Romania, Slovakia.
  (Cyprus, Luxembourg and Slovakia express the blend in **energy content** rather than volume %;
  Greece and Romania are true % v/v; Malta is a "substitution" obligation by energy content.)
- **GHG-reduction (5):** Czechia, Denmark, Germany, Netherlands, Sweden — i.e. the life-cycle
  CO2-intensity-cut design (THG-Quote / reduktionsplikt / CO2e-fortrængningskrav / ERE brandstof-
  transitieverplichting).
- **Tax-incentive (1):** France — TIRUERT sets pool targets but enforces them purely through a
  penalty levy (140 €/hL of shortfall), becoming IRICC from 2027.
- **Hybrid (2):** Austria (dual energy-share substitution **plus** a GHG-intensity reduction quota)
  and Latvia (a 2030 GHG-intensity target **plus** an advanced/RFNBO energy-share sub-obligation
  **plus** transitional volumetric blending).

### 3.2 RED III transposition status across the EU

The RED III (Directive (EU) 2023/2413) transport deadline was **21 May 2025** (permitting
provisions were due 1 July 2024). As at 2026-07-04 the reports show a wide spread:

- **Transposed on time:** only **Denmark** notified full transposition by the deadline (Commission
  statement 24 Jul 2025).
- **Transposed late / substantially complete:** Belgium, Czechia, Germany (BGBl. 2026 I Nr. 163,
  Jun 2026), Ireland (S.I. 664/2025), Italy (D.Lgs. 5/2026, Feb 2026), Latvia and Lithuania
  (transport pillar), Netherlands (Jun 2026), Slovakia (Decree 31/2026) and Slovenia (ZSROVE-1,
  Dec 2025). Several of these still sit on the **24 July 2025 letter-of-formal-notice list**
  (26 member states) pending Commission confirmation — notably Estonia, Finland, Latvia, Lithuania,
  Slovakia and Sweden.
- **Referred to the Court of Justice of the EU (29 Apr 2026, with financial sanctions requested):**
  **Portugal, Greece and Malta** — the three on the RES/RED III non-transposition referral (EL and
  PT "notified no transposition measures"; MT notified some but insufficiently clear).
- **Most-advanced live RED III infringement:** **Cyprus** — an **additional reasoned opinion issued
  4 June 2026 (INFR(2024)0213)**, the only member state escalated on RED III in the June-2026
  package, two months to respond before possible CJEU referral with financial sanctions.
- **Reasoned opinion outstanding (short of CJEU):** **France** (11 Dec 2025, INFR(2025)0220);
  **Spain** (reasoned opinions Feb & Dec 2025) and **Austria** — all three named in the June-2026
  package as still not having communicated full transposition. **Hungary** was escalated to a
  reasoned opinion in December 2025 (with Malta and Poland) despite having domestic measures.
- **Partial / formal-notice or not yet in force:** **Bulgaria** (28 measures notified but Feb-2025
  reasoned opinion on permitting); **Poland** (RED II only adopted, open formal notice, draft UC106);
  **Croatia** and **Romania** (letter-of-formal-notice stage only); **Estonia** (transport partly
  transposed, formal notice); **Luxembourg** (transport measure still before the Conseil d'État —
  PROPOSED); **Austria** (no transport measure adopted).

### 3.3 Highest vs lowest 2026 obligation levels and 2030 ambition

Because designs differ, levels are grouped by metric:

- **Energy-share / energy-content (comparable band):** **Ireland's 32 %** (energy content, RTFO) is
  by far the highest 2026 headline, followed by **Finland's 19.5 %**. The lowest established
  energy-share levels are **Cyprus 7.3 %** (single-source) and **Estonia 7.5 %**, then Croatia
  (~8.8–10.3 %, unresolved), Hungary 9.1 %, Slovakia 9.5 %, Lithuania 9.8 pp and Poland's 10 %
  nominal (~8.5 % effective). Spain and Portugal sit at 14 %/13 %; Belgium 12.2 %; Italy 12.60 %;
  Slovenia 13.8 %.
- **GHG-reduction band:** **Sweden 10 %** and **Germany 12 %** are the highest life-cycle cuts;
  **Denmark's 5.2 %** and **Czechia's ≈6.25 %** are the lowest. The Netherlands' land sector
  (14.4 %) tops the GHG group on paper but is not comparable to the energy-share countries.
- **2030 ambition:** the steepest legislated 2030 trajectories are **Finland (34 % energy)** and
  **Germany (26.5 % GHG)**, with the **Netherlands** land sector at 28.4 % GHG. The flattest is
  **Sweden**, which repealed its step-ups and holds petrol/diesel at 10 % through 2030; **Greece**
  and **Romania** have no legislated 2030 step-up at all (static blend, RED III unenacted).

### 3.4 Biofuel tax-treatment patterns

- **Excise/CO2 relief for pure or high blends:** France (E85/B100/ED95 reduced €/MWh rates),
  Italy (HVO100 617.40 €/1000 L), Latvia (E85 360, B100/HVO100 330), Luxembourg (HVO100 CO2-tax
  exemption), Czechia (B100/SMN30), Ireland (biofuels pay only the non-carbon MOT component),
  Portugal (total ISP exemption for certified advanced biofuels & renewable gases), Sweden (full
  energy+CO2 deduction for pure/high blends and biogas), Finland (structural CO2-tax discount:
  R-fuels ½, T-fuels zero) and Denmark (bio share exempt from CO2-afgift).
- **Biogas/biomethane-only relief:** Lithuania and Slovakia give a reduced/exempt rate to
  biomethane as a motor fuel but no relief to liquid road biofuels.
- **Mandate-only (no biofuel excise relief):** Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Germany, Greece,
  Estonia, Hungary, Malta, Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Slovenia and Spain — biofuel blended into
  road fuel is taxed at the full fossil-equivalent rate; the incentive lives entirely in the
  obligation. Austria is a special case: no dedicated high-blend relief, but its two-tier MÖSt
  embeds a de-facto credit for meeting a minimum biogenic content.
- **Design nuance:** where relief exists, **Sweden (and historically Finland's framing)** restrict
  it to volumes *outside* the obligation (a state-aid condition), whereas France, Italy, Denmark,
  Ireland, Latvia and Luxembourg apply it as a general product-based rate independent of the mandate.

### 3.5 Cross-cutting EU facts (identical across all 27 reports)

- **ETS2:** the emissions-trading system for road-transport and buildings fuels was **established by
  Directive (EU) 2023/959** (inserting Chapter IVa into Directive 2003/87/EC). Its start was
  **postponed one year to 1 January 2028**; auctioning begins in 2027 and the **first allowance
  surrender is in 2029** (for 2028 emissions). The one-year postponement was adopted in the early-2026
  climate package (Regulation (EU) 2026/667 is the 2040-climate-target/postponement vehicle — several
  reports flag that 2026/667 is **not** itself the ETS2 instrument).
- **Union Database (UDB):** operational for economic-operator registration since **15 January 2024**
  (liquid fuels first, gaseous from Nov 2024); legal basis RED Art. 31a + Implementing Reg.
  (EU) 2022/996; EU-wide mandatory-use/sanction date still being finalised (Poland already mandatory
  since 3 Apr 2023 through its scheme).
- **Crop cap / Annex IX rules:** the RED food/feed-crop cap is **7 %** of transport energy (member
  states may set lower operative caps — e.g. ES 2.6 %, IT 2.3 %, NL land 1.2 %); the **Annex IX-B

  EU floor with **national palm/soy exclusions** (Belgium, Denmark, France, Italy, and the
  Netherlands' national soy exclusion), while Germany excludes palm (from 2023) but not soy.
