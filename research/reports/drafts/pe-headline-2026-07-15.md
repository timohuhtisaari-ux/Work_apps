# Peru — Biofuel Regulation Headline Report
**Date:** 2026-07-15 | **Researched by:** Non-EU biofuel mandate researcher

---

## 1. Instrument type & 2026 headline obligation

**Volume-based blending mandate (percentage of transport fuel by volume).** Peru requires:
- **Gasoline:** minimum 7.8% ethanol (E7.8) — in effect since 2010, full phase-in by 2013
- **Diesel:** minimum 5% biodiesel (B5) — in effect since 2011

These are mandatory; fuel suppliers must comply. No energy-efficiency or GHG-intensity alternative is offered.

---

## 2. Trajectory / 2030 target

**No legislated step-up.** Peru has maintained the E7.8 / B5 mandate unchanged since 2011–2013. USDA FAS reports (2025) state: *"Since the biofuels law was approved establishing a blend mandate of 7.8 percent for ethanol and 5 percent for biodiesel, there have not been initiatives to increase these blend mandates."* No amendments are documented for 2026–2030.

---

## 3. Advanced/sub-quotas if any

Not documented. Peru's mandate does not specify advanced-biofuel (e.g., waste-based or Annex IX-type) sub-targets. All ethanol and biodiesel count equally toward the mandate regardless of feedstock or production method.

---

## 4. Fuel-tax treatment of biofuels

**No tax incentive.** Peru does not offer tax relief (reduced excise, VAT exemption, or other fiscal support) to biofuel producers or blenders. Biofuels are taxed under the same excise regime as fossil fuels. The IEA reports that Peru's policy framework relies on **mandatory blending alone**, with no price support or tax subsidy.

---

## 5. Sustainability/certification regime

**Limited documentation as of 2026-07-15.** 
- The mandate under DS-021-2007-EM (Supreme Decree on Biofuel Commercialization) specifies quality and blending requirements but does not codify a sustainability certification scheme.
- MINEM is coordinating with MINAM, MIDAGRI, PRODUCE, and MTC (April 2026) to **strengthen Peru's regulatory framework for biofuel marketing**, but details are not yet published.
- No dedicated national registry or certification body for biofuels is documented in live sources.
- Feedstock restrictions (e.g., on palm or soy) are not currently enforced.

- **No tax intervention:** Peru relies purely on supply-side blending mandates; no fiscal levers.

## 8. Five-line summary

1. **Obligation type:** Volume-based (percentage) blending mandate: E7.8 gasoline, B5 diesel.
2. **Current level (2026):** Unchanged from 2010–2011: 7.8% ethanol, 5% biodiesel.
3. **Next legislated change:** None documented; MINEM is consulting on regulatory framework but no new mandate proposed.
4. **RED III status:** Not applicable — Peru is not an EU member.
5. **Key status flags:** GAP on sustainability certification and non-compliance penalties; regulatory framework strengthening in progress (April 2026); no tax incentives.
