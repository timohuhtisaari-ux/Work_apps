# Poland — Biofuel Mandate & Demand Statistics: HVO, FAME, Ethanol, SAF (incl. 2026 Forecast)

*Prepared: 8 July 2026. Method: multi-agent deep-research run — 5 parallel search angles, 21 sources fetched, 99 claims extracted, top 25 claims adversarially verified by 3-vote panels (25 confirmed, 0 refuted). Confidence levels are noted per section.*

---

## 1. The Polish blending mandate — National Indicative Target (NCW)

Poland's mandate is the **Narodowy Cel Wskaźnikowy (NCW)**, set in the Law on Biocomponents and Liquid Biofuels. The decisive recent change is the **Act of 21 February 2025** (Dz.U. 2025 poz. 303, published 12 March 2025, general entry into force 1 April 2025), which finally transposed EU RED II — years past the mid-2021 deadline. Its NCW provisions took effect **1 January 2026**. RED III transposition (29% RES-in-transport or −14.5% GHG intensity by 2030; 5.5% advanced/RFNBO sub-target) is still pending and could alter the 2026–2030 outlook mid-period.

### 1.1 NCW trajectory (energy/calorific basis)

| Year | NCW (overall, % energy) | Biodiesel sub-target | Bioethanol sub-target | Crop-based cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 8.7% ¹ | — | — | — |
| 2022 | 8.8% | 5.2% | 3.2% | — |
| 2023 | 8.9% | 5.2% | 3.2% | — |
| 2024 | 9.1% | 5.2% | 3.2% | — |
| 2025 | 9.2% | 5.2% | 4.59% | — |
| **2026–2029** | **10.0%** | **6.2%** | **4.59%** | **6.1%** |
| 2030+ | 14.9% | — | — | 6.1% |

Sources: USDA FAS GAIN *Biofuel Mandates in the EU by Member State – 2025* (E42025-0004, Berlin), ISAP Dz.U. 2025 poz. 303, POPiHN annual report 2024, URE. Confidence: **high** (verified 3-0 / 2-1).
¹ 2021 level per URE's announcement of NCW for 2021–2024; extracted from URE but not run through the adversarial verify pass. The commonly cited 2020 level is 8.5% (not independently verified in this run). Note: URE-reported *realized* NCW without multipliers was much lower (5.82% in 2020 → 6.23% in 2023, per KIB citing URE) — realization relies heavily on multiple counting.

### 1.2 Advanced-biofuel floor and waste-based cap (from 2026)

| Year | Annex IX Part A minimum (% energy) | Annex IX Part B cap (UCO/animal fat, % energy) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | 1.0% | 3.4% |
| 2030 | 3.5% | 3.4% |

The 3.4% Part B cap is the RED II 1.7% physical cap doubled under Poland's 2× multiplier. Confidence: **high**.

### 1.3 Multiple-counting rules effective 1 January 2026

| Fuel / energy carrier | Multiplier toward NCW |
|---|---|
| Advanced biofuels (RED II Annex IX feedstocks) | 2.0× |
| Renewable/recycled-carbon fuels in **aviation & marine** fuels | 1.2× |
| Renewable electricity — road (charging) | 4.0× |
| Renewable electricity — rail | 1.5× |

Verified directly in the statute (Act of 21 Feb 2025, art. 1 pkt 14 lit. e amending art. 23 ust. 2; entry into force art. 24 pkt 4). Confidence: **high**.

### 1.4 Accounting basis and fuel-spec constraints

- NCW compliance is accounted on an **energy basis** using fixed values in the mandatory URE quarterly-report template effective 31 January 2026 (Dz.U. 2025 poz. 1618): **bioethanol 27 MJ/kg, FAME 37 MJ/kg, HVO 45 MJ/kg (spark-ignition) / 44 MJ/kg (diesel)**.
- Standard Polish diesel may contain **up to 7.0% vol of conventional biocomponents (the FAME "B7 wall")**; HVO ("biowęglowodory ciekłe – hydrorafinowane oleje") is a separate **uncapped drop-in category**.
- Since **1 January 2024**, 95-octane gasoline must be sold as **E10** (blending obligation 5.3%, of which 4.59% via ethanol); 98-octane remains **E5** (3.2%). The E10 switch adds an estimated ~500,000 t/yr of corn feedstock demand.

Confidence: **high** (primary legal sources).

## 2. Official demand datapoints by fuel

### 2.1 Fuel ethanol

| Year | Consumption (million liters, USDA FAS/Eurostat) |
|---|---|
| 2020 | 359 |
| 2021 | 409 |
| 2022 | 456 |
| 2023 | 470–478 (revised) |
| 2024 | ~505–506 (est.) |
| 2025 | 520 (FAS forecast) |

POPiHN separately estimates major operators blended **~519 thousand m³ of ethanol (counted with ethers/ETBE)** into gasoline in **2024, +41.2% y/y** — the jump reflects the E10 rollout. Confidence: **high**.

### 2.2 FAME / biodiesel (biomass-based diesel)

| Year | BBD consumption (million liters, USDA FAS; FAME + HVO/HDRD incl. small SAF) |
|---|---|
| 2020 | 1,076 |
| 2022 | 1,200 |
| 2023 | 1,247–1,259 (revised) |
| 2024 | 1,234–1,250 (est.) |

Additional official datapoints:
- POPiHN 2024: members blended **>1.3 million m³ of FAME** into diesel (+3.8% y/y), plus **~209 thousand m³ of B100** supplied domestically and ~100 thousand m³ exported (most exported B100 believed consumed abroad).
- KOWR producer reports, **Q4 2025 alone: ~234,061 t of FAME produced** (222,674 t single-counting, mainly from 225,508 t rapeseed oil; 11,387 t from double-counting feedstocks incl. 7,968 t UCO).
- Poland is a top-six EU biomass-based-diesel consumer.

Confidence: **high**.

### 2.3 HVO (renewable diesel)

There is **no official Polish HVO consumption series yet**. Verified status:
- HVO sales only became **legally possible in 2024** after a regulatory interpretation of placement-on-market rules; a quality-requirements regulation was to enter into force in 2025.
- As of POPiHN's 2024 report, HVO was used in **"very small quantities"**; POPiHN advocates exempting HVO from the emission fee (opłata emisyjna) to spur uptake.
- From 2026 HVO is recognized as an **uncapped drop-in diesel biocomponent** in the NCW framework (44–45 MJ/kg accounting values).

Confidence: **high** on status; no volume baseline exists.

### 2.4 SAF and aviation fuel

- Polish **jet fuel consumption: 1,448 thousand m³ in 2024 (+9.2% y/y)** (POPiHN; 1,326 thousand m³ in 2023).
- **ReFuelEU Aviation** requires a **2% SAF share at EU airports from 2025** (6% from 2030); POPiHN notes Polish implementation is **delayed** ("ReFuelEU Aviation, FuelEU Maritime – opóźnienie się pogłębia").
- SAF volumes to date are small and **embedded within biomass-based-diesel statistics** rather than reported separately.
- From 2026, renewable fuels in aviation/marine earn the **1.2× NCW multiplier**, adding a domestic incentive on top of ReFuelEU.

Confidence: **high** on the framework; Poland-specific SAF volumes are not separately published.

## 3. 2026 demand forecast

Derived from the 2026 mandate arithmetic and POPiHN's base-case fuel demand (**diesel 23.6 million m³, gasoline 8.4 million m³, jet ~1.55–1.7 million m³** in 2026), using the official energy-content values from Dz.U. 2025 poz. 1618. A back-test of the method against 2024 reproduces actual FAME blending within a few percent. **This is a derived synthesis — no official body publishes a 2026 per-fuel volume forecast for Poland.** Overall confidence: **medium** (low for HVO).

| Fuel | 2026 forecast (volume) | ≈ Mass | vs. 2024 actual | Key driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FAME** | ~1.4–1.6 million m³ | ~1.25–1.4 million t | >1.3 million m³ | 6.2% energy biodiesel sub-target (~6.8% vol); 2×-counted Annex IX FAME can displace physical volume |
| **Ethanol** (incl. ethers) | ~540–580 thousand m³ | ~430–460 kt | ~519 thousand m³ | 4.59% energy sub-target (~6.9% vol) on 8.4 million m³ gasoline; FAS 2025 forecast 520 ML |
| **HVO** | tens of thousands up to ~150 thousand m³ (low confidence) | up to ~115 kt | "very small quantities" | 10% NCW with 6.1% crop cap leaves ~3.9 pp for non-crop fuels; 1% Part A floor; uncapped drop-in status above the B7 wall |
| **SAF** | ~31–34 thousand m³ | ~25–27 kt | not separately reported | ReFuelEU 2% of a ~1.55–1.7 million m³ jet pool; 1.2× NCW multiplier |

Forecast mechanics worth noting:
- **The B7 wall binds**: 6.2% *energy* biodiesel ≈ 6.8% *volume*, close to the 7.0% vol FAME limit in standard diesel — so incremental compliance beyond B7 must come from HVO, B100 fleets, or double-counted volumes.
- **Multipliers cut physical demand**: with 2× Annex IX FAME, 1.2× aviation fuels and 4× EV charging all creditable from 2026, *accounted* energy and *physical* volumes will diverge; physical demand may land at the low end of the ranges.
- **SAF is an implied national share**: the ReFuelEU obligation applies to fuel suppliers at EU airports in aggregate, so the Polish figure is an implied, not ring-fenced, quantity.

## 4. Caveats

1. The 2026 per-fuel forecasts are derived from the mandate trajectory, POPiHN base-case demand and official energy values — not published official forecasts.
2. USDA FAS series are estimates subject to revision (2023 ethanol revised 470→478 ML; 2024 BBD 1,250→1,234 ML); prefer the newer revised figures.
3. POPiHN blending figures cover major market operators/members, not the whole market, and its ethanol figure includes ethers (ETBE).
4. No verified full-year 2025 consumption data were available at synthesis time (KOWR Q4 2025 production was the most recent official datapoint).
5. RED III transposition (due May 2025, still pending) could change the 2026–2030 trajectory if enacted mid-period.

## 5. Open questions to monitor

- Actual full-year 2025 volumes for ethanol, FAME, HVO and SAF (URE/KOWR/POPiHN 2025 annuals) and first-year performance of the 9.2% NCW / 4.59% ethanol sub-target.
- How obligated parties will split 2026 compliance among physical HVO imports, double-counted Part B FAME, Part A advanced fuels, and 4×-counted charging electricity — this determines actual physical HVO demand.
- Whether Poland exempts HVO from the emission fee, and when the HVO quality regulation takes full effect at retail.
- Actual SAF supplied at Polish airports in 2025–2026 given the acknowledged implementation delay, and whether it is met with domestic (Orlen) production or imports.
