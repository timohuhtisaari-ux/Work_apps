# China SAF mandate & HVO voluntary demand — research note

*Compiled 3 September 2026. All claims sourced to public reporting; links at the end.*

---

## Q1. Has China introduced a 1% SAF mandate starting 2027?

**No.** As of early September 2026 China has **no binding SAF blending mandate at all** — not 1%, not for 2027, not for any year. The "1% from 2027" figure is real, but it belongs to other Asian markets, not China.

### What China actually has

| Instrument | Status | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| NDRC/CAAC SAF pilot programme | Live since 18 Sep 2024 | Phase 1 (Sep–Dec 2024): 12 flights, Air China / China Eastern / China Southern, out of Beijing Daxing, Chengdu Shuangliu, Zhengzhou Xinzheng, Ningbo Lishe. Phase 2 (2025): from March 2025 **all domestic departures from those four airports carry ~1% SAF blend**. Expanded further through 2025–26. |
| 14th Five-Year Plan target | Non-binding | 20,000 t/yr SAF consumption by 2025; 50,000 t cumulative. |
| 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) | Approved 12 Mar 2026 | Emphasises green fuels/SAF industrial build-out. **No published blending obligation.** |
| CAAC stated policy line | July 2026 | CAAC research-body official: China is prioritising **SAF certification, traceability, voluntary markets and book-and-claim** over demand-side mandates. Explicitly contrasted with RefuelEU Aviation. |
| CORSIA | Expected participation ~2027 | Separate from any blending mandate. |

**Where the "1%" likely comes from — two plausible mix-ups:**

1. **The pilot's 1% blend.** The pilot does use a ~1% blend at the four pilot airports from March 2025. It is an administrative trial, not a legal obligation on fuel suppliers, and its start year is 2025, not 2027.
2. **South Korea / India / Malaysia.** These *do* have 1%-from-2027 policies:
   - **South Korea** — binding 1% mandate on departing international flights from 2027, rising to 3–5% by 2030 and 7–10% by 2035.
   - **India** — 1% indicative target for international flights in 2027 (non-binding).
   - **Malaysia** — proposed 1% from 2027, scaling toward 47% by 2050.
   - (Brazil is a further false friend: 1% *GHG reduction* — not 1% blend — from 2027.)

### What is expected instead

- Consensus expectation is a mandate emerging out of the 15th FYP period, most often modelled at **~2% around 2026–27 rising to ~5% by 2030** (NexantECA working assumption: 5% by 2030). Estimates in circulation range 2–5% up to a high-case 15% by 2030. **None of this is announced policy.**
- Chinese academics have been publicly lobbying for a mandate; the CAAC position is that a demand mandate follows supply assurance, not the other way round.
- Meanwhile the industry is scaling anyway on **export** economics: 16 new Chinese SAF projects announced in H1 2026 vs. 4 in the rest of the world combined (BNEF), SOEs (Sinopec, CNPC) now ~half of new projects. Chinese SAF output is roughly **99% export-oriented to Europe**, pulled by EU/UK mandates rather than domestic ones. ~870 kt/yr operational across six certified producers; ~3.3 Mt/yr built + planned. Sinopec absorbed China National Aviation Fuel Group by July 2026, giving it production-to-wingtip integration.
- Beijing scrapped the 13% UCO export tax rebate in late 2024 to retain feedstock domestically.

**Bottom line:** treat "China 1% SAF mandate from 2027" as **not correct**. A domestic mandate is widely anticipated within the 15th FYP window but has not been issued, and China's stated near-term strategy is voluntary-market infrastructure instead.

## Q2. Is the HVO market growing on voluntary demand?

**Partly — but no, not primarily.** HVO volume growth in 2026 is overwhelmingly **compliance-driven**. Voluntary demand is a real, growing, price-supportive *secondary* segment, not the engine.

### The mandate side is doing the heavy lifting

- **Germany is the single clearest case.** HVO consumption in the first five months of 2026 hit **270,700 t vs. 64,900 t in the same period of 2025** — roughly 4x. UFOP projects ~**650,000 t for full-year 2026**. The drivers named are entirely regulatory:
  - GHG reduction quota up from **10.6% (2025) → 12% (2026)**;
  - **removal of double-counting** for Annex IX waste-based biofuels from 1 Jan 2026 (so more physical litres per unit of compliance);
  - closure of the surplus-quota carry-forward loophole;
  - the **7% statutory biodiesel blending cap** in the diesel standard, which forces suppliers into HVO once FAME is maxed out.
  - Notably, Argus expects 2027 physical demand to **plateau** despite the quota rising to 17.5%, because carry-over offsets return — a very mandate-shaped demand curve.
- **EU-wide:** Argus Biofuels Analytics forecasts EU biofuels demand **+10% y/y to 33.7 bn litres in 2026**, with Germany the main HVO driver, under RED III transposition.
- **US:** EPA RVOs guarantee ≥**5.33 bn gal biomass-based diesel in 2026** and **5.75 bn gal in 2027**; several large US plants come online in 2026, adding >3 bn litres of capacity. Voluntary demand outside LCFS/RFS economics remains thin.
- Neste — the largest producer, >13.5% share — attributes its record Q2 2026 renewable products EBITDA (€859m, margin $1,223/t, >1 Mt volumes) to **mandate outlook and premiums**, singling out Germany's RED III adoption as adding ~**1.5 Mt/yr of renewable diesel demand in 2026**. Its own guidance is that 2026 sales volumes are ~flat vs 2025 — margin, not voluntary-volume, expansion.

### The voluntary side is genuine but smaller

- Market consensus phrasing across Argus/Fastmarkets coverage: **"a smaller part of HVO demand is voluntary"**, with mandates setting the fundamentals.
- Where it shows up:
  - **Corporate fleet/logistics decarbonisation** — DHL, DB Schenker-type Scope 1/3 commitments; construction, municipal and defence fleets signing multi-year offtakes.
  - **Retail HVO100** — legal for unrestricted sale in Germany since late May 2024. At participating stations HVO is ~**10% of diesel sales, up to ~25%** at commercial/fleet-heavy sites — but station coverage started around **2% of German forecourts**, so the absolute contribution is modest. Argus's own framing has been "a slow burner rather than a fire starter."
  - **Off-road / power** — data-centre backup generators (e.g. ~500 HVO-run gensets deployed by AVK with Rolls-Royce from Dec 2024), construction plant, temporary power. This is the fastest-growing genuinely voluntary niche, and it sits largely outside road-fuel mandates. Transport is still >70% of consumption.
  - **Petrochemicals** — voluntary bio-naphtha/bio-feedstock buying is cited as a specific 2026 price-support factor.
- **The constraint on voluntary growth is price.** NW European HVO Class II fob ARA premium to gasoil averaged **~$1,075/m³ in Jan–Nov 2025 vs ~$683/m³ in 2024**, and is not expected to ease in 2026. Voluntary buyers pay that premium out of pocket with no compliance ticket to monetise, so the segment is structurally rationed by cost — it expands where the premium is small relative to the buyer's decarbonisation value (brand-sensitive logistics, data centres), and stalls elsewhere.
- Cautionary read-across: Neste has publicly flagged **sluggish voluntary demand on the SAF side**, the same buyer psychology in an adjacent product.

### Bottom line

- **Volume growth 2026: mandate-driven.** Germany's quota step-up, end of double-counting, RED III transposition and US RVOs explain nearly all of the increase.
- **Voluntary demand: real, growing, but second-order** — best understood as a price-supportive premium tier (fleets, retail HVO100, data centres/off-road, petrochemicals) rather than the growth engine. It is capped by a wide and sticky HVO-to-diesel premium.
- Structural watch item: HVO+SAF capacity is expected to balance demand through 2027 then tip into deficit from 2028, with a >10 Mt shortfall modelled by 2030 — which would squeeze the voluntary segment first, since compliance buyers can outbid it.
