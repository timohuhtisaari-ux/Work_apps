# Brazil: Biofuel Mandates—Headline Report (2026-07-15)

## 1. Instrument Type & 2026 Headline Obligation

**Primary Instrument:** Blending mandate via CNPE (Conselho Nacional de Política Energética) resolutions + RenovaBio credit scheme (CBIOs).

**2026 Headline Obligation:**
- **Ethanol:** E32 (temporary, 180 days renewable from 14 July 2026; before: E30 since 1 Aug 2025)
- **Biodiesel:** B15 (mandatory since 1 Aug 2025; may advance to B16 end-2026, per Lei 14.993/2024 tests)
- **RenovaBio:** Distributors must offset 10.1% of 2026 CO2 emissions via CBIOs (Decarbonization Credits); central target 48.09M CBIOs for 2026.

**Legal Basis:** Lei 14.993/2024 (Lei do Combustível do Futuro, signed 8 Oct 2024); CNPE Resolução 14/2026 (E32, 14 Jul 2026, temporary); Lei 13.576/2017 (RenovaBio); Decree 9.888/2019 (RenovaBio regulations); Ordinance 56/2022 (MME, RenovaBio Ordinance).

**Obligated Parties:** Fuel distributors and importers. Automotive fuel suppliers must meet blending or CBIO-buyout targets.

## 3. Advanced-Biofuel Sub-Quotas & Caps

**RenovaBio CBIO Scheme (Credit-based):**
- Fuel distributors must purchase CBIOs (Decarbonization Credits) equal to their CO2-intensity reduction obligation.
- Each CBIO = 1 tonne CO2e avoided via biofuel use (calculated via ANP/EPE methodology).
- No explicit sub-quota for advanced biofuels (Annex IX-A under EU RED); instead, all biofuels compete on GHG savings metric.
- **Double-counting:** RenovaBio allows "double-counting" for advanced biofuels (waste/residue) and certain high-GHG-saving feedstocks under CBIO calculations, increasing credit yield.
- **No Annex IX Part B cap** (EU framework N/A; Brazil has its own GHG-intensity focus).
- **Crop-based feedstock:** Cane-ethanol and soy-biodiesel both eligible; no explicit cap in current law, though cane is dominant ethanol source (~99% of ethanol).

## 4. Fuel-Tax Treatment of Biofuels

**Excise/Fuel Tax Structure:**
- **Ethanol (anhydrous, in gasoline blends):** 
  - CIDE (Contribuição de Intervenção no Domínio Econômico): **0% rate** (zero-rated from unspecified prior year; maintained as of 2026).
  - PIS/COFINS: **0% rate** (zero-rated from April 2023).
  - Result: Ethanol in blends effectively **tax-exempt** on these two federal excises.
- **Biodiesel (in diesel):**
  - CIDE: **0% rate** (zero-rated from 2018).
  - PIS/COFINS: **0% rate** (zero-rated from April 2023).
  - Result: Biodiesel in blends effectively **tax-exempt**.
- **Temporary Tax Relief (2026):**
  - June 2026: Fuel tax exemption extended to 31 July 2026 for biodiesel and aviation kerosene (temporary measure, tied to oil-price volatility).

## 5. Sustainability & Certification Regime

**Certification Schemes Accepted:**
- **ISCC EU:** International Sustainability & Carbon Certification; primary scheme used for biofuels destined for export to the EU.
- **2BSvs:** French voluntary scheme (secondary use).
- **National verification:** ANP maintains registry of certified biofuel producers; sustainability data integrated into CBIO calculations.

**GHG Performance & Sustainability Thresholds:**
- **Cane-ethanol:** Baseline ~40–50% GHG saving vs. fossil gasoline; high-efficiency plants achieve 60%+.
- **Soy-biodiesel:** Baseline ~50–60% GHG saving; some producers report 70%+ via advanced processes.
- **EU RED II criteria (applied by exporters):** 50% GHG saving minimum (from 2021 for installations starting operation after 1 Jan 2021); 65% for advanced biofuels.
- **RenovaBio CBIO calculations:** Use lifecycle GHG methodology; feedstock, production, and transport emissions included.

**No Explicit National Sustainability Law:** Brazil does not have a dedicated national sustainability certification regime (unlike EU's RED II/III). Compliance is via international schemes (ISCC, 2BSvs) for trade; domestic market relies on CBIO audit trail.

## 8. Five-Line Summary

Brazil mandates **E32 ethanol** (temporary, 180-day renewable) and **B15 biodiesel** (since Aug 2025), plus a **RenovaBio credit scheme (CBIOs)** requiring distributors to offset 10.1% of CO2 emissions by 2026. Legislated trajectory under Lei 14.993/2024 reaches **B20 by March 2030** (1% annual step-up) and permits ethanol blends up to **E35**. No explicit advanced-biofuel sub-quota; all biofuels compete on GHG-intensity via CBIO calculations (double-counting allowed for waste/high-saving feedstocks). Ethanol and biodiesel are **tax-exempt** on federal CIDE and PIS/COFINS. **PRIMARY-ONLY** confidence on E32 permanence (temporary status confirmed; CNPE renewal/decision Jan 2027 TBD); **CONFIRMED** on B15 and Lei 14.993 structure; **GAP** on B16 exact CNPE timeline and Lei 14.993 full-text legal basis (Planalto inaccessible).

## Cross-References & Further Notes

- **RenovaBio integration with blend mandate:** RenovaBio (credit-based, CO2-intensity focused) runs in parallel with blend mandates (E27/E30/E32, B15). Distributors must meet BOTH: achieve blending % AND purchase CBIOs. This hybrid model is distinct from EU RED-style mandate-plus-certificate.
- **No ETS2 or RED III relevance:** Brazil is not an EU member; EU regulations (ETS2, RED III) do not apply. RenovaBio is Brazil's own policy instrument.
- **Sugarcane-ethanol export:** Cane ethanol destined for EU markets must meet RED II/III GHG criteria (via ISCC, 2BSvs) independently of domestic RenovaBio scheme.
- **Pending Lei 14.993 amendments:** Government proposals to expand bio-jet (ProBioQAV), bio-diesel (PNDV), and biomethane programs; not yet confirmed as legislated.
