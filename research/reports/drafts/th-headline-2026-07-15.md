# Thailand — Biofuel Instruments (Non-EU) — 2026

**Date-stamped: 2026-07-15**

## 1. Instrument Type & 2026 Headline Obligation

Thailand's primary biofuel framework combines a **volume-based blending mandate** for road fuels under the Department of Alternative Energy Development and Efficiency (DEDE) and the Ministry of Energy, with a complementary **sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) mandate** starting 2026.

### Road Fuels (Gasoline & Diesel)

**2026 Headline Obligation:**
- **Gasoline:** Mandatory nationwide phase-out of E10 (octane 91 and 95) by 2026, with E20 (20% ethanol by volume) established as the primary passenger-car fuel.
- **Diesel:** Biodiesel B7 (7% biodiesel blend) mandatory for all diesel distribution; B10 sales discontinued in 2024; B20 available as alternative.
- **Obligated parties:** Oil refiners, fuel importers, and distributors.
- **Effective from:** Existing E20/B7 standards; October 2023 official SAF mandate announcement effective January 1, 2026.

**Legal basis (road):** No single consolidated act named; regulations issued under DEDE and the Oil Fund framework. Road fuel standards implement via Cabinet announcements and ministry directives (referenced in DEDE announcements and press releases).

**Confidence:** `PRIMARY-ONLY` — road fuel trajectory confirmed through DEDE announcements and press reports (Nation Thailand, MillingandMillers 2025); no single primary legal gazette reference accessible in English; industry sources (Advanced BioFuels USA, USDA GAIN reporting) corroborate.

### Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF)

**2026 Headline Obligation:**
- **1% SAF mandatory blend** in Jet A-1 fuel from **January 1, 2026**, increasing to 8% by 2036.
- **Feedstock:** Hydroprocessed Esters and Fatty Acids (HEFA) initially; blend rules per ASTM D7566.
- **Obligated parties:** Airlines and fuel suppliers.

**Legal basis:** Cabinet approval and DEDE/Ministry of Energy directive; working group to formalize rules in 2026.

**Confidence:** `PRIMARY-ONLY` — SAF mandate announced by DEDE (Nation Thailand, "Thailand mandates 1% SAF blend for jet fuel from January 1, 2026" 2026-01). ASTM D7566 referenced in official government communications; no national gazette act yet published in English.

## 3. Advanced/Sub-Quotas

**No explicit sub-quotas or advanced-biofuel carve-outs found in primary sources for road fuels (E20/B7).** 

- E20 consists of 20% denatured ethanol from any source (sugarcane, cassava primarily domestically); no advanced/first-gen distinction in the mandate itself.
- Biodiesel B7 does not distinguish feedstock type (waste cooking oil, palm, soy, animal fat accepted); no advanced-biofuel sub-target located.

**SAF trajectory (2030–2032 onward) incorporates AtJ (Alcohol-to-Jet), which uses fermentation ethanol; this aligns with recycled carbon fuels (RCF) in some frameworks but is not formally designated "advanced" in Thai documents.**

**Confidence:** `PRIMARY-ONLY` — absence of sub-quota language in DEDE announcements and road-fuel policies; SAF phase-in of AtJ noted as policy but not labeled as "advanced quota."

## 4. Fuel-Tax Treatment of Biofuels

### Road Fuels

**Excise Tax Rates:**

| Fuel Type | Rate (Baht/L) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol (E20/gasoline) | 5.85–7.50 | Varies by blend; biofuel rate lower than mineral baseline |
| Diesel (B7/B20) | 7.44 | Single rate; biodiesel blends taxed uniformly |

**Subsidy Mechanism:**
- **Oil Fuel Fund** (an earmarked government account) subsidizes ethanol (from sugarcane, cassava) and biodiesel (palm oil) prices at pump.
- **Cabinet approval** (extended Sept 24, 2026) authorizes price discounts for E20, E85, B10, B20 blends to make them competitive with mineral fuels.
- **Mechanism:** Direct price subsidy at distribution, not excise exemption or credit.

**Status:** Sunset clause Sept 24, 2026; no post-2026 subsidy announced as of July 15, 2026.

**Confidence:** `CONFIRMED` — excise rates and subsidy structure confirmed by multiple sources: S&P Global (January 2024 feature), Biofuels International, Nation Thailand. Oil Fuel Fund mechanism described in Advanced BioFuels USA and USDA GAIN reporting (GAIN biofuels annual corroborates subsidy through 2026).

### Sustainable Aviation Fuel

**No specific excise tax rate announced for SAF.** The 1% mandate is a blending obligation, not a tax incentive. Airlines may be subject to standard aviation fuel tax; SAF pricing and margin pass-through to passengers via ticket surcharges not yet formalized.

**Confidence:** `GAP` — SAF tax treatment (whether exempted, reduced, or full-rate) not specified in DEDE announcements as of 2026-07-15.

## 5. Sustainability / Certification Regime

### Road Fuels (E20/B7)

**Ethanol (E20):**
- Primarily sourced from **sugarcane and cassava** (domestic); no explicit sustainability certification scheme mandated in Thai legislation found in primary sources.
- Industry standard: **ISO 14001** environmental management; no mandatory ISCC or RSPO requirement identified.

**Biodiesel (B7):**
- Feedstocks include **waste cooking oil, animal fat, palm oil, soy.**
- **Palm oil component:** Thailand produces palm oil at smaller scale than Indonesia/Malaysia; domestic production ~2% RSPO-certified (as of 2019, per RSPO data). Palm oil biodiesel imported or blended may reference RSPO certification but no Thai mandate identified.
- **Imported feedstocks:** May carry RSPO or ISCC certification as supplier requirement; Thai regulations do not explicitly mandate these.

**Confidence:** `UNCORROBORATED` — Thai government does not appear to enforce mandatory sustainability certification for E20/B7 in primary sources. RSPO and ISCC schemes available in Thailand but uptake appears voluntary/supply-side driven, not mandated.

### Sustainable Aviation Fuel

**Required certification:** SAF feedstocks and production must comply with **ASTM D7566** (international SAF standard).
- **Sustainability criteria:** Not detailed in Thai announcement; ASTM D7566 Appendix aligns with CORSIA offsetting requirements.
- **Initial feedstock:** HEFA (hydroprocessed fatty acids) from used cooking oil, animal fat, camelina; latter phases may include AtJ (ethanol).
- **No explicit mention** of ISCC EU, RSB, or other third-party certification schemes in Thai SAF mandate.

**Confidence:** `PRIMARY-ONLY` — ASTM D7566 compliance stated in DEDE/government sources; detailed SAF working-group standards to be finalized in 2026 per Cabinet announcement. No Thai national certification scheme established yet.

## 8. Five-Line Summary

1. **Obligation type:** Thailand mandates volume-based blending: E20 (20% ethanol) for gasoline (phase-out of E10 by 2026), B7 (7% biodiesel) for diesel, plus 1% SAF in jet fuel from January 1, 2026 (escalating to 8% by 2036).

2. **Current 2026 level:** E20 primary gasoline; B7 primary diesel; 1% SAF in aviation fuel as of Jan 1, 2026; subsidy via Oil Fuel Fund through Sept 24, 2026 (sunset pending Cabinet renewal).

3. **Next legislated change:** No published 2030 road-fuel targets; SAF roadmap escalates to 1–2% (2027–2029), then 3–5% with AtJ blend (2030–2032), 5–8% by 2033–2036.

4. **RED III status:** Not applicable (Thailand is non-EU; no RED III transposition required).

5. **Data flags:** `GAP` — no formal post-2026 road-fuel trajectory legislated; SAF sustainability standards working-group output pending 2026 publication. `UNCORROBORATED` — mandatory sustainability certification for E20/B7 not confirmed in primary Thai legislation.
